Phrasal Verbs: Everyday Non-Literal Meanings

2-3 word non-literal phrasal verbs, word formation families (-tion/-ment/-ness), and formal vs informal register pairs.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use non-literal phrasal verbs (*look forward to, put up with, come up with*) and formal vs informal word choices appropriately.

Phrasal verbMeaningExample
look forward tofeel excited about something in the futureI'm looking forward to the weekend.
put up withtolerate something unpleasantI can't put up with the noise anymore.
come up withthink of an idea or planShe came up with a great solution.
get on withhave a good relationship withI get on with my sister really well.
run out ofhave none leftWe ran out of milk this morning.

💡 Note: Non-literal phrasal units

Unlike literal phrasal verbs (*sit down, pick up*), these can't be guessed from the individual words — they need to be learned as fixed units, and often keep their form even when the sentence changes (*put up with it / putting up with it*).

⚠️ Common mistake
❌I'm looking forward the weekend.
tap to see the correct form
✅I'm looking forward to the weekend. (The preposition 'to' is required!)
