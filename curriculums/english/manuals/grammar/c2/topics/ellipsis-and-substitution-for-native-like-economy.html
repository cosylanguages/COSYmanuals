Ellipsis & Substitution for Native-like Economy

Omitting predictable elements and employing advanced pro-forms to achieve concise, elegant discourse flow.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can streamline speech and writing by eliminating repetitive clauses using advanced pro-forms (do so, the former, such) and structural ellipsis.

Core Rule: Achieve native economy by replacing repeated verb phrases with do so, clause complements with so / not, and antecedents with the former / the latter, or by omitting shared subjects/auxiliaries entirely (Ellipsis).

Why this matters: Native speakers instinctively avoid verbatim repetition. Ellipsis and substitution create cohesive momentum, signaling absolute linguistic mastery.

Verb Phrase SubstitutionReplace full predicate with do soAny member wishing to resign may do so in writing.
Clausal SubstitutionReplace clause with so / notWill regulations tighten? I expect so. / I fear not.
Nominal ReferenceDistinguish antecedents with the former / the latterBetween growth and inflation, the former is preferable.
Textual EllipsisOmit redundant auxiliary or noun headSome favored expansion, others [favored] consolidation.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing Ellipsis & Substitution for Native-like Economy, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.

Colour-Coded Examples
  • Should you choose to appeal, you must do so within thirty days.
  • The chairman recommended restructuring, and the board did likewise.

👀 Notice: In "...and the board did likewise", the verb phrase recommended restructuring is replaced smoothly without repeating a single word.

🧠 Think like English: English treats redundant repetition as a breach of elegance; once an idea is in the listener's head, erase or substitute it!

🧩 Language Signature: Pro-form Precision
`Repetition:` He promised to resign, and he resigned ➔ `Substitution:` He promised to resign, and did so.
