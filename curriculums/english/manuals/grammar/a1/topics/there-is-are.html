★ Why this matters

This is how you describe rooms, cities, and anything around you.

AffirmativeNegativeQuestion
one thingThere is a cat in the garden.There isn't a cat in the garden.Is there a cat in the garden?
many thingsThere are two cats in the garden.There aren't two cats in the garden.Are there two cats in the garden?
QuestionYesNo
Is there a cat?Yes, there is.No, there isn't.
Are there two cats?Yes, there are.No, there aren't.

💡 Negative + any

In the negative, any often replaces a number: There isn't any milk. (not There isn't some milk).

There vs. it: use there to say something exists; use it for something already mentioned, or weather/time.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing There is / There are, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.

More examples
  • There is a park near my house.
  • Is there a bank near here? · Yes, there is.
