★ Why this matters

English differs slightly by country, but everyone understands each other. Here are the differences that matter most for a beginner.

PointWhat changesExample
PossessionUKAU have got · USCA have / have gotI've got a car. = I have a car.
Groups of peopleUKAU singular or plural verb · USCA singular onlyMy team is/are winning.
WeekendUKAU at the weekend · USCA on the weekendSee you at/on the weekend!
OffersUK Shall we…? · USCAAU Should we…?Shall we go? (UK) · Should we go? (US)
Past tense spellingUKAUCA learnt, dreamt · US learned, dreamedI learnt / learned French.
Dates in numbersUKAU day/month/year · US month/day/year5/7/2026 = 5 July (UK) or May 7 (US)

Spelling patterns: colour/color, centre/center, realise/realize, travelling/traveling · the left form is UK/AU/CA, the right is US.

💡 A few everyday words that differ

UK flat / US apartment · UK holiday / US vacation · UK autumn / US fall · UK biscuit / US cookie · UK rubbish / US garbage · UK football / US soccer · UK lift / US elevator

★ Why this matters

Good news: grammar structures elsewhere in this manual work the same way in every variety of English · only these small points change, and native speakers understand all of them.
