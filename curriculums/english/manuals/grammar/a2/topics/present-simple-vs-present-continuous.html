Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Habits and permanent states vs actions happening right now

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can contrast daily habits and routines with actions happening at the moment of speaking.

1. "He is cooking dinner." — Is he cooking right now in this current moment?
Correct · Present continuous describes actions happening right now at the time of speaking.
2. "They are staying at a hotel this week." — Is this a permanent home or a temporary situation?
Correct · Present continuous expresses temporary situations around the present time.
TenseUsageSignal WordsExample
Present SimpleHabits, routines, permanent factsalways, usually, every day, oftenI work in London every day.
Present ContinuousActions happening now, temporary trendsnow, at the moment, today, right nowI am working from home today.

Key Distinction: Use Present Simple for regular routines (I play tennis on Saturdays). Use Present Continuous for temporary actions happening right now or around now (I am playing tennis right now).

👀 Stative Verbs Warning

Verbs of thinking, feeling, and possessing (like know, want, like, understand, belong) are rarely used in continuous forms: I know the answer (NOT: I am knowing).

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

The third-person '-s' ending varies phonetically: /s/ after voiceless consonants (walks), /z/ after voiced sounds (runs), and /ɪz/ after sibilants (washes).

Examples
  • He usually drinks tea, but today he is drinking coffee.
  • What are you doing right now? I am studying grammar.
