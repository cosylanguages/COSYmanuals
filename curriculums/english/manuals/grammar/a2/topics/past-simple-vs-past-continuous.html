Part 1 · Talking About the Past

Past Simple vs Past Continuous

The background scene and the interruption

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can tell compelling stories in English by combining background scenes with interrupting events.
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.
🎯 Practice this →

Past Continuous sets the scene (the longer, background action). Past Simple is the shorter action that interrupts it or happens at one point. Joined by when or while.

Two short, sequential actions (one after another) just use past simple + past simple: I heard the phone, so I answered it.

PatternExample
Continuous (background) + when + Simple (interruption)I was cooking when the phone rang.
While + Continuous, ContinuousWhile I was cooking, she was setting the table.

👀 Notice: Which one is longer?

Picture a long line (the background action) with a short arrow crossing it (the interruption). The long line = past continuous. The short arrow = past simple.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

In natural connected speech, '-ing' ends in the velar nasal /ɪŋ/. The auxiliary 'be' is unstressed and blends into the subject.
