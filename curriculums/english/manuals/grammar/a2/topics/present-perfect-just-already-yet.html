WordMeaningWord PositionExample
JUSTvery recentlybetween have/has and past participleI've just finished my homework.
ALREADYaffirmative, sooner than expectedbetween have/has and past participleShe has already left.
YETnegative & questionsat the end of negatives and questionsHave you finished yet? Not yet.

UKAU Present perfect is strongly preferred: Have you eaten yet?
USCA Past simple is also very common in speech: Did you eat yet? Both are understood everywhere.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing Present Perfect: Just, Already, Yet, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.
