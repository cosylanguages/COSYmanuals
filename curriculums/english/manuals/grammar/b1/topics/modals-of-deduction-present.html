Modals of Deduction: Present (Must, Can't, Might/Could)

might be30% possible

should be80% expectation
must be99% sure YES

ModalDegree of CertaintyExample
must99% sure YES (logical conclusion)She has three sports cars. She must be rich.
might / may / could50% possible (maybe yes, maybe no)He's not answering. He might be busy.
can't99% sure NO (impossible)That can't be John. He's on vacation in Japan!

Rule: Modal + base infinitive. Use must when you are almost certain something is true. Use can't (NOT mustn't) when you are almost certain something is impossible.

🔊 Pronunciation

In deductions, "must" is usually pronounced in its weak form /məst/ or /məs/ in fast connected speech (He must be tired), but stressed to /mʌst/ when emphasizing certainty (He MUST be joking!).

Examples
  • Look at the long queue outside. It must be a great restaurant.
  • You've been working all day. You must be exhausted.
