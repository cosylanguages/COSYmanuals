## PART 1 — PERSONAL LIFE, RELATIONSHIPS & CHARACTER

### UNIT 1: Detailed Personal Profiles & Life History (A2.1)
- **1.1 Life History, Upbringing & Biography**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Biography, background, birthplace, hometown, upbringing, childhood, youth, adulthood, memory, milestone, life event, degree, graduation, qualification, transition.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Be born, grow up, move, relocate, graduate, qualify, start work, retire, pass away, remember, recall, adapt, adjust.
  - **Adjectives:** Memorable, unforgettable, early, late, major, significant, gradual, peaceful, challenging.
  - **Collocations:** Early childhood, family background, graduate from university, move house, career milestone, cherish a memory.
  - **Set Phrases:** Where were you born? / I was born in 1998 in a small coastal town. / I grew up in Chicago before moving to London. / When did you graduate? / He moved abroad three years ago.
  - **Sentence Frames:** `I grew up in + [Location] + with + [Family]`, `After graduating from + [Institution], I moved to + [Location]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* Where did you grow up, Marcus?
    - *B:* I grew up in Manchester, but my family moved to London when I was twelve.
    - *A:* How was the transition for you?
    - *B:* It was challenging at first, but I adapted quickly and made new friends.

- **1.2 Personality Traits, Temperament & Character**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Personality, character, trait, quality, flaw, temperament, attitude, sense of humour, mood, nature, behavior.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Seem, appear, behave, describe, admire, value.
  - **Adjectives:** Reliable, hard-working, lazy, generous, selfish, patient, impatient, stubborn, flexible, moody, cheerful, shy, outgoing, polite, rude, honest, dishonest, sensitive, sensible, confident, modest, ambitious, talkative, quiet.
  - **Collocations:** Warm personality, reliable friend, hard-working employee, stubborn attitude, great sense of humour, change in mood.
  - **Set Phrases:** What is she like? / She is very hard-working and reliable. / He can be a bit stubborn at times. / She has a fantastic sense of humour. / Don't be so impatient!
  - **Sentence Frames:** `He is very + [Adjective], but sometimes he can be + [Adjective]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* What is your new manager like?
    - *B:* She's very polite and sensible, but she expects everyone to be hard-working and punctual.

## PART 1 — TENSES & ASPECT IN DEPTH (A2.1 & A2.2)

### UNIT 1: Past Simple vs. Past Continuous
- **Formula & Mechanics:**
  - Past Simple: `Subject + V2 (-ed / irregular)`
  - Past Continuous: `Subject + was/were + V-ing`
  - Interrupted Action: `Past Continuous (ongoing background) + WHEN + Past Simple (short action)`
  - Simultaneous Actions: `WHILE + Past Continuous, Past Continuous`
- **Rules & Usage:**
  - Use Past Continuous for background actions in progress at a specific past moment.
  - Use Past Simple for completed single events that interrupt or follow.
- **Examples:**
  - *I was sleeping when the alarm went off.*
  - *While we were driving home, it started to rain heavily.*
  - *She was studying while her brother was playing video games.*
- **Common Mistakes Diagnostic:**
  - Incorrect: *I was see him while I walked.* ➔ Correct: *I saw him while I was walking.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What were you doing when the power went out?
  - *B:* I was cooking dinner in the kitchen when everything suddenly went dark.

### UNIT 2: Present Perfect Simple (Experience, Duration & Recent Events)
- **Formula & Mechanics:**
  - Positive: `Subject + have/has + V3 (Past Participle)`
  - Negative: `Subject + have/has NOT (haven't/hasn't) + V3`
  - Question: `Have/Has + Subject + V3?`
- **Key Signal Words:**
  - *Ever / Never* (life experience): *Have you ever visited Rome? I have never eaten sushi.*
  - *For / Since* (duration from past to present): *I have lived here for 3 years / since 2023.*
  - *Just / Already / Yet* (recent events): *He has just left. I have already finished. Have you eaten yet?*
- **Contrast with Past Simple:**
  - Present Perfect = Unspecified time / Connection to present (*I have lost my key*).
  - Past Simple = Specific finished time (*I lost my key yesterday*).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Have you ever been to Paris?
  - *B:* Yes, I've been there twice! I went there last summer with my family.

### UNIT 3: Future Tenses Contrast (Will vs. Be Going To vs. Present Continuous)
- **Rules & Mechanics Matrix:**
  - `Will + Base Verb`: Spontaneous decisions, promises, offers, predictions without present proof. (*I'll have a coffee, please.* / *I'll help you.*)
  - `Be going to + Base Verb`: Prior plans, intentions, predictions based on clear physical evidence. (*I'm going to study law next year.* / *Look at those dark clouds; it's going to rain!*)
  - `Present Continuous (am/is/are + V-ing)`: Fixed arrangements with a specific time and location. (*I am meeting the doctor at 3 PM tomorrow.*)
- **Comparison Table:**
  - *I will meet him.* (Instant decision right now)
  - *I am going to meet him.* (My intention/plan)
  - *I am meeting him at 10 AM at Cafe Nero.* (Fixed arrangement with time & place confirmed)
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What are you doing this weekend?
  - *B:* I'm meeting Sarah on Saturday afternoon. We're going to buy a present for Alex.

## PART 2 — MODALS & EXPRESSING OBLIGATION / PERMISSION

### UNIT 4: Obligation & Necessity (Have to / Must / Don't have to / Mustn't)
- **Rules & Structural Mechanics:**
  - `Must + Base Verb`: Personal obligation / felt duty by speaker (*I must call my mother.*).
  - `Have to + Base Verb`: External rule, law, or authority (*Drivers have to stop at red lights.*).
  - `Don't have to + Base Verb`: Lack of obligation / optional (*You don't have to wear a tie; it's casual.*).
  - `Mustn't + Base Verb`: Prohibition / forbidden! (*You mustn't smoke in the hospital!*).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Do we have to wear formal clothes for the meeting?
  - *B:* No, you don't have to wear a suit, but you mustn't wear shorts!

### UNIT 5: Advice & Recommendations (Should / Shouldn't)

  - `Should / Shouldn't + Base Verb`
  - Used to give friendly advice, suggestions, or soft opinion.
- **Examples:**
  - *You look exhausted; you should go to bed early.*
  - *You shouldn't drink so much coffee before sleep.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* I have a terrible headache.
  - *B:* You should drink some water and rest in a quiet room.

### UNIT 6: Polite Requests & Permission (Could / Would you mind / May)
- **Structures:**

  - `Would you mind + Verb-ing?` (*Would you mind closing the window?*)

- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Would you mind opening the door for me?
  - *B:* Not at all! Here you go.

## PART 3 — CONDITIONALS & COMPARISONS

### UNIT 7: Zero & First Conditionals
- **Zero Conditional (General truths, scientific facts, habits):**
  - Formula: `IF + Present Simple, Present Simple`
  - Example: *If you heat ice, it melts.* / *If I drink coffee late, I don't sleep well.*
- **First Conditional (Real future possibilities):**
  - Formula: `IF + Present Simple, WILL + Base Verb`
  - Example: *If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.*
  - Unless = If not: *Unless you study, you won't pass.* (= *If you don't study...*)
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What will you do if the weather is nice tomorrow?
  - *B:* If it's sunny, we will go for a picnic in the park.

### UNIT 8: Second Conditional Introduction (Hypothetical Situations)
- **Formula & Rules:**
  - `IF + Past Simple, WOULD + Base Verb`
  - Expresses imaginary, unreal, or unlikely situations in the present or future.
  - Note: `If I were you...` (Standard advice form).
- **Examples:**
  - *If I won the lottery, I would buy a house on the coast.*
  - *If I were you, I would accept the job offer.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What would you do if you had a million pounds?
  - *B:* If I had a million pounds, I would travel around the world!

### UNIT 9: Advanced Comparatives & Superlatives
- **Modifiers for Comparison:**

  - `As + Adjective + As`: *He is as tall as his brother.*

  - `Too + Adjective + to + Verb`: *It's too cold to swim in the sea today.*
  - `Adjective + Enough + to + Verb`: *He is old enough to drive a car.*

### UNIT 11: Discourse Connectors & Conjunctions
- **Contrast:** *Although, Even though, However, But*
  - *Although it was raining, we went for a walk.*
  - *The exam was hard. However, everyone passed.*
- **Reason & Result:** *Because, Because of, So, Therefore*
  - *We stayed home because it was cold.*
  - *We stayed home because of the cold weather.*
  - *It was cold, so we stayed home.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Why was the flight cancelled?
  - *B:* Although the weather was clear here, the flight was cancelled because of a storm in Paris.

### UNIT 12: Basic Reported Speech (Statements & Backshift)
- **Backshift Rules (Present ➔ Past):**
  - Present Simple ➔ Past Simple (*"I am tired" ➔ He said he was tired.*)
  - Present Continuous ➔ Past Continuous (*"I am working" ➔ She said she was working.*)
  - Can ➔ Could (*"I can help" ➔ He said he could help.*)
  - Will ➔ Would (*"I will call you" ➔ She said she would call me.*)
- **Say vs Tell:**
  - `Say (that)...` (*He said that he was busy.*)
  - `Tell + Person (that)...` (*He told me that he was busy.*)
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* What did Mark say to you yesterday?
  - *B:* He told me that he was moving to London next month.

## PART 5 — PASSIVE VOICE (A2 PRODUCTION & RECOGNITION)

### UNIT 13: Passive Voice System
- **Formula:** `Subject + BE (in appropriate tense) + Past Participle (V3)`
- **Tenses:**
  - **Present Simple Passive (`am/is/are + V3`):**
    - *English is spoken all over the world.*
    - *Millions of emails are sent every day.*
  - **Past Simple Passive (`was/were + V3`):**
    - *This house was built in 1995.*
    - *The window was broken by the storm.*
  - **Future Passive Recognition (`will be + V3`):**
    - *The results will be announced tomorrow.*
- **Common Everyday Passive Expressions:**
  - *I was born in... / It is made of... / He was invited to... / She was told that... / The shop is closed.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* When was this historic bridge built?
  - *B:* It was built in 1850, and it is still used by thousands of cars every day.

## PART 6 — GERUNDS, INFINITIVES & VERB PATTERNS

### UNIT 14: Verb Patterns & Gerund vs. Infinitive System
- **Verbs Followed by Infinitive (`Verb + to + Base Verb`):**
  - *want to, need to, hope to, plan to, decide to, agree to, promise to, learn to, offer to, afford to*.
  - *I want to learn English.* / *She plans to travel next month.*
- **Verbs Followed by Gerund (`Verb + Verb-ing`):**
  - *like doing, love doing, hate doing, enjoy doing, finish doing, avoid doing, suggest doing, mind doing, keep doing*.
  - *I love swimming in the sea.* / *He finished writing the report.*
- **Verbs Changing Meaning (Gerund vs. Infinitive):**
  - **Start / Begin:** *start doing / start to do* (Same meaning: *It started raining / It started to rain*).
  - **Stop:**
    - `Stop doing` (Quit action): *He stopped smoking.*
    - `Stop to do` (Pause in order to do something else): *He stopped to smoke a cigarette.*
  - **Remember / Forget:**
    - `Remember to do` (Future obligation): *Remember to buy milk!*
    - `Remember doing` (Past memory): *I remember buying the tickets.*
    - `Forget to do` (Failed obligation): *I forgot to lock the door.*
    - `Forget doing` (Past memory): *I'll never forget meeting the President.*
  - **Try:**
    - `Try to do` (Make physical effort): *I tried to lift the heavy box.*
    - `Try doing` (Experiment/Test method): *If you can't sleep, try reading a book.*
- **Object + Infinitive Pattern (`Verb + Object + to + Infinitive`):**
  - *ask someone to do, tell someone to do, want someone to do, advise someone to do, invite someone to do*.
  - *She asked me to help her.* / *The doctor advised him to rest.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Did you remember to post the letter?
  - *B:* Oh no! I forgot to post it. I will do it right now.

## PART 7 — QUESTION TAGS

### UNIT 15: Question Tag System & Intonation
- **Forming Rules:** Affirmative statement ➔ Negative tag; Negative statement ➔ Affirmative tag.
- **Tense Balances:**
  - *Present Simple:* *You speak English, don't you?* / *She doesn't like coffee, does she?*
  - *Be Verb:* *It's cold today, isn't it?* / *They aren't late, are they?*
  - *Past Simple:* *You saw him yesterday, didn't you?*
  - *Modals:* *You can swim, can't you?* / *We shouldn't stay long, should we?*
- **Intonation Usage:**
  - Falling pitch ↘ (Confirming agreement, not a real question): *Lovely day, isn't it? ↘*
  - Rising pitch ↗ (Real question, seeking unknown info): *You haven't seen my keys, have you? ↗*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* You're coming to the party tonight, aren't you?
  - *B:* Yes, I am! I'll be there around 8 PM.

## PART 8 — INTENSIFIERS, AGREEMENT & ADDITIONS

### UNIT 16: So & Such / Either & Neither / Too, Also & As Well
- **So vs. Such:**
  - `So + Adjective/Adverb`: *She is so intelligent! / He runs so fast!*
  - `Such + (a/an) + Adjective + Noun`: *It was such a beautiful city! / They are such nice people!*
  - `So... that` / `Such... that`: *It was so hot that we couldn't sleep.*
- **Agreement: Either, Neither & Me Too / Me Neither:**
  - Positive agreement: *I love pizza. ➔ Me too! / So do I!*
  - Negative agreement: *I don't like horror movies. ➔ Me neither! / Neither do I!*
  - *Either... or / Neither... nor:* *You can choose either tea or coffee.* / *Neither Mark nor David came to the party.*
- **Too vs. Also vs. As Well:**
  - *Too* (end of sentence): *I want to come too.*
  - *As well* (end of sentence): *She speaks Spanish as well.*
  - *Also* (mid-position before main verb / after be): *He also plays the piano. / She is also a doctor.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* I didn't enjoy the movie at all.
  - *B:* Neither did I! It was such a boring plot.

## PART 9 — HABITS & PAST STATES

### UNIT 17: Used to & Be Used to
- **Used to + Base Verb (Past Habits & States):**
  - Meaning: Things that were true in the past but are no longer true now.
  - Positive: *I used to play football when I was a teenager.*
  - Negative: *She didn't use to like vegetables.*
  - Question: *Did you use to live in Manchester?*
- **Be Used to + Noun / Verb-ing (Familiarity / Habituation):**
  - Meaning: Accustomed to something; it is normal and no longer strange.
  - Positive: *I am used to getting up early for work.*
  - Negative: *He is not used to the cold weather here.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Did you use to commute by bicycle when you lived in Cambridge?
  - *B:* Yes, I did! But now I live in London, so I am used to taking the metro every day.

## PART 10 — TENSE ASPECT RECOGNITION

### UNIT 18: Present Perfect vs. Present Perfect Continuous (A2 Recognition)
- **Present Perfect Simple (`have/has + V3`):**
  - Focus: Result, completion, quantity (*how many*).
  - Example: *I have written three letters this morning.*
- **Present Perfect Continuous (`have/has been + V-ing`):**
  - Focus: Ongoing activity, process, duration (*how long*).
  - Example: *I have been writing letters all morning.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* You look exhausted, Alex! What have you been doing?
  - *B:* I have been painting the kitchen all morning, but I have already finished two walls!

## PART 11 — INDIRECT QUESTIONS & DESIRES

### UNIT 19: Indirect Polite Questions & Basic Wish
- **Indirect Questions (Polite Word Order - No Inversion!):**
  - Standard Direct: *Where is the bank?*
  - Polite Indirect: `Could you tell me where the bank is?`
  - Direct: *What time does the train leave?*
  - Indirect: `Do you know what time the train leaves?`
  - Direct: *Is there a supermarket nearby?*
  - Indirect: `Do you know if there is a supermarket nearby?`
- **Basic Wish (`I wish + Past Simple`):**
  - Expressing present regret or desire for a different state.
  - Example: *I wish I had a bigger house.* / *I wish I spoke fluent French.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Excuse me, could you tell me where the nearest pharmacy is?
  - *B:* Yes, go straight down this street and it's on the right, next to the bank.
