## PART 1 — ABOUT ME & PERSONAL IDENTITY

### UNIT 1: Personal Information & First Contact
- **1.1 First Contact & Basic Greetings**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Name, first name, last name, full name, nickname, greeting, hello, hi, morning, afternoon, evening, night, goodbye, bye.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Be, call, spell, say, meet, introduce, greet, wave.
  - **Adjectives:** First, last, full, nice, glad, new.
  - **Collocations:** First name, last name, full name, spell your name, say hello, wave goodbye.
  - **Set Phrases:** What's your name? / My name is... / I am... / Nice to meet you. / Good morning! / Good afternoon! / Good evening! / How are you? / I'm fine, thank you. / See you tomorrow! / Bye!
  - **Sentence Frames:** `My name is + [Name]`, `Nice to meet you, + [Name]`.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* Hello! My name is David. What's your name?
    - *B:* Hi David! I'm Sarah. Nice to meet you.
    - *A:* Nice to meet you too!
- **1.2 Personal Details & Contact Info**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** Age, birthday, date of birth, address, street, avenue, house number, apartment number, postcode/ZIP code, phone number, mobile phone, email address, website, passport, ID card, driver's licence, signature, form.
  - **Essential Verbs:** Live, have, write, sign, fill in, complete, show, check.
  - **Collocations:** Home address, email address, mobile phone number, date of birth, identity card, fill in a form, sign here.
  - **Set Phrases:** How old are you? / I'm 25 years old. / When is your birthday? / My birthday is on May 15th. / What's your email address? / My phone number is... / Could you spell your address? / Here is my passport.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* Can I have your phone number, please?
    - *B:* Sure! It's 07700 900123.
    - *A:* Thank you. And what's your email address?
    - *B:* It's sarah.smith@email.com.
- **1.3 Origin, Countries, Nationalities & Languages**
  - **Core Vocabulary:** World, continent, country, capital city, hometown, nationality, citizenship, language, mother tongue, foreign language, native speaker, flag, border, culture.
  - **Country / Nationality / Language Triads:**
    - United Kingdom ➔ British ➔ English
    - United States ➔ American ➔ English
    - Canada ➔ Canadian ➔ English / French
    - Australia ➔ Australian ➔ English
    - France ➔ French ➔ French
    - Germany ➔ German ➔ German
    - Italy ➔ Italian ➔ Italian
    - Spain ➔ Spanish ➔ Spanish
    - Portugal ➔ Portuguese ➔ Portuguese
    - Greece ➔ Greek ➔ Greek
    - Japan ➔ Japanese ➔ Japanese
    - China ➔ Chinese ➔ Mandarin / Chinese
    - India ➔ Indian ➔ Hindi / English
    - Brazil ➔ Brazilian ➔ Portuguese
    - Mexico ➔ Mexican ➔ Spanish
    - Ukraine ➔ Ukrainian ➔ Ukrainian
    - Poland ➔ Polish ➔ Polish
    - Turkey ➔ Turkish ➔ Turkish
  - **Essential Verbs:** Come from, speak, learn, study, teach, translate, understand, repeat, pronounce, practice.
  - **Set Phrases:** Where are you from? / I'm from Canada. / Where were you born? / I was born in Toronto. / I live in London. / Do you speak English? / I speak a little French. / How do you say this in English? / I'm learning English.
  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* Where are you from?
    - *B:* I'm from Spain, but I live in London now. Do you speak Spanish?
    - *A:* Un poco! I speak a little Spanish, but I'm learning.

  - **Micro-Dialogue:**
    - *A:* What's wrong?
    - *B:* I have a terrible headache and my throat is sore.

## PART 2 — NOUNS & POSSESSION

### UNIT 4: What is a Noun?
- Definition: A person, place, thing, or idea.
- Common Nouns: *city, book, dog, woman*. Proper Nouns: *London, Harry Potter, David*. Concrete Nouns: *table, apple*. Basic Abstract Nouns: *love, time, name*.

### UNIT 5: Singular Nouns
- Intro with Indefinite Articles *a/an*: Use *a* before consonant sounds (*a car, a house, a university*); use *an* before vowel sounds (*an apple, an egg, an hour*).

### UNIT 6: Plural Nouns
- **6.1 Regular Plural Ending :** *book ➔ books, car ➔ cars, pen ➔ pens*.
- **6.2 Plural Endings  after :** *bus ➔ buses, watch ➔ watches, box ➔ boxes, dish ➔ dishes*.
- **6.3 Nouns Ending in :**
  - Consonant + y ➔ -ies: *city ➔ cities, baby ➔ babies, country ➔ countries*.
  - Vowel + y ➔ -s: *boy ➔ boys, day ➔ days, key ➔ keys*.
- **6.4 Nouns Ending in :** *knife ➔ knives, half ➔ halves, wife ➔ wives, leaf ➔ leaves*.
- **6.5 Nouns Ending in :** *potato ➔ potatoes, tomato ➔ tomatoes* (vs *photo ➔ photos, piano ➔ pianos*).
- **6.6 Irregular Plurals:** *man ➔ men, woman ➔ women, child ➔ children, person ➔ people, foot ➔ feet, tooth ➔ teeth, mouse ➔ mice, sheep ➔ sheep, fish ➔ fish*.

### UNIT 7: Countable Nouns
- Definition: Things you can count individually (*one apple, two apples*).
- Rules: Can be singular or plural; can use numbers, *a/an*, *many*, *a few*.
- Examples: *I have two books. How many apples are there?*

### UNIT 8: Uncountable Nouns
- Definition: Mass items, liquids, materials, concepts you cannot count individually.
- Examples: *water, coffee, milk, bread, rice, money, information, advice, furniture, luggage, weather*.
- Rules: Always singular verb; no *a/an*; use *some, much, a little*.
- Container Phrases: *a glass of water, a cup of coffee, a loaf of bread, a piece of information*.

### UNIT 9: Nouns with Dual Usage
- Context determines countable vs uncountable:
  - *a chicken* (the animal) vs *chicken* (the meat).
  - *a coffee* (a cup of coffee) vs *coffee* (the substance).
  - *a glass* (the container) vs *glass* (the material).

### UNIT 10: Expressing Possession
- **10.1 Possessive  for Singular:** *John's car, my mother's house, the cat's tail*.
- **10.2 Possessive  for Regular Plurals:** *my parents' house, the students' books*.
- **10.3 Irregular Plural Possession:** *the children's toys, men's clothes, women's shoes*.
- **10.4 Possessive  for Inanimate Objects:** *the door of the house, the top of the page, the end of the movie*.
- **10.5 Question Word :**
  - *Whose bag is this? It's Sarah's.*
  - *Whose shoes are these? They're mine / John's.*

## PART 3 — ARTICLES & DETERMINERS

### UNIT 11: Indefinite Article 'a'
- Used before countable singular nouns starting with a consonant sound.
- Examples: *a book, a teacher, a car, a European city, a university* (starts with /j/ sound).

### UNIT 12: Indefinite Article 'an'
- Used before countable singular nouns starting with a vowel sound.
- Examples: *an apple, an egg, an insect, an orange, an umbrella, an hour* (silent h).

### UNIT 13: Definite Article 'the'
- Used when the speaker and listener know the specific item, when previously mentioned, or for unique things.
- Specific / Known: *The car outside is blue. Pass me the salt, please.*
- Second Mention: *I bought a book yesterday. The book is very interesting.*
- Unique Things: *the sun, the moon, the sky, the world, the internet, the capital city*.

### UNIT 14: Zero Article
- No article used for:
  - Plural & uncountable nouns in general sense: *I love dogs. Water is essential for life.*
  - Names of people, most countries, cities, streets: *John, France, Paris, Oxford Street*.
  - Languages & school subjects: *I speak English. She studies maths.*
  - Meals: *eat breakfast, have lunch, eat dinner*.
  - Certain institutions (in general role): *go to bed, be at school, go to work, at home*.

### UNIT 15: Master Article Choice Flowchart
- Step 1: Is the noun countable or uncountable?
- Step 2: If countable plural or uncountable ➔ Is it general or specific? General = Zero Article. Specific = *The*.
- Step 3: If countable singular ➔ Is it general/first mention or specific/known? First mention = *A / An*. Specific = *The*.

## PART 5 — ADJECTIVES & COMPARISONS

### UNIT 21: Adjective Order & Descriptive Categories
- Position: Adjectives go BEFORE nouns (*a blue car*) or AFTER linking verbs (*the car is blue*).
- Adjective Order Rules (OSASCOMP):
  1. Opinion (*beautiful, nice*)
  2. Size (*big, small*)
  3. Age (*old, young, new*)
  4. Shape (*round, square*)
  5. Colour (*red, blue*)
  6. Origin (*British, French*)
  7. Material (*wooden, leather*)
- Example: *A beautiful small old round wooden table.*

### UNIT 22: Comparatives & Superlatives
- **Comparatives (comparing two things):**
  - Short adjectives (1 syllable): Add  (*tall ➔ taller than, fast ➔ faster than*).
  - Short ending in :  (*happy ➔ happier than, easy ➔ easier than*).
  - Long adjectives (2+ syllables):  (*more expensive than, more beautiful than*).
  - Irregulars: *good ➔ better than, bad ➔ worse than, far ➔ farther/further than*.
- **Superlatives (comparing three or more):**
  - Short adjectives:  (*the tallest, the fastest*).
  - Ending in :  (*the happiest, the easiest*).
  - Long adjectives:  (*the most expensive, the most beautiful*).
  - Irregulars: *good ➔ the best, bad ➔ the worst, far ➔ the farthest/furthest*.
- **Equality Comparison:**  (*He is as tall as his father.*).

## PART 7 — QUANTIFIERS

### UNIT 24: Quantifiers & Determiners
- **Some vs Any:**
  - *Some* in affirmative sentences (*I have some apples. I want some water.*) and polite offers/requests (*Would you like some coffee? Can I have some water?*).
  - *Any* in negative sentences (*I don't have any money.*) and general questions (*Do you have any questions?*).
- **Much vs Many:**
  - *Much* with uncountable nouns (*How much money do you have? Not much.*).
  - *Many* with countable plural nouns (*How many books are there? Not many.*).
- **A lot of / Lots of:** Used with both countable and uncountable nouns in affirmative sentences (*I have a lot of friends. There is a lot of water.*).
- **A few vs A little:**
  - *A few* with countable plural nouns (*a few days, a few friends*).
  - *A little* with uncountable nouns (*a little sugar, a little time*).
- **Each / Every / All:**
  - *Each / Every* + singular noun (*Every student has a book.*).
  - *All* + plural/uncountable noun (*All students are here. All the water is gone.*).

## PART 8 — PREPOSITIONS

### UNIT 25: Prepositions of Time, Place & Movement
- **Prepositions of Time:**
  -  for specific times, clock times, holidays: *at 5 o'clock, at night, at noon, at Christmas*.
  -  for days and dates: *on Monday, on my birthday, on May 15th*.
  -  for months, years, seasons, centuries, parts of day: *in May, in 2026, in summer, in the morning/afternoon/evening*.
- **Prepositions of Place:**
  -  inside an enclosed space/city/country: *in the room, in London, in France, in a car*.
  -  on a surface/floor/street: *on the table, on the wall, on the 2nd floor, on Oxford Street*.
  -  at a specific point/location/event: *at the bus stop, at the door, at home, at school, at work, at a party*.
  - Other position prepositions: *under, over, behind, in front of, next to, beside, between, opposite, near*.
- **Prepositions of Movement:** *to, into, out of, onto, off, across, through, along, past, towards*.

## PART 9 — VERB SYSTEM & MODALS

### UNIT 26: Verb System & Modal Verbs
- **Auxiliary Verbs (Be, Have, Do):**
  - *Be* (am, is, are, was, were) ➔ used for continuous tenses & passive voice.
  - *Have* (have, has, had) ➔ used for perfect tenses.
  - *Do* (do, does, did) ➔ used for negatives & questions in simple tenses.
- **Modal Verbs System:**
  - **Can / Cannot (Can't):** Ability & permission in present (*I can swim. Can I sit here?*).
  - **Could / Couldn't:** Past ability & polite requests (*I could swim when I was 5. Could you help me?*).
  - **Must / Must not (Mustn't):** Strong obligation & prohibition (*You must stop at red light. You mustn't smoke here.*).
  - **Have to / Don't have to:** External obligation vs lack of obligation (*I have to go to work. You don't have to pay; it's free.*).
  - **Should / Shouldn't:** Advice & recommendations (*You should eat vegetables. You shouldn't stay up late.*).
  - **May / Might:** Possibility (*It might rain later. May I come in?*).

## PART 10 — TENSE SYSTEM

### UNIT 27: Present Simple, Present Continuous, Past Simple & Future Forms
- **27.1 Present Simple Tense**
  - Use: Habits, daily routines, general truths, permanent states.
  - Formula:
  - Examples: *I live in London. She works in a bank. Water boils at 100°C.*
  - Negative/Question:  /
- **27.2 Present Continuous Tense**
  - Use: Actions happening right now, temporary situations, changing trends.
  - Formula:
  - Examples: *I am writing an email right now. She is staying at a hotel this week.*
  - Stative verbs exception: Verbs of state, emotion, possession (*know, like, love, want, have, believe*) are NOT usually used in continuous forms (*I want a coffee*, NOT *I am wanting*).
- **27.3 Past Simple Tense**
  - Use: Completed actions in the past with a finished time expression (*yesterday, last week, in 2020*).
  - Formula:
  - Regulars: *walk ➔ walked, play ➔ played, live ➔ lived*.
  - Common Irregulars: *go ➔ went, see ➔ saw, eat ➔ ate, buy ➔ bought, take ➔ took, come ➔ came, do ➔ did, have ➔ had, be ➔ was/were*.
  - Negative/Question:  /
  - Examples: *I visited Paris last year. Did you see John yesterday? I didn't go out.*
- **27.4 Future Forms: Be Going To vs Will**
  - **Be going to ():** Prior plans/intentions & predictions based on present evidence (*I'm going to study engineering. Look at those dark clouds, it's going to rain!*).
  - **Will ():** Instant decisions, promises, offers, future facts (*I'll help you with those bags! I'll call you tomorrow. Next year will be 2027.*).

## PART 11 — QUESTIONS & NEGATION

### UNIT 28: Question Formation & Negation System
- **28.1 Yes/No Questions**
  - Structure:
  - Present Simple: *Do you speak English? Does she live here?*
  - Present Continuous: *Are you reading? Is it raining?*
  - Past Simple: *Did you enjoy the trip?*
  - Verb To Be: *Are you hungry? Was he at home?*
- **28.2 Wh- Questions**
  - Question Words: *Who, What, Where, When, Why, Which, Whose, How, How much, How many, How old, How far*.
  - Structure:
  - Examples: *Where do you live? What time does the lesson start? Why did you call me?*
- **28.3 Question Tags (Basic A1 intro)**
  - Positive statement ➔ Negative tag: *It's cold today, isn't it? You speak English, don't you?*
  - Negative statement ➔ Positive tag: *You aren't tired, are you? She doesn't like tea, does she?*
- **28.4 Imperatives & Instructions**
  - Positive: Base verb (*Open the door! Sit down, please.*).
  - Negative: *Don't + base verb* (*Don't touch that! Don't worry.*).
- **28.5 There is / There are**
  - Singular / Uncountable: *There is a book on the table. There is water in the glass.*
  - Plural: *There are three apples in the bag. Are there any questions?*
- **28.6 Have got vs Have**
  - British/Informal variant of possessive *have*:
  - *I have got a new car. / Have you got a pen? / She hasn't got any money.*
