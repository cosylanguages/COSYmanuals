## PART 1 — ADVANCED SYNTAX & MODAL MECHANICS

### UNIT 1: Advanced Modal Nuance & Deduction
- **Modal Deduction & Speculation in Past & Present:**
  - `Must have + V3` (High confidence positive past deduction): *The floor is wet; it must have rained.*
  - `Can't have / Couldn't have + V3` (High confidence negative past deduction): *He can't have committed the fraud; he was abroad.*
  - `Might have / May have / Could have + V3` (Possibility past deduction): *They might have missed the announcement.*
  - `Bound to / Highly likely to + Base Verb` (Expectation/Certainty): *The proposal is bound to succeed.*
- **Modal Criticism & Unfulfilled Expectation:**
  - `Should have / Ought to have + V3`: *You should have informed the team earlier.*
  - `Was/Were supposed to + Base Verb`: *The flight was supposed to depart at 8 AM.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Why wasn't the report submitted on time?
  - *B:* Alex was supposed to send the final draft, but he must have run into technical difficulties.

### UNIT 2: Ellipsis & Substitution Techniques
- **Ellipsis (Omitting redundant clauses/phrases):**
  - Omission in comparative clauses: *She scored higher than expected [to score].*
  - Omission in compound sentences: *Sarah handled the client call, and Marcus [handled] the contract.*
- **Substitution via Pronouns & Auxiliaries:**
  - `one / ones`: *I don't like the blue shirt, but I'll take the red one.*
  - `do so`: *If you wish to appeal the decision, you must do so in writing within 14 days.*
  - `so / not`: *Will the market recover this quarter? I hope so. / I suspect not.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Are you planning to apply for the managerial vacancy?
  - *B:* If my supervisor advises me to do so, I definitely will.

### UNIT 3: Advanced Determiners & Quantifiers
- **Nuanced Quantifiers:**
  - *Few vs A few* (Few = almost none [negative connotation]; A few = some [positive connotation]): *Few candidates passed the exam.* vs *A few candidates passed.*
  - *Little vs A little* (Little = scarce; A little = small amount).
  - *Far fewer vs Far less* (Fewer for countable nouns; Less for uncountable nouns): *We received far fewer applications this year, resulting in far less work for HR.*
  - *A great deal of / A vast amount of / Plenty of*: *A great deal of effort was put into the presentation.*

### UNIT 4: Nominalization & Academic Register Shifts
- **Nominalization (Converting verbs/adjectives into noun structures for academic/formal weight):**
  - Active/Verbal: *We analyzed the market data thoroughly and decided to expand.*
  - Nominalized: **A thorough analysis of the market data led to the decision to expand.**
  - Active/Verbal: *The government intervened quickly, which prevented the crisis from escalating.*
  - Nominalized: **Prompt government intervention prevented the escalation of the crisis.**

### UNIT 5: Hedging, Nuance & Stance Devices
- **Hedging Devices (Softening claims to ensure academic accuracy and caution):**
  - Probability Adverbs: *arguably, apparently, presumably, relatively, reportedly*.
  - Epistemic Verbs: *tend to, appear to, seem to, indicate that, suggest that*.
  - Modal Expressions: *It is widely believed that..., The findings would suggest that...*
  - Example: *The data would seem to indicate a gradual shift in consumer behavior toward sustainable products.*

### UNIT 6: Fronting & Cleft Sentences
- **Fronting for Rhetorical Emphasis (Inversion):**
  - Inversion after negative/restrictive adverbs: *Never have I seen such dedication.* / *Rarely do we encounter such resistance.* / *Not only did he complete the project, but he also saved costs.*
- **It-Clefts & Wh-Clefts:**
  - It-Cleft: `It + BE + [Focused Item] + THAT/WHO...`: *It was the CEO who approved the budget allocation.*
  - Wh-Cleft: `WHAT + [Clause] + IS/WAS...`: *What we urgently require is a clear strategic direction.*

### UNIT 7: Advanced Relative Clauses & Reduced Structures
- **Advanced Relative Pronouns:** *whereby, wherein, whose, of which, to whom*.
  - *The contract contains a clause whereby either party may terminate the agreement.*
- **Reduced Relative Clauses (Active -ing vs Passive -ed):**
  - Active participle clause: *The lawyer representing the firm issued a statement.* (= who represents...)
  - Passive participle clause: *The report submitted to the board highlighted major risks.* (= which was submitted...)

### UNIT 8: Advanced Reporting Verbs & Distancing
- **Reporting Verbs for Stance:** *alleged, claim, assert, contend, concede, refute, imply*.
  - *The plaintiff contends that the company breached the agreement.*
- **Impersonal Passive Structures for Distancing:**
  - `It is + V3 + THAT...`: *It is alleged that corporate funds were misused.*
  - `Subject + BE + V3 + TO + Verb`: *The company is reported to be planning a major restructuring.*

### UNIT 9: Advanced Discourse Markers & Linking Masterclass
- **Transitioning across complex logical relationships:**
  - Concession: *albeit, notwithstanding, even so, nevertheless*.
  - Contrast: *conversely, in stark contrast, whereas, on the contrary*.
  - Addition/Reinforcement: *furthermore, moreover, not to mention, in equal measure*.
  - Result/Consequence: *consequently, hence, thereby, accordingly*.
