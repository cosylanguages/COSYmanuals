## PART 1 — PERSONALITY, PSYCHOLOGY & EMOTIONS

### UNIT 1: Character Nuances, Temperament & Self-Identity (B1.1)
- **Core Nouns:** Identity, ambition, empathy, self-confidence, insecurity, temperament, charisma, reliability, integrity, attitude, perspective, self-esteem, flaw, virtue.
- **Essential Verbs:** Express, project, reflect, admire, struggle with, overcome, perceive, misjudge, cultivate, embody.
- **Adjectives:** Ambitious, compassionate, insecure, charismatic, level-headed, unpredictable, conscientious, arrogant, humble, introverted, extroverted, dependable, manipulative, sincere.
- **Adverbs:** Conscientiously, sincerely, arrogantly, humbly, predictably, remarkably.
- **Phrasal Verbs:** Come across as, look up to, judge by, pass for, stand out.
- **Collocations:** Project self-confidence, level-headed leader, boost self-esteem, charismatic personality, quiet confidence, inherent flaw.
- **Fixed Expressions:** First impressions can be deceiving. / What you see is what you get. / She has a heart of gold.
- **Sentence Starters:** `What I admire most about her is...`, `He has a tendency to be... under pressure`.
- **Discourse Markers:** `As a matter of fact`, `Mind you`, `Strictly speaking`.
- **Synonyms & Antonyms:** Level-headed ↔ Unstable; Conscientious ↔ Careless; Humble ↔ Arrogant.
- **Word Families:** Empathy (n) ➔ Empathize (v) ➔ Empathetic (adj) ➔ Empathetically (adv).
- **False Friends:** *Sensible* (means reasonable/wise, NOT sensitive!).
- **Register Control:** Neutral (*reliable*) vs Formal (*dependable*) vs Informal (*solid*).
- **Pronunciation Alert:** Conscientious /ˌkɒn.ʃiˈen.ʃəs/ (stress on 3rd syllable).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* How did the interview with the candidate go?
  - *B:* She made a great impression! She comes across as highly conscientious and level-headed.

## PART 1 — TENSES & ASPECT IN DEPTH (B1.1 & B1.2)

### UNIT 1: Present Perfect Continuous vs. Present Perfect Simple
- **Formula & Structural Mechanics:**
  - Present Perfect Continuous: `have/has been + Verb-ing`
  - Present Perfect Simple: `have/has + Past Participle (V3)`
- **Key Distinctions:**
  - *Continuous* emphasizes duration, ongoing activity, or temporal activity: *I have been studying for 3 hours.*
  - *Simple* emphasizes completion, result, or quantity: *I have finished 3 chapters of the book.*
- **Stative Verbs Exception:** Stative verbs (*know, believe, belong, like*) use Simple form even for duration (*I have known him for ten years*, NOT *I have been knowing*).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* You look tired! What have you been doing?
  - *B:* I've been writing my dissertation all afternoon. I've completed two sections so far.

### UNIT 2: Past Perfect Simple & Past Perfect Continuous
- **Formula & Structural Mechanics:**
  - Past Perfect Simple: `had + Past Participle (V3)`
  - Past Perfect Continuous: `had been + Verb-ing`
- **Usage:**
  - Past Perfect Simple describes an action completed BEFORE another past event: *When we arrived at the cinema, the movie had already started.*
  - Past Perfect Continuous describes an ongoing background action up until a past point: *He was exhausted because he had been driving for 8 hours.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Did you manage to see the manager yesterday?
  - *B:* No, by the time I arrived at the office, she had already left for a conference.

### UNIT 3: Future in the Past (Was going to / Was about to / Was due to)
- **Structures:**
  - `Was/Were going to + Base Verb`: Unfulfilled or fulfilled past intentions (*I was going to call you, but my battery died.*).
  - `Was/Were about to + Base Verb`: Immediate past intentions (*The train was about to leave when she ran onto the platform.*).
  - `Was/Were due to + Base Verb`: Scheduled past expectations (*The meeting was due to start at 9 AM.*).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Why didn't you attend the seminar yesterday?
  - *B:* I was about to leave home when an urgent client issue came up.

## PART 2 — PASSIVES, CAUSATIVES & IMPERSONAL STRUCTURES

### UNIT 4: Passive Voice Across Tenses
- **Formula Matrix:** `Subject + BE (tense) + Past Participle (V3) (+ BY agent)`
  - Present Continuous Passive: *The building is being renovated.*
  - Past Continuous Passive: *The car was being repaired when I arrived.*
  - Present Perfect Passive: *The report has been published.*
  - Modal Passive: *The rules must be followed.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Has the new software patch been deployed yet?
  - *B:* Yes, it was deployed last night, and all systems are being monitored.

### UNIT 5: Causatives (Have / Get Something Done)
- **Formula:** `Subject + HAVE / GET + Object + Past Participle (V3)`
- **Usage:** Used when arranging for someone else (usually a professional) to perform a service.
  - *I had my car repaired yesterday.* (= A mechanic fixed it).
  - *She is getting her hair cut tomorrow.* (= A hairdresser will cut it).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Your apartment looks incredible!
  - *B:* Thanks! We had it painted and decorated by professionals last month.

## PART 3 — CONDITIONALS, WISH & MODALS OF DEDUCTION

### UNIT 6: Second & Third Conditionals + Mixed Conditionals Recognition
- **Second Conditional (Unreal Present):** `IF + Past Simple, WOULD + Base Verb` (*If I had time, I would travel.*).
- **Third Conditional (Unreal Past Regret):** `IF + Past Perfect, WOULD HAVE + V3` (*If I had studied, I would have passed the exam.*).
- **Mixed Conditional Recognition (Past Cause ➔ Present Result):** `IF + Past Perfect, WOULD + Base Verb` (*If I had taken that job in London, I would be living there now.*).
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Did you buy that apartment last year?
  - *B:* No, but if I had saved more money, I would have bought it!

### UNIT 7: Expressing Wishes & Regrets (Wish + Past / Would)
- `I wish + Past Simple` (Present regret): *I wish I had a bigger car.*
- `I wish + Past Perfect` (Past regret): *I wish I hadn't said those words.*
- `I wish + WOULD + Base Verb` (Annoyance at someone's behavior): *I wish you would stop tapping your pen!*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* I'm so stressed about the upcoming exam.
  - *B:* I wish I had started revising earlier instead of procrastinating!

### UNIT 8: Modals of Deduction & Speculation (Present & Past)
- **Present Deduction:**
  - *Must be* (90% certain positive): *He has three cars; he must be rich.*
  - *Can't be* (90% certain negative): *She just ate; she can't be hungry.*
  - *Might / Could / May be* (50% possible): *He might be at the library.*
- **Past Deduction:**
  - `Must have + V3`: *She passed with 100%; she must have studied hard.*
  - `Can't have + V3`: *He was in London; he can't have committed the crime.*
  - `Might have + V3`: *They might have missed the train.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Sarah hasn't arrived for the meeting yet.
  - *B:* She must have been delayed by the heavy morning traffic.

## PART 4 — CLAUSES, RELATIVES & CLEFT STRUCTURES

### UNIT 9: Relative Clauses (Defining, Non-defining, Whose & Reduced Relatives)
- **Defining Relative Clauses:** Essential information without commas (*The engineer who designed the bridge won an award.*).
- **Non-defining Relative Clauses:** Extra information separated by commas (*My brother, who lives in Paris, is an architect.*).
- **Possessive Relative `Whose`:** *I met a writer whose book won the Nobel Prize.*
- **Reduced Relative Clauses (Recognition):**
  - Active (`V-ing`): *The girl standing by the door is my sister.* (= who is standing...)
  - Passive (`V3`): *The cars manufactured in Japan are reliable.* (= which are manufactured...)
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Is that the candidate who applied for the senior role?
  - *B:* Yes, and her portfolio, which was submitted yesterday, is impressive.

### UNIT 10: Basic Cleft Sentences & Structural Emphasis
- **It-Clefts:** `It was + [Focus] + THAT / WHO...` (*It was John who solved the issue.*).
- **Wh-Clefts:** `What I + [Verb] + IS / WAS...` (*What I need right now is a good rest.*).
- **Emphasis with Auxiliary DO/DID:** *I do hope you can come! / She did complete the project on time.*
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Did Sarah design the company logo?
  - *B:* No, it was Alex who created the logo, but Sarah approved it.

## PART 5 — DISCOURSE CONNECTORS & LINKING DEVICES MASTERCLASS

### UNIT 11: Masterclass in Discourse Connectors
- **Addition:** *Furthermore, moreover, in addition, besides, also*.
- **Contrast:** *However, although, even though, in spite of, despite, on the other hand, whereas*.
- **Cause & Reason:** *Because, since, as, due to, owing to, on account of*.
- **Result & Consequence:** *Therefore, consequently, as a result, thus, so*.
- **Purpose:** *In order to, so as to, so that*.
- **Condition:** *Provided that, as long as, unless, in case*.
- **Exemplification & Summary:** *For instance, such as, to sum up, in conclusion*.
- **Micro-Dialogue:**
  - *A:* Although the project faced budget constraints, we completed it on schedule.
  - *B:* Consequently, the client was extremely satisfied with our team's performance.
