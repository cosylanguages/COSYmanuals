Русский язык — Личные местоимения
A1 / Beginner

Russian personal pronouns point to who is speaking or being spoken about.

Why is this important? Personal pronouns are the foundational building blocks for forming any simple sentence in Russian. Without them, you cannot introduce your family, talk about your friends, or describe objects.
⚠ Why learners struggle: Russian splits "you" into informal (ты) and formal/plural (вы). Additionally, non-human objects are referred to as "he" (он) or "she" (она) depending on grammatical gender—there is no universal "it".

Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
1st Person Singular Я говорю по-русски. "Я" (I) is not capitalized unless it's the first word.
3rd Person Feminine Она любит кофе. "Она" (she) is used for women and feminine objects.
1st Person Plural Мы читаем книги. "Мы" means "we" in Russian.

👀 Notice

Which pronoun do we use when addressing a single person formally (e.g., a teacher or boss)?

🧠 Think like Russian

Russian cares more about endings than position. Nouns carry their gender wherever they go. Because of this, Russian speakers don't think of objects as genderless "its"—a table is "he", a book is "she", and a window is "it" (оно). Match your pronouns to the word's ending!

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence / Form ✓ Correct Standard Form
Где книга? — Оно тут. Где книга? — Она тут. (книга is feminine!)
To teacher: Ты учитель. To teacher: Вы учитель. (Use formal Вы with seniors!)

💡 Memory Trick

Think of Я (Ya) as saying "Ya, that's ME!" Think of the informal Ты (Ty) as pointing to a single friend, and formal/plural Вы (Vy) as a polite bow to a group or a teacher.

🔗 This prepares you for learning Russian noun genders and matching verb endings.
