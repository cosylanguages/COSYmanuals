English — Plural Nouns
A1 / Beginner

We add -s to nouns to talk about more than one thing.

Why is this important? You will use this rule constantly when buying things, ordering food, or describing collections of objects. Adding plurals expands your world beyond "just one".
⚠ Why learners struggle: While adding a simple -s is easy, English has a small handful of very common irregular nouns (like "child" → "children" or "person" → "people") that do not follow this pattern at all.

⭐ Fully Coloured Examples
Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
Regular +S I buy apples. Standard regular plural ending.
Regular +ES She has boxes. Add -es to words ending in -ch, -sh, -x, -s.
Irregular Plural We see people. Irregular plural of "person" (no ending added).

👀 Notice

Which letters do we add when a noun ends in a hissing sound like -x or -s?

🧠 Think like English

English loves making words easy to say. When words end in hissing sounds (like box, brush, beach), adding a simple "s" is hard to pronounce. That's why English inserts an "e" to make "boxes" or "brushes" sound clear and natural.

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence ✓ Correct Standard Sentence
I have three childs. I have three children. (Never add -s to irregulars!)
Many persons buy books. Many people buy books.

💡 Memory Trick

Think of irregular plurals as VIP words: they are too special to wear the standard "+s" jacket, so they completely change their outfits (like "man" to "men") instead!

🔗 This prepares you for using "there are" when talking about multiple objects or items.
