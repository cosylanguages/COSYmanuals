English — Pronouns
A1 / Beginner

Pronouns replace nouns so we don't have to repeat names.

Why is this important? You will use pronouns in almost every sentence you speak. They keep conversations fluid and natural by letting you point to people without repeating their names over and over.
⚠ Why learners struggle: English strictly requires subject pronouns. You cannot omit them like you can in Spanish, Italian, or Russian when the context seems obvious.

Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
1st Person Singular I am a teacher. "I" is always capitalized in English.
3rd Person Singular She is Italian. Replaces a singular female person.
3rd Person Plural They are here. Replaces multiple people or objects.

👀 Notice

Which pronoun always stays capitalized, no matter where it appears in a sentence?

🧠 Think like English

English cares about word order and explicit subjects. Because English verbs rarely change their spelling to show who is speaking (e.g., "speak" can mean I speak, you speak, we speak, they speak), the pronoun is absolutely mandatory to understand the sentence.

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence ✓ Correct Standard Sentence
Is a nice day. It is a nice day. (Never leave out the subject pronoun!)
Me am ready. I am ready.

💡 Memory Trick

Subject pronouns are the "main actors" in the sentence. They must stand proudly *before* the verb to kick off the action!

🔗 This prepares you for the Present Simple, where subject pronouns decide the ending of the verb.
