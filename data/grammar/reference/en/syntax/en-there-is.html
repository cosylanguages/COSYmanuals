English — There is / There are
A1 / Beginner

We use "there is" and "there are" to state that something exists.

Why is this important? You will use this grammar constantly when looking for places in a new city, ordering items at a counter, or describing your surroundings during travel.
⚠ Why learners struggle: Many languages use the exact same word or verb for both single and multiple items (like "hay" in Spanish, "il y a" in French, or "есть" in Russian). English strictly splits them.

⭐ Fully Coloured Examples
Standard sentence patterns (Between 2 and 5 examples maximum)
Singular (+ Singular Noun) There is a park here. Use "is" with one item (often with "a" or "an").
Plural (+ Plural Noun) There are three shops nearby. Use "are" with multiple items (noun gets plural ending).
Singular Negative There isn't a bus today. Negative form for a single missing item.

👀 Notice

Which word changes inside "There is" and "There are" to tell us if we are talking about one item or many?

🧠 Think like English

English is obsessed with numbering and counting. Before we even say what the item is, we prepare the listener by choosing "There is" (prepare for one) or "There are" (prepare for a crowd). It keeps the listener's brain organized!

⚠ Common Mistakes
✗ Incorrect Sentence ✓ Correct Standard Sentence
There is many shops here. There are many shops here.
Have a park in the city. There is a park in the city. (Never use "have" to mean "there is/are"!)

💡 Memory Trick

Remember: IS is for Individual (singular, 1 item). ARE is for All of them (plural, 2+ items)!

🔗 This prepares you for making questions like "Is there...?" and "Are there...?" when traveling.
