# COSYlanguages A0-A1 Grammar Revision Roadmap

This document outlines the systematic, page-by-page revision mapping and checklists for all A0-A1 (Starter/Elementary) grammar points in the English and Russian manuals, fully calibrated to ensure maximum cognitive accessibility for pure beginner (A0) and high-beginner (A1) adult learners.

---

## 1. English A0-A1 Revision Checklist

| Page/Concept | Target Vocabulary Theme | Visual Diagram Type | "Think like English" Key Insight | "Can you already use this?" Communication Checkboxes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Basic Word Order** | Family | `Who?` ➔ `does what?` ➔ `to whom?` | Word order is absolute; endings don't tell the story. | ☐ I can structure a basic English sentence.<br>☐ I can identify Subject, Verb, and Object. |
| **Pronouns** | People you know | Grid mapping of: `I ➔ me ➔ my ➔ mine` | English separate pronouns clearly so who is who is never confused. | ☐ I can refer to myself and others correctly.<br>☐ I can express basic ownership. |
| **Verb 'To Be'** | Jobs & Nationalities | Positive, Negative, Question Block transitions | "To Be" links people to who or what they are. | ☐ I can state my name, job, and nationality.<br>☐ I can ask someone where they are from. |
| **Articles: A / An / The** | Everyday Objects | `New` (a) ➔ `Same One` (the) story flow | Articles let the listener know if info is new or shared. | ☐ I can introduce a new object with "a/an".<br>☐ I can refer to a specific object with "the". |
| **Plural Nouns** | Shopping | Ending `-s` magnet diagram | Regular endings get an `-s` but some are irregular and change shape. | ☐ I can talk about more than one object.<br>☐ I can shop for multiple items correctly. |
| **Present Simple** | Routines & Habits | `Subject` ➔ `Verb(s)` ➔ `Object` | English uses Present Simple for general truths, separate from now. | ☐ I can talk about my daily routine.<br>☐ I can express my likes and dislikes. |

## 2. Russian A0-A1 Revision Checklist

| Page/Concept | Target Vocabulary Theme | Visual Diagram Type | "Think like Russian" Key Insight | "Can you already use this?" Communication Checkboxes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Alphabet & Sounds** | Names | Sound Softness / Hardness blocks | Letters are stable; sounds can change, but spelling holds true. | ☐ I can read basic Russian words.<br>☐ I can spell my name in Russian. |
| **Noun Gender** | Family & Names | Hard/Soft Ending Families (`-а/-я`, `-о/-е`) | Word endings (not order) tell the brain the word's role. | ☐ I can identify the gender of any Russian noun.<br>☐ I can match genders to members of my family. |
| **Verb 'To Be' (быть)** | Locations & Past | Present/Past Tense timeline with null/был/была | Present "is" is hidden (zero verb), but past is loud and clear. | ☐ I can state where I am right now.<br>☐ I can say where I was yesterday. |
| **Pronouns** | People | Personal ➔ Possessive matches | Pronouns agree with the gender of the noun being owned. | ☐ I can say "my", "your", and "our" in Russian.<br>☐ I can refer to family members and their belongings. |
| **Basic Case (Prepositional)** | Cities & Countries | Location `где?` ➔ `-е` ending flow | Cases are roles; changing the ending turns "where" into an action. | ☐ I can say which city/country I am in.<br>☐ I can talk about where my family lives. |

## 3. General A0-A1 Pedagogical Rules

1. **No jargon:** Avoid terms like "Present Indicative" or "Nominative Declension" without immediate, plain-language translation.
2. **Recognition First:** Always ask the student to identify endings or patterns before forcing them to construct a full word form.
3. **No early exceptions:** Establish 100% confidence in the primary rule before exposing any exceptions.
