## PART I — The COSYlanguages Philosophy

### 1. Mission & Vision

#### Mission
COSYlanguages exists to help learners understand how languages work, not simply memorize how they look. Our materials are designed for learners who want to think in another language rather than constantly translate from their native language.
- Grammar is not treated as a list of rules.
- Vocabulary is not treated as a list of words.
- Instead, every lesson helps learners discover patterns, recognise systems, and gradually build an intuitive understanding of the language.

#### Vision
We aim to create the world's most coherent monolingual learning ecosystem for adult and teenage learners. Every COSYlanguages resource should feel immediately familiar, regardless of the language being studied. A learner who opens an English manual today and an Italian manual five years later should instantly recognise the same teaching philosophy while also discovering what makes each language unique.

### 2. Who We Teach & What We Are NOT

#### Our Learners
COSYlanguages is designed primarily for **adults** and **teenagers** learning a foreign language. The materials assume:
- Curiosity
- Independence
- Analytical thinking

They **never** assume previous linguistic knowledge.

#### What COSYlanguages is NOT
We are not:
- ❌ **Phrasebooks** (No random survival phrases without systemic understanding)
- ❌ **Traditional School Textbooks** (No rigid institutional classroom progression with translation crutches)
- ❌ **Grammar Encyclopedias** (No dense, academic 600-page rule listings with zero practical motivation)
- ❌ **Translation Courses** (No word-for-word L1-to-L2 matching)

Instead, **we build language operating systems**. Students learn **how the language thinks**, not simply how to pass a test.

---

### 3. The COSYlanguages Method: 7 Core Principles + 1

1. **Learn the language, not the translation.**
   - Everything remains monolingual: pictures, patterns, examples, questions, and observation.
   - Instead of `Russian = English`, learners experience `Russian = Russian`.
2. **Grammar is a pattern.**
   - Never memorize. Always discover.
   - Every page asks 👀 *"What do you notice?"* before presenting formal explanations. Adults enjoy solving puzzles.
3. **Every language thinks differently.**
   - Frame grammar around native logic.
   - *English*: Word order & helper verbs.
   - *Russian*: Information organization through case endings.
   - *Greek*: Noun agreement & family belonging.
   - *French*: Pronunciation rhythm & sound-to-spelling agreement.
4. **Grammar exists to communicate.**
   - Grammar is never isolated. Every page explicitly answers: *What can I communicate after learning this?*
5. **Visual before verbal.**
   - Replace 300 words of explanation with 1 clear structural diagram or timeline. Show first; explain second.
6. **Confidence before perfection.**
   - Teach: `Pattern ➔ Practice ➔ Exception`. Never overwhelm with 5 edge cases on the first exposure.
7. **Build a language network.**
   - Every page connects backward to previous topics and forward to future topics, vocabulary, speaking clubs, and practice engines. Students build a connected city, not collect isolated bricks.
8. **Monolingual teaching creates independent learners.**
   - Context, visuals, emojis, and organic patterns replace L1 translation support.

---

### 4. The COSYlanguages Formula & Key Tests

#### The 5-Question Formula
Every learning unit, page, or exercise must answer:
1. **WHAT?** — What is this concept?
2. **WHY?** — Why does it exist in the language?
3. **HOW?** — How do I construct it?
4. **WHEN?** — When do I use it in real life?
5. **WHAT NEXT?** — Where does this connect next?

#### The 30-Second Test (Learner Rule)
If a learner opens any page for 30 seconds, they must immediately know:
- What this page teaches
- Why it matters
- What the main visual pattern is
- Where to look first

If they have to search for the main point, the page is incomplete.

#### The Monolingual Teacher Test (Teacher Rule)
Can a teacher teach this page without translating a single word into the student's native language?
- **YES** ➔ Page succeeds.
- **NO** ➔ Page requires better visual progression, clearer examples, or simpler contextual scaffolding.

### 5. Editorial Motto

> **Understand. Recognize. Communicate.**

- **Understand** the language as a logical system.
- **Recognize** its recurring structural patterns.
- **Communicate** with confidence in authentic real-world situations.

## PART II — Editorial & Visual Standards

### 1. Tone of Voice & Writing Style

- **Warm, Non-Institutional & Encouraging**: Replace cold academic terminology (*student, classroom, homework, curriculum, grade*) with warm, empowering language (*learner, space, practice, learning path, progress*). Preserve formal certification titles (e.g. DELF, TOEFL).
- **No Em Dashes (`—`)**: Replace em dashes site-wide with colons (`:`), commas (`,`), periods (`.`), or middle dots (`·` for level badges like `A1 · Beginner`). *Note: Russian grammatical dashes in predicate constructions and song lyric interruptions are preserved.*
- **Direct & Action-Oriented**: Write short, clear sentences. Use bullet points and visual callouts rather than continuous walls of text.

### 2. Visual Architecture & Semantic Role Palette

COSYlanguages uses a standardized semantic color-coding palette across all grammar manuals, diagrams, sentence builders, and syntax tables:

| Semantic Role | CSS Variable | Color Code | Purpose / Visual Meaning |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Subject / Topic** | `--role-subject` | `#3562e0` (Blue) | Sentence focus, subjects, agent nouns |
| **Verb / Action** | `--role-verb` | `#1c8f56` (Green) | Action verbs, auxiliary verbs, predicates |
| **Object / Target** | `--role-object` | `#c9740a` (Orange) | Direct/indirect objects, recipient nouns |
| **Affixes / Endings** | `--role-ending` | `#7c4fd6` (Purple) | Declension endings, conjugation suffixes |
| **Adjectives / Describers** | `--role-adjective` | `#d99b00` (Yellow) | Modifiers, descriptive attributes |
| **Mistakes / Errors** | `--role-error` | `#a3195b` (Red) | Pitfalls, incorrect forms in `.mflip` cards |

### 3. Typography & Information Density

- **Clear Hierarchy**: Use distinct font sizes and weights for titles (`h1`), section hubs (`h2`), concept blocks (`.box`, `.card`), and sub-tags (`.sec-tag`).
- **Touch Targets**: Minimum 44px–48px tap target height for all interactive buttons, flip cards, tab controls, and filter pills to ensure mobile ergonomics.
- **Scannable Layout**: Maximize white space; keep line length between 60–75 characters for optimal readability across desktop and mobile screens.

## PART III — Curriculum Framework

### 1. CEFR Level Calibrations (A0–C2)

- **A0 (Absolute Beginner)**: Recognition of basic sound-to-letter correspondences, core subject pronouns, essential present tense forms, and immediate concrete survival vocabulary.
- **A1 (Breakthrough)**: Basic sentence building using word order patterns, article gender agreement, simple present/past tenses, and high-frequency thematic domains (family, routines, food).
- **A2 (Waystage)**: Expanded tenses (imperfect vs. compound past), basic modality, prepositions of time/space, and active participation in everyday transactions.
- **B1 (Threshold)**: Expressing opinions, describing emotions, hypothetical situations (conditional), and discussing abstract topics in Speaking & Cinema Clubs.
- **B2 (Vantage)**: Complex syntax, nuanced aspect, nuanced discussion of socio-cultural themes, subtle debate, and idiom/collocation integration.
- **C1–C2 (Mastery)**: Stylistic agility, regional register awareness, advanced rhetoric, literature, and implicit cultural nuances.

## PART IV — Grammar Manual Standards

### 1. Anatomy of a Standard Grammar Page

Every topic page across all monolingual grammar manuals must feature the standard **14-point visual layout**:

1. **Top Bar Navigation**: Back link to native language hub (`← Hub`), breadcrumb parts, and brand mark.
2. **Hero Header**: Native topic title, CEFR level badge, and concise topic subtitle.
3. **Observation Box (👀 "What do you notice?")**: Monolingual introductory visual pattern or puzzle before rule explanation.
4. **Communicative Outcome Banner**: Explicit statement of what the learner can say/do after mastering this page.
5. **Structural Formula / Blueprint**: Color-coded CSS role blocks (`--role-subject`, `--role-verb`, `--role-ending`).
6. **Native Thinking Box ("How this language thinks")**: Conceptual shift explanation avoiding L1 translation reliance.
7. **Visual Diagram / Timeline**: Graphical representation of tense, space, or agreement mechanics.
8. **Pattern Examples Grid**: Clean target language examples with highlighted morphosyntactic features.
9. **Interactive Sentence Builder**: Live clickable word-block assembler validating correct syntax order.
10. **Common Mistakes Flip Cards (`.mflip`)**: Interactive click-to-reveal cards contrasting common learner errors with correct native forms.
11. **Memory Trick Callout**: Visual mnemonic or structural formula hint.
12. **LocalStorage Skill Checklist (`cosy-check-*`)**: Interactive self-evaluation checklist persisting progress locally.
13. **Diagnostic Quiz Panel (`.quiz-panel`)**: Interactive 3–5 question quiz with instant diagnostic explanation.
14. **Topic Pager Nav**: Bidirectional navigation links (`← Previous Topic` | `Next Topic →`).

## PART V — Vocabulary Manual Standards

### 1. Structural Separation: Grammar vs. Vocabulary

- **Grammar Manuals** teach *mechanics*: syntax, declensions, conjugations, prepositions, and structural rules.
- **Vocabulary Manuals** teach *lexical fields*: thematic word groups, contextual usage, collocation patterns, flashcards, and search tools.
- *Rule*: Grammar manuals do not contain exhaustive word lists; Vocabulary manuals do not teach structural verb conjugations or case declension paradigms.

### 2. Word Selection, Metadata & Lexical Networks

- **Gender & Article Integrity**:
  - Languages with articles (e.g. French, Spanish): Include explicit articles (`le`, `la`, `el`, `la`) or explicit gender tags `(m.)` / `(f.)` for contracted forms (`l'`).
  - Languages without articles (e.g. Russian): Mark gender explicitly with parenthetical indicators `(м.)`, `(ж.)`, `(ср.)`.
- **Interactive Components**:
  - **Flip Flashcards (`.flashcard`)**: Front shows term + gender badge; back shows definition + example sentence.
  - **Real-time Search (`#vocab-search`)**: Client-side filtering across terms and categories.

## PART VI — Speaking Manual & Club Standards

### 1. Discussion Prompt Architecture

- **Lower-case Highlighted Vocabulary**: `<strong>` highlighted vocabulary terms inside discussion rounds must be lowercased unless sentence-initial, proper nouns, or Grammar Focus titles.
- **Concrete Parenthetical Anchors**: Abstract or complex discussion questions must append concrete, relatable examples in parenthetical format: `(e.g. ...)` to facilitate immediate conversation entry.
- **Strict CEFR Alignment**: Discussion rounds in Stage 4 must strictly respect target level ceilings (A2, B1, B2, C1).

### 2. Multimedia Integration

- **Cinema & Karaoke Integration**: Sync discussion rounds with exact scene timestamps or song lyrics.
- **No Em Dashes**: Ensure all prompt texts, theme summaries, and teacher notes replace em dashes with colons or middle dots.

---

## PART VII — Exercise & Practice Standards

### 1. Task Taxonomy & Progression

- **Types**: Sentence builders, fill-in-the-blank, error correction flip cards, flashcards, and audio speech waveform tasks (`cosyDrawTTSWaveform`).
- **Recycling**: Every unit reintroduces 20% of previously learned vocabulary/grammar items alongside 80% new material to reinforce long-term memory retention.

---

## PART VIII — Teacher Standards

### 1. Monolingual Facilitation

- **Zero Translation Rule**: Classrooms and online sessions operate 100% in the target language. Teachers use body language, visual cards, sentence builders, and contextual rephrasing instead of L1 translation.
- **Scaffolding**: Provide structured sentence starters (`In my opinion...`, `Because...`) on session cards so learners never feel stuck.

## PART X — Quality Assurance & Rubrics

### 1. 8-Point Pre-Publishing Rubric

Before any new manual topic page, vocabulary module, or speaking club session is published, it must pass all 8 checks:

1. ✅ **Monolingual Instruction**: Is 100% of the content written in the target language without translation fallback?
2. ✅ **CEFR Level Validation**: Is the grammatical and lexical depth strictly calibrated to the target level?
3. ✅ **One Main Concept**: Does the page focus on a single core pattern without overwhelming exceptions?
4. ✅ **At Least One Visual Diagram**: Is there a CSS role-colored diagram, timeline, or visual formula?
5. ✅ **Interactive Common Mistakes**: Are there `.mflip` click-to-reveal mistake cards with target-language error analysis?
6. ✅ **Native Thinking Callout**: Does the page explain how the target language thinks?
7. ✅ **Network Cross-References**: Are there bidirectional links to previous/next topics and language hub pages?
8. ✅ **Communicative Outcome Stated**: Does the page clearly state what the learner can say or build?

### 2. Technical & Link Integrity Checks

- Run `python3 scripts/verify_manual_links.py` to confirm 100% resolution across all manual hubs and topic pages.
- Ensure all relative script/asset depth paths (`assets/style.css`, `assets/app.js`, `../manuals/...`) match their exact directory nesting level.
