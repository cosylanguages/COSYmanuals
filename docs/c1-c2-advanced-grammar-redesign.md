# Advanced English Grammar Curriculum Redesign Specification (C1/C2)

> **Document Status**: Active Specification Blueprint
> **Scope**: Advanced Level (C1/C2) English Grammar Redesign
> **Target Audience**: Curriculum Developers, Content Authors, and Platform Engineers

---

## 🎯 Executive Overview & Pedagogical Directives

At the C1/C2 Advanced CEFR level, grammar instruction must transcend mechanical structure and verb table drills. Advanced learners require mastery over **rhetorical control, stance-taking, information packaging, syntactic compression, and diplomatic nuance**.

### 1. Key Pedagogical Principles
* **Outcome-Oriented Titles**: Lessons are named after their communicative purpose and rhetorical effect (e.g., *"The Spotlight Effect: Cleft Sentences for Emphasis & Focus"* rather than *"Cleft Sentences (L45)"*).
* **High-Stakes Modern Contexts**: Contexts move beyond basic everyday scenarios to modern professional, academic, and media domains: AI ethics, tech disruption, executive communications, crisis PR, debate rebuttals, policy design, investigative journalism, and podcast commentary.
* **CEFR Calibration & Elevation**:
  * Basic topics (like standard future forms or basic modals) are elevated from A2/B1/B2 to focus on **predictive stance, epistemic commitment, statutory obligation, and narrative future-in-the-past**.
  * Related syntax topics are unified into strategic thematic modules.

## 📚 Master Redesign Index (32 Lessons)

### 🔹 Module 1: Stance, Nuance & Strategic Framing

#### 1. Continuous aspect (L1, L2)
* **New Title**: *Dynamic Trends & Polite Distancing: Advanced Continuous Framing*
* **Modern Context**: Tech startup trend reports & diplomatic client negotiation.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Moving beyond temporary states to express emotional tone, polite hedging (*"I was wondering if..."*), backgrounding, and irritating habits with *always/constantly* (*"Algorithms are constantly evolving..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *pivot, disrupt, fluctuate, perpetually, incrementally*.

#### 2. Perfect aspect (L3)
* **New Title**: *Retrospective Strategy & Future Projections: Mastering Perfect Stance*
* **Modern Context**: Quarterly earnings post-mortems & multi-year project forecasting.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Framing historical precedents (*"Having established..."*) and future-perfect contingencies (*"By the time regulations pass, the tech will have transformed..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *culminate, yield, materialize, supersede, by the time*.

#### 3. Future tenses (L5)
* **CEFR Elevation Note**: *Basic future forms belong in B1/B2.* Elevated to C1/C2 through **predictive stance & narrative future-in-the-past**.
* **New Title**: *Strategic Forecasting & Narrative Destiny: Advanced Future Trajectories*
* **Modern Context**: Strategic consultancy reports & biography/retrospective documentaries.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Distinguishing degrees of commitment and handling future-in-the-past (*"The product was to launch in Q3, but..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *projected to, slated for, destined to, bound for, inadvertently*.

#### 4. Other ways to express future (L6)
* **New Title**: *On the Brink of Disruption: Expressing Imminence & High-Probability Futures*
* **Modern Context**: Breaking geopolitical news & stock market alerts.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Mastering high-level phrases (*be on the verge/brink of, be set/due/bound to, be poised to*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *poised to, on the precipice of, tipped to, set to, bound to*.

#### 5. Passive for distancing (L7)
* **New Title**: *Crisis Management & Institutional Objectivity: Passive Distancing & Impersonal Framing*
* **Modern Context**: Corporate PR statements, crisis control, academic journal articles.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using passives to avoid blame (*"Mistakes were made"*), establish institutional consensus (*"It is widely accepted that..."*), and maintain scientific objectivity.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *it is alleged that, deemed, attributed to, construed as, reportedly*.

#### 6. Modals (L15)
* **CEFR Elevation Note**: *Basic permission/obligation is A2/B1.* Redesigned around **diplomatic negotiation & statutory duty**.
* **New Title**: *Diplomatic Persuasion & Statutory Duty: High-Level Modal Nuances*
* **Modern Context**: International contract negotiations & policy drafting.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Expressing soft pressure (*"You might well consider..."*), formal obligation (*"The licensee shall uphold..."*), and tentative suggestions (*"Dare we suggest..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *shall, dare, might well, ought to have, needlessly*.

#### 7. Modals of deduction (L16)
* **New Title**: *Forensic Analysis & Deductive Speculation: Modals of Past Speculation*
* **Modern Context**: Investigative journalism podcasts & cyber-security breach analysis.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Synthesizing evidence to make graded deductions about past events (*must have been compromised, couldn't possibly have known, might conceivably have occurred*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *incontestably, conceivably, plausibly, undeniably, corrupt*.

#### 8. Certainty, willingness and habit (L17)
* **New Title**: *Behavioral Patterns & Systemic Tendencies: Expressing Inclination & Resistance*
* **Modern Context**: Behavioral psychology, consumer trends & tech system friction.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using *will/would* for characteristic habit or stubborn refusal (*"The legacy code simply won't compile"*), and *prone/inclined/apt to*.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *prone to, inclined to, apt to, persistent, obstinate*.

#### 9. Adverbs (L23)
* **New Title**: *Editorial Stance & Nuance Framing: Viewpoint & Comment Adverbs*
* **Modern Context**: Op-eds, media critiques, podcast commentaries.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Structuring editorial voice using sentence adverbs (*arguably, presumably, ironically, regrettably, undeniably*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *arguably, ostensibly, perceptibly, conceivably, characteristically*.

### 🔹 Module 2: Hypotheticals & Counterfactual Reasoning

#### 10. Conditional sentences (L11)
* **CEFR Elevation Note**: *Standard 1st/2nd/3rd conditionals are B1/B2.* Pivoted to **mixed conditionals & formal inversion**.
* **New Title**: *Scenario Planning & Counterfactual Strategy: Advanced & Mixed Conditionals*
* **Modern Context**: Risk assessment in venture capital & climate scenario modeling.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Combining timeline references (*If we had invested then, we would be market leaders today*) and formal conditional inversion (*Should you require, Had we known*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *had it not been for, failing that, inadvertently, contingent upon*.

#### 11. Other unreal past expressions (L12)
* **New Title**: *Post-Mortems & Unfulfilled Ambitions: Unreal Past & Subjunctive Expressiveness*
* **Modern Context**: Executive board post-mortems & personal memoirs.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using *wish, if only, would rather, it's high time, as if/though* for expressive hypothetical nuance.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *it's high time, would rather, as though, regretful, imperative*.

#### 12. Reduced conditionals
* **New Title**: *Executive Precision: Concise Conditions & Legal Provisos*
* **Modern Context**: Legal contracts, terms of service, API documentation, policy briefs.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Streamlining conditions using reduced clauses (*If applicable, Unless otherwise stated, Provided that, Given, Where necessary*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *provided that, failing which, subject to, barring, where applicable*.

### 🔹 Module 3: Information Packaging & Rhetorical Focus

#### 13. Inversion and fronting (L43)
* **New Title**: *Rhetorical Impact & Keynote Delivery: Inversion & Stylistic Fronting*
* **Modern Context**: TED talks, keynote addresses, high-impact advertising campaigns.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Reordering clauses to create dramatic tension or seamless narrative flow (*"Rarely has an innovation...", "On the horizon lies..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *seldom, rarely, under no circumstances, paramount, pivotal*.

#### 14. Inversion with ‘only’, ‘neither’ and ‘so’ (44)
* **New Title**: *Debate Rebuttals & High-Impact Emphasis: Limiting & Negative Inversion*
* **Modern Context**: Formal parliamentary debate, court cross-examination, strategic rebuttals.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using negative/limiting focus structures (*"Not only did they overlook..., but...", "Only after rigorous testing can we..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *not only, only when, neither, so profound was, scarcely*.

#### 15. Cleft sentences (45)
* **New Title**: *The Spotlight Effect: Cleft Sentences for Emphasis & Information Focus*
* **Modern Context**: Product launch keynotes & clarifying misunderstandings in high-profile interviews.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using *It-clefts* (*It was the culture, not the budget, that failed*) and *Wh-clefts* (*What drives us is innovation*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *what matters most is, it is precisely because, all that remains, crucial factor*.

#### 16. Relative sentences (L47)
* **New Title**: *Syntactic Richness: Sentential Relative Clauses & Formal Preposition Placement*
* **Modern Context**: Long-form investigative journalism & formal legal opinion pieces.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using sentential relatives (*"...which caused widespread concern"*) and formal preposition fronting (*"the framework within which we operate"*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *whereby, in which case, to whom, whereof, amidst which*.

#### 17. Reduced relative clauses (L48)
* **New Title**: *Executive Density: Concise Information Packaging via Reduced Relatives*
* **Modern Context**: Executive summaries, financial briefs, scientific abstracts.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Converting full relative clauses into sleek participle modifiers (*The agreement signed yesterday vs. The agreement which was signed...*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *pertaining to, stemming from, outlined in, governing, under consideration*.

#### 18. Nominal relative clauses (L49)
* **New Title**: *Abstract Concepts & Negotiation Flexibility: Nominal Relative Clauses*
* **Modern Context**: High-stakes mediation, executive policy formation, philosophical debate.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using free relative clauses (*what, whatever, whoever, whichever, where*) to construct noun phrases representing abstract ideas.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *whatever it takes, whichever path, what concerns us, whoever is designated*.

### 🔹 Module 4: Syntactic Compression & Structural Density

#### 19. The infinitive (L33)
* **New Title**: *Complex Goals & Retrospective Evaluation: Perfect & Passive Infinitives*
* **Modern Context**: Project retrospectives, strategic goal setting, audit reviews.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Mastering split infinitives, continuous infinitives (*to be monitoring*), and perfect infinitives (*to have achieved*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *aimed at, presumed to have, bound to be, eager to*.

#### 20. Complex subject & object (L35)
* **New Title**: *Market Rumors & Intelligence Briefs: Complex Subject & Object Structures*
* **Modern Context**: Financial market intelligence, political commentary, peer-reviewed research summaries.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using structures like *is thought/said/alleged to be* and *expect X to be Y*.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *purported to be, considered to have, reported to be, estimated at*.

#### 21. The gerund (L37)
* **New Title**: *Formal Grievances & Conceptual Framing: Complex & Possessive Gerunds*
* **Modern Context**: Board meeting minutes, formal legal disputes, corporate policy manuals.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Using possessive gerunds (*his resigning was unexpected*) and passive gerunds (*objected to being excluded*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *notwithstanding, prior to, resentment at, justification for*.

#### 22. Gerund vs Infinitive (L38)
* **CEFR Elevation Note**: *Basic verb lists (like like/enjoy) are A2/B1.* Redesigned for C1/C2 around **subtle cognitive & stylistic shifts**.
* **New Title**: *Psychological Precision: Nuanced Shifts in Gerund vs Infinitive Choice*
* **Modern Context**: Executive leadership coaching, psychological analysis, subtle contract interpretation.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Contrast subtle semantic shifts (*regret to inform vs. regret informing*, *mean doing vs. mean to do*, *propose doing vs. propose to do*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *contemplate, dread, envision, propose, regret*.

#### 23. Participle clauses (L53)
* **New Title**: *Fluid Narrative & Concise Pacing: Present, Past & Perfect Participle Clauses*
* **Modern Context**: Documentary scripts, narrative podcasts, fast-paced case studies.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Replacing conjunctions with participle clauses for temporal, causal, and conditional flow (*"Having completed the audit, the team..."*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *having evaluated, given that, burdened by, driven by*.

#### 24. Subjects in participles (L54)
* **New Title**: *Stylistic Polish: Absolute Participle Clauses & Dangling Modifier Pitfalls*
* **Modern Context**: Publishing copy-editing, legal resolutions, policy drafting.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Mastering absolute participle clauses with independent subjects (*"Weather permitting...", "The budget having been approved..."*) while correcting dangling participles.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *all things considered, conditions permitting, pending approval*.

#### 25. Participle phrases (L55)
* **New Title**: *Dense Description & Market Trends: Post-Modifying Participle Structures*
* **Modern Context**: Financial market analysis, tech trend breakdown, brand positioning.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Pre-modifying and post-modifying complex nouns with participle phrases (*a rapidly expanding market, issues surrounding governance*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *burgeoning, declining, encompassing, surrounding, underlying*.

### 🔹 Module 5: Discourse Cohesion & Narrative Economy

#### 26. Clauses of reason and result (L21)
* **New Title**: *Root-Cause Dynamics & Strategic Impact: Explanatory & Consequential Architecture*
* **Modern Context**: Root-cause analysis, investigative journalism, scientific argument building.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Moving beyond *because/so* to high-level connectors (*owing to, by virtue of, thereby, as a consequence of, such that*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *by virtue of, thereby, in consequence of, stems from, triggers*.

#### 27. Purpose and concession clauses (L22)
* **New Title**: *Strategic Trade-offs & Counter-Argumentation: Concession & Purpose Alignment*
* **Modern Context**: Political debates, executive summaries, balancing competing priorities.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Expressing nuance and concession (*albeit, notwithstanding, much as, so as not to, lest*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *notwithstanding, albeit, much as I agree, lest we forget, in an effort to*.

#### 28. Reporting verbs (L25)
* **New Title**: *Journalistic Rigor & Academic Attribution: Nuanced Reporting Verbs*
* **Modern Context**: Academic research reviews, news reporting, investigative blogging.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Categorizing verbs by speaker stance (*concede, refute, assert, maintain, allege, intimate, imply*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *concede, refute, contend, postulate, intimate, allege*.

#### 29. Linking words and phrases (L26)
* **New Title**: *Architectural Cohesion: Advanced Discourse Markers & Logical Connectors*
* **Modern Context**: Public speaking, keynote essays, formal debates.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Sequencing, contrasting, and summarizing smoothly (*conversely, by the same token, incidentally, furthermore, in light of the above*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *conversely, by the same token, notwithstanding, paradoxically*.

#### 30. Subject-verb agreement (L57)
* **New Title**: *Editorial Perfection: Complex Concord & Subject-Verb Agreement in High-Stakes Texts*
* **Modern Context**: Legal drafting, publishing copy-editing, formal governance.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Tricky agreement with collective nouns, quantifiers, compound subjects, proximity rules (*Neither the CEO nor the directors were...*).
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *majority, spectrum, fraction, neither...nor, along with*.

#### 31. Ellipsis (L58)
* **New Title**: *Conversational Economy & Rhetorical Compression: Strategic Ellipsis*
* **Modern Context**: High-speed business discussions, podcast dialogue, script writing, literary economy.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Omitting redundant words natural to C1/C2 speech and writing without losing syntax validity.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *if so, if not, where possible, when needed*.

#### 32. Substitution (L59)
* **New Title**: *Discourse Cohesion: Mastering Substitution Devices for Elegant Prose*
* **Modern Context**: Academic essay writing, speechwriting, editorial articles.
* **Pragmatic Shift**: Avoiding awkward repetition using *do so, so/not, one/ones, such, the former/the latter*.
* **Target Lexical Pairings**: *do so, in doing so, the former... the latter, such is*.
