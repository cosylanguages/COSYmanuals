DAY 13 / 14 · GRAMMAR INTENSIVE

Gerunds vs Infinitives (Stop doing vs Stop to do)

Master verb patterns with -ing vs. to-infinitive, prepositions, and critical meaning changes.

Stage 1 · Elicitation

Compare these two sentences about driving:

A: "He stopped smoking."
B: "He stopped to smoke."

Look closely at the meaning: In sentence A, he quit the habit of smoking completely. In sentence B, he paused another action (like driving) in order to smoke a cigarette!

Stage 2 · The Rule

Core Rule: Verb Pattern Categories & Meaning Shifts

Gerund Only (V-ing)

Always use gerunds after prepositions and specific verbs: enjoy, avoid, mind, consider, suggest, finish, admit, deny, practice, look forward to.

  • Example: I enjoy reading books.
  • Example: He is good at swimming.

Infinitive Only (To + V1)

Use infinitives after future-oriented verbs: want, decide, hope, promise, refuse, offer, agree, plan, expect, afford, manage.

  • Example: I decided to buy a car.
  • Example: We hope to see you soon.

⚡ Meaning Shifts (Stop / Remember / Forget / Try)

  • Remember/Forget doing: Look back at past memory. (I remember visiting Paris in 2010.)
  • Remember/Forget to do: Duty/task to perform. (Remember to lock the door!)
  • Try doing: Experiment with a method. (Try drinking tea for your cough.)
  • Try to do: Attempt a difficult task. (I tried to lift the heavy box.)
💡 Memory Trick: Preposition? → ALWAYS -ING! Future decision/wish? → TO + V1! Memory/Past experience? → -ING! Task to do? → TO + V1!
Stage 3 · Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: "I forgot to call my boss." Did I call my boss?
    Answer: No, I didn't perform the task.
  2. Question: "I will never forget visiting Japan." Did I visit Japan?
    Answer: Yes, and I have a clear memory of that past experience.
  3. Question: Why do we say "I look forward to meeting you" instead of "to meet you"?
    Answer: Because "to" in "look forward to" is a preposition, so it must be followed by a gerund (`V-ing`).
  4. Question: Which structure follows "decide"?
    Answer: Infinitive (`to + V1`) — e.g. "She decided to leave."
Stage 4 · Guided & Free Practice

Complete the Sentences (Gerund V-ing vs Infinitive to + V1)

1. She suggested _____ (go) to the cinema on Friday evening.
2. We cannot afford _____ (buy) a new house right now.
3. Are you interested in _____ (learn) foreign languages?
4. Please remember _____ (turn off) the lights before you leave.
5. I regret _____ (inform) you that your application was unsuccessful.
🔍 Click to Reveal Answer Key & Explanations
  1. going — Verb "suggest" takes a gerund (`V-ing`).
  2. to buy — Verb "afford" takes a `to-infinitive`.
  3. learning — Preposition "in" requires a gerund (`V-ing`).
  4. to turn off — Duty/task to perform in the future requires `to-infinitive`.
  5. to inform — Formal statement of bad news requires `to-infinitive` ("regret to inform/say").
