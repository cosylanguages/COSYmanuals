Passive Voice vs Active Voice & Get-Passive

Master focusing on the receiver of the action vs. the doer/agent, formal passive, and informal get-passive.

Stage 1 · Elicitation

Read these two news headlines:

Active: "Someone stole a famous painting from the museum last night."
Passive: "A famous painting was stolen from the museum last night."

Why use the passive? Because the stolen painting is the main topic of interest, and the exact thief ("someone") is either unknown, obvious, or unimportant!

Stage 2 · The Rule

Core Rule: BE + V3 (Past Participle)

Standard Passive (BE + V3)

The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.

  • Present Simple: Coffee is grown in Colombia.
  • Past Simple: The letter was sent yesterday.
  • Agent: Use by + doer only if necessary (e.g. by Shakespeare).

Informal Get-Passive (GET + V3)

Used in spoken English for accidental, unexpected, or sudden events.

  • Example: He got injured playing football.
  • Example: Watch out or you will get fired!
💡 Memory Trick: Active = Doer does Action to Receiver. Passive = Receiver gets Action done to them (BE + V3)!
Stage 3 · Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: In "The bridge was built in 1890," do we know or care who the individual construction workers were?
    Answer: No, the focus is on the bridge itself and its construction date.
  2. Question: What is the passive form of "They are repairing the road"?
    Answer: "The road is being repaired" (Present Continuous Passive: is/are + being + V3).
  3. Question: When do we include "by [agent]" in a passive sentence?
    Answer: Only when the doer is important or specific information (e.g., "Hamlet was written by Shakespeare").
  4. Question: Is "My phone got stolen" formal or informal?
    Answer: Informal spoken English (using the Get-passive).
Stage 4 · Guided & Free Practice

Convert the Active sentences into Passive Voice

1. Active: Millions of people read this newspaper every day.
Passive: This newspaper _____ by millions of people every day.
2. Active: The architect designed the new city hall.
Passive: The new city hall _____ by the architect.
3. Active: They will announce the winner tomorrow.
Passive: The winner _____ tomorrow.
4. Active: Someone has stolen my bicycle!
Passive: My bicycle _____!
5. Active: A dog bit him during his morning run. (Use Get-passive)
Passive: He _____ during his morning run.
🔍 Click to Reveal Answer Key & Explanations
  1. is read — Present Simple passive (`is + V3`).
  2. was designed — Past Simple passive (`was + V3`).
  3. will be announced — Future Simple passive (`will be + V3`).
  4. has been stolen — Present Perfect passive (`has been + V3`).
  5. got bitten — Informal Get-passive (`got + V3`).
