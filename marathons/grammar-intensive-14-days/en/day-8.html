DAY 8 / 14 · GRAMMAR INTENSIVE

Modals of Obligation & Permission (Must vs Have To vs Should)

Master internal personal feeling vs. external law/rule obligation vs. recommended advice.

Stage 1 · Elicitation

Compare these three statements about work:

1. "I must call my mother tonight." (Personal feeling)
2. "I have to wear a uniform at work." (Company rule)
3. "You should take an aspirin for your headache." (Good advice)

Notice the source of authority: Statement 1 comes from inside the speaker. Statement 2 comes from an outside authority/rule. Statement 3 is a friendly recommendation!

Stage 2 · The Rule

Core Rule: Authority Source & Negative Shifts

Must vs Have To (Positive Obligation)

Must + V1: Internal obligation felt by the speaker (personal duty).

Have to + V1: External obligation imposed by laws, rules, or circumstances.

Should + V1: Advice or moral recommendation (not mandatory).

Mustn't vs Don't Have To (CRITICAL SHIFT!)

Mustn't (Must not): PROHIBITION — It is forbidden / illegal!

Don't / Doesn't have to: NO OBLIGATION — You can if you want, but it's not necessary.

💡 Critical Warning: "Mustn't" means DO NOT DO IT (forbidden!). "Don't have to" means OPTIONAL (no necessity)!
Stage 3 · Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: "You mustn't park here." Is it allowed if I pay money?
    Answer: No! It is strictly prohibited and illegal.
  2. Question: "You don't have to pay for the museum today." Is it forbidden to pay, or is it free?
    Answer: It is free! There is no obligation to pay.
  3. Question: What is the past tense of "must" and "have to"?
    Answer: Both become had to in the past! ("Yesterday I had to work late.")
  4. Question: "You should see a doctor." Is the speaker forcing you, or giving advice?
    Answer: Giving friendly recommendation/advice.
Stage 4 · Guided & Free Practice

Complete the Sentences (must / have to / should / mustn't / don't have to)

1. You _____ smoke in the hospital. It is strictly against the law.
2. Tomorrow is Sunday! I _____ wake up early.
3. In the UK, drivers _____ drive on the left side of the road.
4. You look very exhausted. I think you _____ go to bed early tonight.
5. I _____ remember to buy a birthday card for my sister before I go home.
🔍 Click to Reveal Answer Key & Explanations
  1. mustn't — Strict prohibition / illegal act.
  2. don't have to — Lack of necessity (optional on Sunday).
  3. have to — External law / national regulation.
  4. should — Friendly advice / recommendation based on appearance.
  5. must — Internal personal resolution felt strongly by the speaker.
