DAY 3 / 14 · GRAMMAR INTENSIVE

Past Simple vs Present Perfect

Master finished action in a finished time vs. past actions connected to the present/life experience.

Stage 1 · Elicitation

Consider these two statements:

A: "I lost my keys yesterday."
B: "I have lost my keys!"

Which sentence implies you are still looking for your keys right now? In sentence A, the time (yesterday) is finished. In sentence B, the result is active right now (I cannot open my door!).

Stage 2 · The Rule

Core Rule: Finished Time Box vs. Open Door to Present

Past Simple (V2)

Finished action in a specific, finished time period. The connection to now is broken.

  • Finished Time Words: yesterday, last year, in 2019, 3 days ago, when I was young.
  • Example: I visited Rome in 2019. (Specific finished date)

Present Perfect (have/has + V3)

Life experiences (time unmentioned), unfinished time periods (today, this week), or past actions with present result.

  • Open Time Words: ever, never, already, yet, so far, recently, since, for.
  • Example: I have visited Rome three times. (In my life so far)
💡 Memory Trick: Specific past time word (yesterday, ago, in 2020)? → MUST use Past Simple. Unfinished time or life experience? → Present Perfect keeps the door open!
Stage 3 · Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: Is "I have lived in Spain for 5 years" finished or ongoing?
    Answer: Ongoing! You moved there 5 years ago and you still live in Spain today.
  2. Question: Is "I lived in Spain for 5 years" finished or ongoing?
    Answer: Finished! You do not live in Spain anymore.
  3. Question: Can we say "I have seen him yesterday"?
    Answer: No! "Yesterday" is a finished past time word, so you must say "I saw him yesterday."
  4. Question: "She has broken her leg." Can she walk normally right now?
    Answer: No, because Present Perfect emphasizes the current present result (her leg is currently broken/in a cast).
Stage 4 · Guided & Free Practice

Complete the Sentences (Past Simple vs Present Perfect)

1. I _____ (never / see) a live opera in my life.
2. Shakespeare _____ (write) over 30 plays in his lifetime.
3. Ouch! I _____ (just / cut) my finger! It's bleeding.
4. They _____ (finish) the report two hours ago.
5. How long _____ (you / know) your best friend?
🔍 Click to Reveal Answer Key & Explanations
  1. have never seen — Life experience with "never".
  2. wrote — Shakespeare's life is finished; completed historical past.
  3. have just cut — Action just completed with immediate present result ("It's bleeding").
  4. finished — Specific finished time marker ("two hours ago").
  5. have you known — Unfinished state continuing up to the present.
