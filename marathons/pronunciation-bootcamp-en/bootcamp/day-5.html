Stage 1 🎯 Warmer

Read these three past-tense verbs out loud:

« WALKED »   |   « PLAYED »   |   « WANTED »

Notice how -ED sounds like /t/ in walked, /d/ in played, and adds a full new syllable /ɪd/ in wanted! Why?

Stage 2 🤔 Elicitation

Touch your throat while pronouncing the final sound before -ED:

  • walk [k] → voiceless (no throat vibration) → -ED becomes /t/ (walked)
  • play [ay] → voiced (throat vibrates) → -ED becomes /d/ (played)
  • want [t] or need [d] → ends in T or D → -ED adds extra syllable /ɪd/ (wanted)

Voicing Rule: Your voice box determines whether -ED is pronounced as /t/, /d/, or /ɪd/!

Stage 3 📖 Rule Explanation

1. The 3 Rules of Past Tense -ED

Base Verb Ends With-ED SoundExamplesMechanics
Voiceless Sound (/p, k, f, s, ʃ, tʃ/) /t/ walked [wɔːkt], kissed [kɪst], washed [wɒʃt] Air only; no throat vibration.
Voiced Sound (Vowels, /b, g, v, z, m, n, l, r/) /d/ played [pleɪd], called [kɔːld], cleaned [kliːnd] Vocal cords vibrate continuously.
/t/ or /d/ sound /ɪd/ wanted [ˈwɒntɪd], needed [ˈniːdɪd] Adds a full extra syllable!

2. The 3 Rules of Plural & 3rd Person -S / -ES

Ending Sound-S SoundExamplesLogic
Voiceless (/p, k, f, t/)/s/cats [kæts], books [bʊks]Matches voiceless ending.
Voiced (Vowels, /b, d, g, v, m, n, l, r/)/z/dogs [dɒgz], moms [mɒmz], bees [biːz]Matches voiced ending.
Sibilant (/s, z, ʃ, tʃ, dʒ/)/ɪz/ (-es)buses [ˈbʌsɪz], churches [ˈtʃɜːrtʃɪz]Adds extra syllable /ɪz/.

3. The 1-1-1 Consonant Doubling Rule

Rule: If a word has 1 Syllable + 1 Vowel + 1 Final Consonant, DOUBLE the consonant before adding a vowel suffix (-ing, -ed, -er).

  • stop + ing → stopping (protects short o vowel!)
  • run + er → runner (protects short u vowel!)
  • Compare: diner (one n = long i) vs. dinner (double n = short i)!
💡 Y to I Rule: If a word ends in Consonant + Y (happy), change Y to I before adding suffixes (happily, happiness) EXCEPT before -ing (study → studying)!
Stage 4 ❓ Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: How is -ED pronounced in "stopped"?
    Answer: /t/ (stopped [stɒpt]), because 'p' is a voiceless consonant.
  2. Question: Why does "hoping" have 1 'p' while "hopping" has 2 'p's?
    Answer: Hopping comes from 1-1-1 word 'hop' (short o); hoping comes from 'hope' (Magic E long o).
  3. Question: Is -s in "dogs" pronounced /s/ or /z/?
    Answer: /z/ sound (dogs [dɒgz]), because 'g' is a voiced sound.

Stage 7 🔑 Day 5 Takeaway

Golden Rule of Day 5:

-ED ends in /t/ (voiceless), /d/ (voiced), or /ɪd/ (after T/D). Double final consonants to lock in short vowels (hopping vs. hoping)!
