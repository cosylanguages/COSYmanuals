Day 7 — Connected Speech, Homographs & Master Text

Combine all 7 days of pronunciation, phonics, and spelling rules into native connected speech and continuous reading fluency.

Stage 1 🎯 Warmer

Read this phrase as spoken in natural native English:

« FAR AWAY » → /fɑːr əˈweɪ/   |   « WATER » → /ˈwɑːdər/ (Flap T)

Native speakers do not pronounce words in isolation; words blend together smoothly using Linking R, Intrusive R, T-flapping, and H-dropping!

Stage 2 🤔 Elicitation

Observe Homographs (Same Spelling, Different Sound & Meaning):

  • bow: /boʊ/ (ribbon tied on a gift) vs. /baʊ/ (bend down to audience)
  • lead: /liːd/ (to guide a team) vs. /lɛd/ (heavy metal element)
  • tear: /tɪr/ (water from crying) vs. /tɛr/ (rip paper apart)
  • wind: /wɪnd/ (blowing air) vs. /waɪnd/ (twist a clock)

Context Principle: Word meaning and sentence context determine which pronunciation homographs take!

Stage 3 📖 Rule Explanation

1. Connected Speech Phenomena

PhenomenonRuleExample & IPA Realization
Linking R Connect final silent 'r' to initial vowel of next word "far away" → /fɑːr əˈweɪ/
Intrusive R Insert /r/ between two vowels at word boundary "law and order" → /lɔːr ən ˈɔːrdər/
T-Flapping (American) T/TT between vowels becomes quick tap /d/ "water" → /ˈwɑːdər/, "little" → /ˈlɪdəl/
H-Dropping (Informal) Drop initial 'h' in unstressed pronouns/function words "tell him" → /tɛl ɪm/

Stage 4 ❓ Concept Check Questions (CCQs)
  1. Question: How is "water" pronounced in American connected speech?
    Answer: /ˈwɑːdər/ (T-flapping turns double T/intervocalic T into quick D tap).
  2. Question: What determines whether "lead" is /liːd/ or /lɛd/?
    Answer: Context! Verb "lead a group" = /liːd/; metal "lead pipe" = /lɛd/.
  3. Question: What happens to "tell him" in natural rapid speech?
    Answer: H-dropping turns it into /tɛl ɪm/!

Stage 6 📖 Master Continuous Reading Text

Read this continuous passage out loud, applying all rules learned across the 7 days:

On a bright autumn[1] day[2], a famous[3] knight[4] walked[5] to the city[6] center. He wanted[7] to preSENT[8] a PREsent[9] to the president[10]. While stopping[11] by a lake[12], he saw a girl[13] with a tear[14] in her eye holding a bow[15]. "We must lead[16] with care," he said[17], linking[18] his hands for a[19] moment.
Phonetic Rule Breakdown:
[1] autumn: Silent N (-mn). [2] day: AY vowel team at word end. [3] famous: Unstressed -ous schwa /əs/. [4] knight: Silent K (kn-) and silent GH (igh = /aɪ/). [5] walked: Past -ed = /t/ after voiceless /k/. [6] city: Soft C before 'i' (/s/). [7] wanted: Past -ed = /ɪd/ after 't'. [8] preSENT: Verb 2nd syllable stress. [9] PREsent: Noun 1st syllable stress. [10] president: Unstressed 'e' reduces to schwa. [11] stopping:1-1-1 consonant doubling. [12] lake: Magic E long 'a'. [13] girl: Bossy R IR = /ɜːr/. [14] tear: /tɪr/ (cry water). [15] bow: /boʊ/ (ribbon). [16] lead: /liːd/ (to guide). [17] said: High-frequency exception /sɛd/. [18] linking: NG nasal /ŋ/. [19] for a: Linking R /fər ə/.
