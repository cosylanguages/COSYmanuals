Unit 1 — The Schwa [ə] and Weak Forms

14-Field Fiche — Lesson 1.1: Master of English Rhythm — Schwa [ə]

Definition
The schwa [ə] is the most frequent vowel sound in spoken English, representing an unarticulated neutral vowel in unstressed syllables.
Rule
Any unstressed vowel letter (A, E, I, O, U) can reduce to schwa [ə] in multisyllabic words or weak function words.
Why?
English is stress-timed: stressed syllables take longer, forcing unstressed vowels to shorten to [ə].
How to recognize?
Locate the primary stress mark; all surrounding non-primary syllables are prime candidates for schwa.
Common examples
a-bout /əˈbaʊt/, ba-na-na /bəˈnɑːnə/, doc-tor /ˈdɒktə/, pho-to-graph /ˈfəʊtəɡrɑːf/
Counter-examples
Full vowels in stressed syllables: photo /ˈfəʊtəʊ/ (first 'o' is full).
Exceptions
Some unstressed syllables reduce to /ɪ/ (e.g. music /ˈmjuːzɪk/) rather than /ə/.
Common mistakes
Pronouncing every written vowel with full phonetic weight (spelling pronunciation).
Mnemonic Hint
Relax your mouth! Schwa is the lazy vowel sound of spoken English.
Key vocabulary
schwa, weak form, stressed syllable, unstressed syllable, stress-timed
