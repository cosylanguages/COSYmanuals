Unit 1 — The Great Vowel Shift (1400–1700)

14-Field Fiche — Lesson 1.1: Historical Vowel Transformation

Definition
The Great Vowel Shift was a massive sound change in Middle English where long vowels raised their tongue position or diphthongized.
Rule
Long /iː/ became /aɪ/ (bite); long /uː/ became /aʊ/ (house); long /eː/ raised to /iː/ (meet). Spelling froze before the shift completed.
Why?
Standardization of printing press spellings by William Caxton froze 14th-century spellings while spoken vowels shifted.
How to recognize?
Discrepancies between European continental vowel values (Continental A, E, I) and English long vowel sounds.
Common examples
bite /baɪt/ (Middle English /biːt/), house /haʊs/ (Middle English /huːs/)
Counter-examples
Short vowels that were unaffected by raising (e.g. bit, cat, dog).
Exceptions
Words shortened before early modern period (e.g. blood, flood /blʌd/).
Common mistakes
Assuming English vowel letters match Latin or Romance phonetic values.
Mnemonic Hint
Spelling is 1400 Middle English; pronunciation is Modern English!
Key vocabulary
Great Vowel Shift, Middle English, diphthongization, William Caxton, orthographic freeze
