Unit 3 — Soft C and Soft G Phonetic Decision Tree

14-Field Fiche — Lesson 3.1: Soft C (/s/) and Soft G (/dʒ/)

Definition
C and G soften their sounds from hard (/k/, /ɡ/) to soft (/s/, /dʒ/) when followed by front vowels E, I, or Y.
Rule
C + E/I/Y = /s/; G + E/I/Y = /dʒ/. Preceded by A/O/U, they remain hard /k/ and /ɡ/.
Why?
Palatalization in Vulgar Latin and Old French modified consonant articulation before front vowels.
How to recognize?
Examine the immediate vowel letter following C or G.
Common examples
city, cent, cycle, gem, giant, gym, page, place
Counter-examples
cat, cold, cup, go, game, gun (hard sounds before A, O, U).
Exceptions
Germanic origin words keeping hard G: get, give, girl, gift, tiger.
Common mistakes
Pronouncing soft C as /k/ in words like 'circle' or soft G as /ɡ/ in 'giant'.
Mnemonic Hint
E, I, Y soften C and G like ice in tea!
Key vocabulary
soft C, soft G, hard C, hard G, palatalization, front vowels
