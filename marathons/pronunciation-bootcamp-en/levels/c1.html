Unit 1 — The Three Layers of English Orthography

14-Field Fiche — Lesson 1.1: Germanic vs French vs Greek Phonetic Strata

Definition
English vocabulary is divided into Germanic (Old English), Romance (Norman French/Latin), and Greek layers, each following distinct sound-spelling rules.
Rule
CH = /tʃ/ in Germanic (chest), /ʃ/ in French loanwords (chef), /k/ in Greek loanwords (choir, chemist).
Why?
Historical borrowing retained source language spelling patterns across historical eras.
How to recognize?
Academic terms are often Greek/Latin; informal daily terms are Germanic.
Common examples
chef /ʃɛf/, Machine /məˈʃiːn/, Echo /ˈɛkəʊ/, Architecture /ˈɑːkɪtɛktʃə/
Counter-examples
Hybrid compounds or anglicized words that altered original sound values.
Exceptions
False friends and irregular spellings from Renaissance re-latinization.
Common mistakes
Applying English /tʃ/ to French loans like 'chivalry' or Greek terms like 'character'.
Mnemonic Hint
Chef is French (/ʃ/), Choir is Greek (/k/), Chair is Germanic (/tʃ/)!
