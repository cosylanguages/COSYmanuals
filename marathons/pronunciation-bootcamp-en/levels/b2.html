Unit 4 — Connected Speech Mechanisms

14-Field Fiche — Lesson 4.1: Linking, Catenation & Elision

Definition
Connected speech refers to the phonetic modifications (linking, intrusive sounds, elision) that occur across word boundaries in fluent speech.
Rule
Consonant-to-vowel linking shifts final consonants onto initial vowels of subsequent words (catenation).
Why?
Human articulation optimizes muscular effort to maintain steady rhythm.
How to recognize?
Word boundary ending in consonant followed immediately by word starting with vowel.
Common examples
pick it up ➔ /pɪ.kɪ.tʌp/, check it out ➔ /tʃɛ.kɪ.taʊt/, turn off ➔ /tɜː.nɒf/
Counter-examples
Formal speeches where glottal stops create artificial pauses between words.
Exceptions
Intrusive /r/ in British English (e.g. media /r/ event).
Common mistakes
Inserting choppy pauses between words in continuous sentences.
Mnemonic Hint
Push the final consonant into the next word!
Key vocabulary
catenation, linking, intrusive R, elision, word boundary
Frequency
★★★★★ (Fluent default)

Key Takeaway
Linking words together transforms staccato pronunciation into natural flow.
