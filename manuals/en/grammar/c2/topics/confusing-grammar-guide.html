Fine-grained semantic distinctions, register shifts, archaic/legal connectors, and native-level syntactic control

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these two sentences: "The board made an economic decision." (Related to economy/finance) vs. "The board made an economical decision." (Cost-effective / money-saving). One letter changes the financial implication entirely!

💡 Check your understanding (CCQs)

1. Subordinator check: "The policy was enacted, notwithstanding public protest." What is the logical relationship between the policy enacted and public protest?
Correct · 'Notwithstanding' functions as a formal concessive preposition/prepositional subordinator meaning 'despite' or 'in spite of'.
2. Nuance check: Is a "historic agreement" an agreement that took place in history, or an agreement of major historical significance?
Correct · 'Historic' means famous or momentous in history, whereas 'historical' simply means pertaining to history or the past.

🗣️ Stylistic & Register Note

At C2 level, avoiding register dissonance is paramount. Combining high-register statutory subordinators like whereby or notwithstanding with casual informal vocabulary creates jarring stylistic incoherence. Maintain register integrity across the sentence.
