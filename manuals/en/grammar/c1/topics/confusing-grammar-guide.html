👀 What do you notice?

Compare these two academic statements:

  • "This drug causes side effects." (Categorical / unhedged → risks overgeneralization)
  • "The evidence suggests that this drug may potentially give rise to adverse side effects under specific conditions." (Nuanced academic hedging → precise stance)

🧠 Logic Map: Core C1–C2 Advanced Grammar Contrasts

Concept AConcept BCore Precision DifferenceKey Examples
Categorical Claim Hedged Academic Claim Absolute fact claim vs. Evidence-based probabilistic claim Results prove X. vs. The data strongly point to X.
Ellipsis Lexical Substitution Silent omission of recoverable words vs. Replacing words with 'do so', 'one', 'such' She ordered tea and he [ordered] coffee. vs. He wanted tea and I wanted the same.
Loose Sentence Periodic Sentence Main clause first, details added vs. Main thought held until the end for climax He failed the exam despite studying all night. vs. Despite studying all night, he failed.
Given Information (Theme) New Information (Rheme) Starting with known anchor context vs. Placing the key new message at the end As for climate change, recent models indicate rapid sea level shifts.
⚠️ Common C1–C2 Precision Pitfalls
❌This study proves that all subjects behave identically.

✅This study indicates that subjects tend to exhibit similar behavioral patterns.
(Academic prose prefers hedged verbs like 'indicate' or 'tend to' over absolute 'proves')
❌He promised to submit the paper and he submitted the paper.
tap to see the correct form
✅He promised to submit the paper and did so promptly.
(Use 'did so' substitution to avoid awkward repetition)
