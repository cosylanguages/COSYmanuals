Nominalisation & Register

Turn a clause into a noun when the text has to sound formal.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can compress a clause into a noun phrase and keep the sentence readable.
Examples in context
  • When we automate the line, costs fall. The automation of the line reduces costs. — Clause becomes subject.
  • They refused to publish the data. Their refusal to publish the data delayed the audit. — Agent moves into a possessive.
  • Complaints rose sharply. There was a sharp rise in complaints. — Manner becomes an adjective.

👀 What do you observe?

Nominalisation turns a verb or adjective into the head of a noun phrase: implement → the implementation of, refuse → their refusal to, rise → a sharp rise in. The original subject either becomes a possessive (their refusal) or disappears (the implementation of the policy), and the original verb's object becomes an of-phrase.

Formula / rule: Nominalise when the action, not the actor, is the topic: the implementation of the policy took two years. Keep the agent in a possessive when responsibility matters: their refusal delayed the audit. Limit yourself to one nominalisation per clause, and keep a verb in the sentence so it still moves.

★ Why it matters

Formal English compresses: a report says the introduction of the levy, not when the government introduced the levy. Compression raises the register but hides who did what, which is why the passive nominal is the favourite structure of anyone avoiding responsibility - and why an editor should ask for the agent back.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Nominalisations are heavy in speech and sound bureaucratic. Native speakers use them in writing and in prepared statements, and they lighten them in conversation: 'we introduced the levy' rather than 'the introduction of the levy'.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Nominalised forms shift the stress: implement /ˈɪm.plɪ.ment/ but implementation /ˌɪm.plɪ.menˈteɪ.ʃən/, with the main stress on the syllable before -tion.

Additional example bank
  • Compressed: The withdrawal of the offer ended the negotiation.
  • Agent kept: Their withdrawal of the offer ended the negotiation.
  • Presented: There was a marked decline in applications after the change.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The implement of the policy took two years (verb used as a noun)

✅ Right: The implementation of the policy took two years

❌ Wrong: The policy was implemented and it took two years and it was expensive (chain of clauses)

✅ Right: The implementation of the policy took two years and cost more than planned
