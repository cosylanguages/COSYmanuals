Cleft Sentences and Emphasis

Put the important element in its own clause.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use it-clefts and what-clefts to make one part of a sentence carry the emphasis.
Examples in context
  • It was the board that rejected the plan. The board rejected the plan. — It-cleft: highlights the board.
  • What the board rejected was the plan. The board rejected the plan. — What-cleft: highlights the plan.
  • It was in June that the policy changed. The policy changed in June. — Highlights a time.

👀 What do you observe?

A cleft splits one sentence into two clauses so that the highlighted element sits in its own slot. The it-cleft opens with it was and places the focus after it: it was the delay that cost us the contract. The what-cleft opens with what and places the focus at the end: what cost us the contract was the delay. Both keep the original meaning and change only the emphasis.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
it-cleft focus after it was it was the delay that cost us the contract
what-cleft focus at the end what cost us the contract was the delay
negative cleft focus on what is not the cause it is not the cost that worries me
all-cleft focus on the only thing all I asked for was a decision
there-cleft presents an entity there was a delay that cost us the contract
cleft with a time or place highlights an adverbial it was in June that the policy changed

Formula / rule: Pick the element the reader must remember, then put it in the focus slot: after it was for the it-cleft, at the end for the what-cleft. Watch the agreement in the what-cleft, where what is singular even when the complement is plural.

★ Why it matters

Clefts are how English marks contrast without raising the voice in writing. A report that says it was the approval process that delayed the launch has already told you where to look for the fix, which a plain sentence does not.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, the cleft is carried by stress rather than by syntax: a speaker may simply stress the delay. Clefts become frequent in speech when correcting an assumption: it wasn't the cost, it was the timing.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The cleft marker carries the stress if the correction is the point: it WAS the delay; what we NEED is an owner.

Additional example bank
  • Correction: It wasn't the price that lost the deal, it was the delivery date.
  • Narrowing: All the board asked for was a timetable.
  • Focus on time: It was in March that the first warning appeared.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: What we need are a clear owner (agreement with a plural complement)

✅ Right: What we need is a clear owner

❌ Wrong: It was the delay what cost us the contract (what after an it-cleft)

✅ Right: It was the delay that cost us the contract
