Level B2 Grammar

Mixed Conditionals

Match the condition's time to the result's time.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can talk about a past condition with a present result, or a present condition with a past result.
Examples in context
  • If I had studied medicine I would be a doctor now. — Past condition, present result.
  • If she were not so stubborn she would have accepted the offer. — Present condition, past result.
  • If they had invested in 2019 they would own the market today. — Same form, different decades.

👀 What do you observe?

A mixed conditional joins two time frames. Past condition with present result: if + past perfect, would + base verb (If I had taken the job, I would be in Berlin now). Present condition with past result: if + past simple, would have + participle (If she were more careful, the error would not have happened). The condition and the result do not have to share a time.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
past condition + present result if + past perfect, would + base if I had taken it, I would be there now
present condition + past result if + past simple, would have + participle if she were careful, it would not have happened
past condition + past result third conditional if I had known, I would have called
present condition + present result second conditional if I knew, I would tell you
unreal past trait were for all persons if he were here
inverted form Had / Were / Should + subject had I taken the job, I would be in Berlin

Formula / rule: Choose the tense of each half by the time it refers to, not by the other half. If the condition is past and the result is now: if + past perfect, would + base. If the condition is now and the result is past: if + past simple, would have + participle. Never put would in the if-clause.

★ Why it matters

Mixed conditionals are how English argues about consequences that outlive their causes. A policy decision in 2019 explains a present shortage, and the third conditional alone cannot say that. Getting the mix right is the difference between a history essay and an analysis.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers mix conditionals freely in speech and rarely notice the time shift; what they notice is would in the if-clause, which marks a learner. In writing, the mixed form signals that the writer has thought about which half belongs to which period.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The stress falls on the time marker, which is what makes the mix audible: I would BE there NOW; it would not HAVE happened.

Additional example bank
  • Past to present: If we had kept the factory, the town would still have work.
  • Present to past: If he were better organised, the deadline would have been met.
  • Inverted: Had the warning been heeded, we would not be in this position.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: If I would have taken the job, I would be in Berlin (would in the if-clause)

✅ Right: If I had taken the job, I would be in Berlin

❌ Wrong: If she was more careful, the error would not have happened (indicative for an unreal present)

✅ Right: If she were more careful, the error would not have happened
