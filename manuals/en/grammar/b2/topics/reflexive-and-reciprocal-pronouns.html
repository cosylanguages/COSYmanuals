Reflexive and Reciprocal Pronouns

Use myself, themselves and each other where they belong.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use reflexive pronouns for the same subject and reciprocal pronouns for two-way actions.
Examples in context
  • She taught herself to code. Reflexive: the subject acts on itself. — Subject and object are the same.
  • They blamed each other. Reciprocal: two parties act on one another. — Each other for two, one another for more.
  • The board itself approved the change. Emphatic reflexive. — Adds emphasis, not an object.

👀 What do you observe?

A reflexive pronoun is used when the subject and the object are the same person or thing: she taught herself, the board itself approved the change. A reciprocal pronoun is used when two or more parties act on one another: they blamed each other, we consult one another. Reflexives are also used after certain verbs (behave yourself, help yourself) and in fixed phrases (by itself, in itself), but not after verbs that do not take an object.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
reflexive object subject acts on itself she taught herself to code
emphatic reflexive emphasis on the subject the board itself approved the change
reciprocal each other two parties, two-way action they blamed each other
reciprocal one another more than two, formal we consult one another
by + reflexive alone or unaided the system rebooted by itself
in + reflexive considered alone the delay is not a problem in itself

Formula / rule: Ask whether there is one party or two. One party acting on itself takes a reflexive: she taught herself. Two parties acting on each other take each other or one another. Use a reflexive for emphasis only when the subject is already present, and never use one where the verb has no object (she woke up, not she woke herself up).

★ Why it matters

The distinction matters in reports, where themselves suggests internal action and each other suggests mutual action: the departments blamed themselves is a very different finding from the departments blamed each other. Reflexives also carry responsibility, which is why a minute that says the board itself approved it names the body rather than the staff.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use each other for two and one another for more than two, but the distinction is soft and often ignored. Reflexives after verbs of movement are wrong: she sat down, not she sat herself down.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The reflexive ending is weak: /məˈself/, /jɔːˈself/, and the stress falls on self when the emphasis is the point: the board itSELF approved it.

Additional example bank
  • Reflexive: He hurt himself lifting the crate.
  • Reciprocal: The two teams briefed each other before the meeting.
  • Fixed phrase: The decision is not a problem in itself.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They blamed themselves for the delay with two teams (reflexive where mutuality is meant)

✅ Right: They blamed each other for the delay

❌ Wrong: She woke herself up at six every day (reflexive with a verb that takes no object)

✅ Right: She woke up at six every day
