Distancing Passive Reporting

Report a claim without naming who made it.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can report an allegation, a belief or a rumour while keeping the source out of the sentence.
Examples in context
  • It is reported that the talks collapsed. The talks are reported to have collapsed. — Source removed, claim kept.
  • It is believed that she resigned. She is believed to have resigned. — Perfect infinitive for a past event.
  • Officials think the delay will grow. The delay is expected to grow. — Expectation reported, not predicted.

👀 What do you observe?

Two shapes carry the same claim. The impersonal shape opens with it is plus a reporting verb and a that-clause: it is said that he left. The personal shape moves the subject forward and uses the infinitive: he is said to have left. For a past event the infinitive is perfect (to have left); for a simultaneous state it is simple (he is said to be abroad).

Element / structure Role in English Application example
It is said that + clause impersonal report it is said that the plan failed
X is said to + infinitive report about a present state the plan is said to be unworkable
X is said to have + participle report about the past the plan is said to have failed
It is alleged / reported / understood claim without commitment it is alleged that funds were moved
X is believed / thought / expected to belief or expectation the talks are expected to resume
X is understood to quiet official confirmation the board is understood to have voted

Formula / rule: Use the impersonal form when the claim itself is the topic: It is alleged that funds were moved. Use the personal form when the person is the topic and the claim is background: He is alleged to have moved the funds. The reporting verb decides the strength: alleged signals dispute, understood signals a briefing, reported signals the press.

★ Why it matters

This is the structure of institutional caution. A ministry that says the contract is understood to have been awarded reports what it has heard without committing to it, and a newspaper that writes it is alleged avoids being sued. Reading these verbs tells you how much the writer trusts the claim, which is often the most important information in the sentence.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers treat these as fixed collocations and rarely invent new ones: is said to, is believed to, is understood to, is expected to, is alleged to. Is thought to is slightly more tentative than is believed to, and is reported to suggests a source the writer has not checked.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The weak forms carry the structure: /ɪt ɪz əˈledʒd/ with the stress on the reporting verb, and in the personal form the stress falls on the infinitive: he is said to have LEFT.

Additional example bank
  • Impersonal: It is understood that the deal was signed in June.
  • Personal: The deal is understood to have been signed in June.
  • Expected: The results are expected to be published on Friday.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: It is said that he left yesterday, he is said to leave yesterday (present infinitive for a past event)

✅ Right: He is said to have left yesterday

❌ Wrong: It is believed that she is resigning, she is believed resigning (no infinitive after the reporting verb)

✅ Right: She is believed to be resigning
