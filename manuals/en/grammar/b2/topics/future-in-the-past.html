Future in the Past

Describe a future that was still ahead at a past moment.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use would, was going to and was about to to report past intentions and predictions.
Examples in context
  • They would announce the result at noon. Future from a past viewpoint. — Would + base.
  • We were going to publish in March. Intention, often unfulfilled. — Was going to + base.
  • She was about to sign. Immediate future, interrupted. — Was about to + base.

👀 What do you observe?

Future in the past reports a future that was still ahead when the story is set. Would plus the base verb is the neutral form in reported speech and narrative. Was going to marks an intention, and often implies that it was not carried out. Was about to marks the immediate future, usually interrupted. Was to marks an arrangement or a plan in formal narrative.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
would + base neutral future from the past he said he would call
was / were going to + base intention, often unfulfilled we were going to launch in March
was / were about to + base immediate future, interrupted she was about to sign
was / were to + base formal arrangement or destiny the board was to meet the next morning
was / were due to + base scheduled future the report was due to appear in June
past perfect of be going to earlier intention in reported speech he had been going to resign

Formula / rule: Anchor the story in the past, then choose the shade of future: would for neutral reporting, was going to for intention, was about to for the next moment, was to for a formal plan. In reported speech the future shifts back one step, which is why will becomes would.

★ Why it matters

This structure is how a report explains that a plan existed before the outcome was known, and it is the only way to write a sequence without breaking the past tense. It also carries judgement: was going to signals that the intention lapsed, which is often the point of the sentence.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use was going to constantly in speech, and often reduce it to was gonna. Would in reported speech is neutral, and was to is reserved for written narrative and obituaries.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Would reduces to /d/ after a pronoun (he'd call), and was going to reduces to was gonna, which is normal in speech and absent from formal writing.

Additional example bank
  • Reported: They said the review would start in May.
  • Unfulfilled: We were going to expand, but the funding was withdrawn.
  • Interrupted: He was about to speak when the chair closed the meeting.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: He said he will call on Friday (future not shifted in reported speech)

✅ Right: He said he would call on Friday

❌ Wrong: She was about signing when the call came (missing to after about)

✅ Right: She was about to sign when the call came
