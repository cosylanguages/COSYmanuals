Level B2 Grammar

Clauses of Contrast, Purpose, Reason and Result

Link two ideas with the relation the argument needs.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can signal contrast, purpose, cause and consequence with the right connector.
Examples in context
  • Contrast: Even though the data was thin, the board approved the pilot. — Concession.
  • Purpose: They doubled the checks in order to catch the errors earlier. — Intention.
  • Reason: Since the audit failed, the launch was postponed. — Cause.
  • Result: Sales fell so sharply that the target was abandoned. — Consequence.

👀 What do you observe?

Four relations, each with its own connectors. Contrast: although, even though, though, while, whereas, despite + noun or -ing. Purpose: so that + clause, in order to / so as to + infinitive, for + noun. Reason: because, since, as, given that. Result: so ... that, such ... that, consequently, as a result. The relation decides both the connector and the grammar that follows it.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
although / even though concession with a clause although the costs rose, the project continued
despite / in spite of concession with a noun or -ing despite the rising costs
while / whereas direct contrast between two facts while exports grew, domestic sales fell
so that / in order to purpose so that the launch could go ahead
because / since / as reason because the supplier failed an audit
so ... that / such ... that result so fast that the system stalled

Formula / rule: Pick the relation first, then the connector, then the grammar: concession with a clause needs although; concession with a noun needs despite; purpose with a subject of its own needs so that; purpose with the same subject needs in order to. Reason and result look similar but answer different questions: because answers why, so ... that answers what came of it.

★ Why it matters

These connectors are the joints of an argument, and a wrong joint is the most common reason a B2 text reads as a list rather than a case. The register matters too: since and as are more formal than because, and thereby or consequently belong in writing, not in speech.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers front concession (Although the costs rose) and postpone result (so fast that the system stalled). Fronted concession signals a balanced view; a result clause at the end lands the consequence where the reader expects the conclusion.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Although is stressed on the second syllable /ɔːlˈðəʊ/; the clause after it carries a fall-rise, and the main clause falls, which is how a listener hears where the concession ends.

Additional example bank
  • Concession with a noun: Despite the warning, the launch went ahead.
  • Purpose with a change of subject: They added a review step so that errors would surface earlier.
  • Result: Such was the backlog that the system stalled.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Although the costs rose, but the project continued (double connector)

✅ Right: Although the costs rose, the project continued

❌ Wrong: Despite the costs rose (noun form after despite with a full clause)

✅ Right: Despite the rise in costs, the project continued
