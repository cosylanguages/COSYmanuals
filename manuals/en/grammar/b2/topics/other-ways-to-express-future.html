Other Ways to Express the Future

Use the semi-modals that carry plan, obligation and imminence.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use be to, be about to, be due to and be on the point of.
Examples in context
  • The panel is to report by June. Formal plan or instruction. — Be to + base.
  • The train is about to leave. Immediate future. — Be about to + base.
  • The audit is due to start in April. Scheduled future. — Be due to + base.

👀 What do you observe?

Beyond will and going to, English has semi-modals that carry a plan, a schedule or an imminent event. Be to plus the base verb states a formal plan or an instruction (the panel is to report by June). Be about to and be on the point of state what will happen in the next moment. Be due to states what is scheduled. Be bound to states what is certain.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
be to + base formal plan or instruction the panel is to report by June
be about to + base immediate future the train is about to leave
be on the point of + -ing immediate future, formal we are on the point of signing
be due to + base scheduled future the audit is due to start in April
be bound to + base certain future the cost is bound to rise
be set to + base expected future in news the firm is set to announce cuts

Formula / rule: Pick by the kind of certainty: a plan in a document is be to, a timetable is be due to, the next moment is be about to or be on the point of, and a logical certainty is be bound to. All of them take the base verb except be on the point of, which takes the gerund.

★ Why it matters

These semi-modals are what make a written plan read as a plan: is to report sounds like a decision, will report sounds like a prediction, and is due to report sounds like a timetable. In news English, is set to has become the standard way to report an expected event without committing to it.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, be about to is the common form and be on the point of sounds literary. Be to survives mostly in official notices and in the negative (you are not to leave), which is a prohibition.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The stress falls on the semi-modal when the timing is the point: the train is ABOUT to leave; the panel IS to report by June.

Additional example bank
  • Instruction: Staff are to submit timesheets by Friday.
  • Schedule: The hearing is due to resume on Monday.
  • Certainty: The delay is bound to affect the launch.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: We are on the point to sign (infinitive after on the point of)

✅ Right: We are on the point of signing

❌ Wrong: The panel is to reporting by June (gerund after be to)

✅ Right: The panel is to report by June
