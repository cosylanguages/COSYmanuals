Present Tenses Review

Choose between the simple, the continuous and the perfect present.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the four present tenses for habits, current states, temporary actions and experience.
Examples in context
  • He works in Leeds. Permanent situation. — Present simple.
  • He is working from home this week. Temporary situation. — Present continuous.
  • He has worked there since 2019. Experience up to now. — Present perfect.

👀 What do you observe?

Present simple for habits, routines and permanent states (she works in Leeds, the train leaves at seven). Present continuous for actions happening now or around now, and for temporary situations (she is working from home this week). Present perfect for experience or a situation continuing to now, with for and since. Present perfect continuous for an activity that has been going on up to now, usually with a visible result.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
present simple habit, routine, permanent state she works in Leeds
present continuous now, around now, temporary she is working from home this week
present perfect experience, situation up to now she has worked there since 2019
present perfect continuous activity up to now, visible result she has been working all morning
stative verbs no continuous form I know the answer, not I am knowing
timetables present simple for schedules the train leaves at 07:40

Formula / rule: Ask whether the situation is permanent, temporary, continuing from the past, or still in progress. Permanent goes to the simple, temporary to the continuous, continuing with for or since to the perfect, and in progress up to now to the perfect continuous. Keep stative verbs out of the continuous.

★ Why it matters

These four tenses cover most of what a report says about the present, and the choice between them is a claim about permanence: she works there says the arrangement is settled, she is working there says it may change. Getting the pair right is how a writer signals how much to trust a finding.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the present continuous for irritations with always (he is always losing the file) and the present simple for narrative commentary (then she walks in and says nothing). In formal writing, the perfect continuous is rarer than the perfect.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The contracted auxiliary in the perfect forms is weak: she's worked, she's been working, with the stress on the participle.

Additional example bank
  • Habit: The committee meets on the first Monday of the month.
  • Temporary: The team is covering two regions while the post is vacant.
  • Experience: We have tested the system in three countries.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: She is knowing the answer (continuous with a stative verb)

✅ Right: She knows the answer

❌ Wrong: I work here since 2019 (present simple with since)

✅ Right: I have worked here since 2019
