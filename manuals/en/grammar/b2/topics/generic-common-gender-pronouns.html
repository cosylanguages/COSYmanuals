Generic and Common-Gender Pronouns

Refer to people without guessing their gender.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use they, one and the singular they in formal and neutral writing.
Examples in context
  • Everyone submitted their form. Singular they. — Agreement with a singular antecedent.
  • One should check one's sources. Formal generic one. — Impersonal reference.
  • You should check your sources. Generic you. — Informal general reference.

👀 What do you observe?

English has three generic pronouns. They, with they, them and their, refers to a person of unknown or unspecified gender, and it is standard in modern usage even with a singular antecedent: everyone submitted their form. One, with one's, is impersonal and formal, and works best in rules. You is generic in speech and informal writing, where it means people in general.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
singular they unknown or unspecified gender if a candidate is late, they must inform the panel
their with everyone / anybody agreement with singular antecedent everyone submitted their form
one / one's formal impersonal one should check one's sources
generic you informal general reference you should check your sources
he or she avoided in modern usage use they unless the person is known
who for people relative reference the candidate who applied

Formula / rule: Choose by register and by knowledge: a known person takes he or she, an unknown person takes they, a rule takes one, and a conversation takes you. Keep the pronoun consistent through the paragraph, since switching from one to you in the same rule is a register error.

★ Why it matters

Singular they is the modern default in institutional writing precisely because it avoids both the clumsy he or she and the assumption that a role has a gender. A rulebook that uses one reads as formal and distant, which is sometimes the point and sometimes the problem.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use singular they constantly in speech without noticing, and generic you even in semi-formal writing. One is rare in speech outside Britain and can sound affected; in a report it is still normal.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Generic one is unstressed and often avoided in speech by using you or we instead; the singular they is always stressed when it is the subject of its clause.

Additional example bank
  • Singular they: Anyone who misses the deadline must explain themselves to the panel.
  • Formal one: One should not assume the figures are complete.
  • Generic you: You can't publish without a second reviewer.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: If a candidate is late, he must inform the panel (gendered pronoun for an unknown person)

✅ Right: If a candidate is late, they must inform the panel

❌ Wrong: One should check your sources (one and your mixed in one clause)

✅ Right: One should check one's sources
