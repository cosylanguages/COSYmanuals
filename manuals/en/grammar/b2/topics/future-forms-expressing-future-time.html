Future Forms

Pick the future form by what the speaker knows, not by the clock.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can choose between will, going to, the present continuous, the present simple and the future perfect.
Examples in context
  • Arrangement: I am seeing the minister on Tuesday. — Fixed in a diary.
  • Prediction from evidence: Costs are going to rise. — Visible now.
  • Decision made now: I will send it today. — Announced at the moment of speaking.
  • Deadline: By Friday we will have sent the file. — Completed before a future point.

👀 What do you observe?

The form follows the evidence. Present simple for timetables and programmes. Present continuous for arrangements already made with other people. Going to for intentions and for predictions with present evidence. Will for decisions taken at the moment of speaking, offers and promises. Will be + -ing for an activity in progress at a future time, and will have + participle for what is complete before a future point.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
present simple timetable, programme, schedule the train leaves at 07:40
present continuous arrangement with a time and a person we are meeting the board on Thursday
going to intention or evidence-based prediction it is going to rain
will decision now, offer, promise I will send it today
will be + -ing activity in progress at a future time at ten we will be presenting
will have + participle completed before a future point by June we will have finished

Formula / rule: Ask three questions: is the future fixed by a schedule, agreed with someone, or decided as I speak? Is my prediction based on evidence I can see, or on opinion? Will the action be in progress, or complete, at the reference time? Each answer picks a different form, and mixing them changes what the listener understands about how firm the plan is.

★ Why it matters

Getting future forms right is mostly about commitment. Are you going to do it means the decision is made; I will do it can announce a decision or offer help; I am doing it means someone else is expecting you. In professional English these three carry very different weight, which is why contracts are not written in will.

💭 Think like a native speaker

With go and come, native speakers prefer the present continuous for arrangements even in the far future (we are going to Lisbon in July), and the present simple for departure times. Going to is the most frequent form in speech for predictions about the visible world.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Will contracts to /əl/ or /l/ in fast speech (we'll, I'll), and going to reduces to gonna. The reduced form is normal in speech and wrong in writing except in dialogue.

Additional example bank
  • Schedule: The hearing starts at nine and ends at noon.
  • Arrangement: We are signing the contract on Friday.
  • Deadline: By the end of March we will have trained every team.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: We will meet the board on Thursday (will for an arrangement already made)

✅ Right: We are meeting the board on Thursday

❌ Wrong: By June we will finish the audit (will + base for a completed action before a deadline)

✅ Right: By June we will have finished the audit
