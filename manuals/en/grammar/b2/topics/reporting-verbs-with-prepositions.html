Reporting Verbs with Prepositions

Report what someone said with the preposition the verb demands.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can accuse, blame, congratulate and warn with the right pattern.
Examples in context
  • accuse somebody of doing something — accused the supplier of delaying
  • blame somebody for something — blamed the ministry for the delay
  • congratulate somebody on something — congratulated her on the result
  • warn somebody against doing something — warned us against signing

👀 What do you observe?

Each reporting verb owns its preposition, and the preposition decides what follows: of, for, on and against take a noun or an -ing form, while warn, remind, tell and persuade take an object plus to + infinitive. Blame allows both patterns with a change of focus: blame somebody for something, or blame something on somebody.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
accuse somebody of names the offence accused of leaking the memo
blame somebody for / blame on assigns responsibility blamed for the delay / blamed the delay on the ministry
congratulate somebody on praises a success congratulated her on the result
warn somebody against / not to advises caution warned against signing / warned not to sign
criticise somebody for finds fault criticised for the tone of the reply
thank somebody for acknowledges help thanked the team for the analysis

Formula / rule: Learn the verb and its preposition as one unit, then check what follows: an -ing form after of, for, on and against; a to-infinitive after warn, remind, persuade and tell. Do not translate the preposition from your own language, because the mapping is arbitrary: accuse of but blame for, congratulate on but praise for.

★ Why it matters

In press reporting these verbs do the arguing: accused of is stronger than said to have, and warned against commits the speaker to a recommendation. Choosing the verb is choosing how much responsibility the sentence assigns, which is why lawyers read reported speech so carefully.

💭 Think like a native speaker

With blame, native speakers prefer blame somebody for when a person is the topic and blame something on when a cause is the topic. Blame is not used with to + infinitive, and accuse is never followed by that + clause in formal writing.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Accuse of links across the consonant: /əˈkjuːz əv/, and the -ing form after a preposition keeps its full vowel, which makes the pattern audible in fast speech.

Additional example bank
  • Warn: The regulator warned against relying on a single supplier.
  • Blame: The report blames the delay on the approval process.
  • Thank: The chair thanked the auditors for their patience.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They accused the supplier to delay delivery (infinitive after accuse)

✅ Right: They accused the supplier of delaying delivery

❌ Wrong: Critics blamed the delay for the ministry (preposition reversed)

✅ Right: Critics blamed the ministry for the delay
