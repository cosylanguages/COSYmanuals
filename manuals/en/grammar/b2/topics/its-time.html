It's Time, Would Rather, Had Better

Give advice, express preference and set a deadline.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use it's time, would rather and had better with the right verb form.
Examples in context
  • It's time we left. Present or future meaning, past form. — Past subjunctive after it's time.
  • You'd better check the figures. Advice with a consequence. — Had better + base verb.
  • I'd rather you told her. Preference about someone else. — Would rather + subject + past form.

👀 What do you observe?

It's time (that) and it's high time take a past form with present meaning: it's time we left, meaning we should leave now. Had better takes the base verb and implies a consequence if the advice is ignored: you'd better check. Would rather takes the base verb when the subject is the same (I'd rather stay) and a past form when the subject changes (I'd rather you stayed).

Element / structure Role in English Application example
it's time + past form deadline, present meaning it's time we left
it's high time + past form stronger deadline, often critical it's high time the board acted
had better + base verb advice with a warning you'd better check the figures
would rather + base verb same subject I'd rather stay
would rather + subject + past different subject I'd rather you stayed
would rather + than comparison of options I'd rather wait than sign today

Formula / rule: Decide who is acting. Same subject with would rather: base verb. Different subject: past form. A deadline with it's time: past form. Advice with a consequence: had better plus the base verb, never to.

★ Why it matters

These three structures carry different pressures. It's time signals that something is overdue; had better signals that not doing it has a cost; would rather signals a preference the speaker is entitled to. Using them interchangeably makes advice sound either weak or rude.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use it's time we went and it's time to go with the same meaning, the first being slightly more personal. Had better is usually contracted to you'd better, and in speech it can sound like a threat.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Had better reduces to /ˈbetə/ with the auxiliary almost lost, and would rather to /aɪd ˈrɑːðə/, with the stress on rather.

Additional example bank
  • Deadline: It's high time the policy was reviewed.
  • Warning: You'd better tell the client before Friday.
  • Preference: I'd rather the team decided together.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: It's time we leave (base verb after it's time + subject)

✅ Right: It's time we left

❌ Wrong: You'd better to check the figures (to after had better)

✅ Right: You'd better check the figures
