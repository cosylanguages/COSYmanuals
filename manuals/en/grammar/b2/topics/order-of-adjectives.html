Order of Adjectives

Put several adjectives in the order English expects.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can order two to four adjectives before a noun without sounding foreign.
Examples in context
  • A beautiful old wooden desk opinion, age, material — Three adjectives in order.
  • A small round glass table size, shape, material — No opinion, still ordered.
  • A long grey silk scarf size, colour, material — Colour before material.

👀 What do you observe?

Adjectives fall into slots: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose. The order is fixed, and opinion always comes first even when it is the least important word. When two adjectives belong to the same slot, join them with and: a long and difficult year, not a long difficult year.

Formula / rule: Learn the slot order as a single string and place each adjective in its slot: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose. Two or three adjectives are normal; four is the practical limit, and a fifth means the sentence should be rewritten.

★ Why it matters

Native speakers hear the wrong order immediately, even when they cannot name the rule, which is why adjective order is one of the clearest markers of non-native writing. Getting it right costs nothing and removes an accent from the page.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, the last adjective before the noun carries the stress, and that is usually the material or origin: a lovely old STONE cottage. A fronted opinion adjective is often followed by a slight pause.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Each adjective keeps its own stress, but the strongest falls on the last slot: a beautiful old WOODEN desk, with the material as the most informative item.

Additional example bank
  • Opinion and age: an ugly new building
  • Size, colour, origin: a large blue Japanese print
  • Same slot: a quiet and careful report
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: A wooden beautiful old desk (material before opinion and age)

✅ Right: a beautiful old wooden desk

❌ Wrong: A French remarkable clear report (origin before opinion)

✅ Right: a remarkably clear French report
