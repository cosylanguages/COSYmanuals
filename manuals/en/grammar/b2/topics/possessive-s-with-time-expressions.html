Possessive 's with Time Expressions

Use the possessive for time, distance and value.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can write a week's notice, two hours' delay and today's figures.
Examples in context
  • a day's work singular time expression — One day, so 's.
  • three days' work plural ending in s — Three days, so s'.
  • today's figures fixed time word — Today's, yesterday's, tomorrow's.

👀 What do you observe?

Time expressions take the possessive like people do: a week's notice, an hour's delay, two hours' delay, three months' salary. The apostrophe follows the normal rule: 's for singular, s' for a plural ending in s, and 's for irregular plurals (children's). Fixed words such as today, yesterday and tomorrow always take 's. The same pattern covers distance (a mile's walk) and value (a pound's worth).

Element / structure Role in English Application example
singular + 's one unit of time a week's notice, an hour's delay
plural + s' more than one unit two hours' delay, three months' salary
irregular plural + 's children, people a children's programme
fixed time words today, yesterday, tomorrow today's figures
distance the same pattern a mile's walk
value the same pattern a pound's worth

Formula / rule: Decide whether the time expression is singular or plural, then place the apostrophe: 's for singular and irregular plurals, s' for plurals in s. Keep the possessive only when the time expression is the owner (a week's notice), not when it is a plain adverbial (we met last week).

★ Why it matters

These possessives are frequent in business English (a month's notice, two weeks' notice, a year's budget) and the apostrophe is where they go wrong. Because the phrase often sits in a contract, the error is expensive: a six months notice clause is ambiguous about where the apostrophe belongs.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers say a week's notice but write the possessive inconsistently in informal text; the apostrophe is one of the first casualties of casual writing. In speech the possessive is audible only as a small /s/ or /z/.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The possessive ending is /s/ after a voiceless sound (a week's), /z/ after a voiced one (a day's), and /ɪz/ after a sibilant (a month's).

Additional example bank
  • Singular: We require a month's notice.
  • Plural: The board gave two weeks' notice.
  • Fixed word: Yesterday's meeting was cancelled.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: two weeks notice (missing apostrophe)

✅ Right: two weeks' notice

❌ Wrong: a hours delay (singular article with a plural possessive)

✅ Right: an hour's delay
