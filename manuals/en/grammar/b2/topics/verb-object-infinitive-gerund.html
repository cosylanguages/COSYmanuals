Level B2 Grammar

Verb + Object + Infinitive or Gerund

Choose the pattern the main verb requires.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use tell, ask, persuade, allow and prevent with the right complement.
Examples in context
  • They asked us to leave. Verb + object + to-infinitive. — Ask, tell, persuade, advise, encourage.
  • They allowed us to stay. Verb + object + to-infinitive. — Allow, permit, enable.
  • They prevented us from staying. Verb + object + from + -ing. — Prevent, stop, discourage.

👀 What do you observe?

The main verb decides the complement. Persuade, advise, tell, ask, encourage, allow, permit and enable take an object plus to-infinitive. Prevent, stop, discourage and prohibit take an object plus from + -ing. Verbs of perception (see, hear, watch, notice) take an object plus a bare infinitive or an -ing form, with a difference of completeness: I saw them leave, I saw them leaving.

verb + object + to-infinitive persuade, tell, advise, allow they persuaded the board to delay
verb + object + from + -ing prevent, stop, discourage we prevented the error from spreading
verb + object + bare infinitive perception and let, make, help I saw them leave
verb + -ing verbs of liking and avoiding they avoided signing the deal
verb + to-infinitive agree, refuse, promise, manage they refused to sign
change of meaning remember, stop, try stop to check and stop checking

Formula / rule: Learn each verb with its pattern, then fill the slots in that order: verb, object, complement. Watch the change-of-meaning verbs, where the complement decides the sense: stop to check means pause in order to check, stop checking means no longer check.

★ Why it matters

These patterns carry the argument: preventing, allowing, persuading and discouraging are the four moves of negotiation, and each takes a different frame. A sentence that puts the wrong frame in place is understood, but it marks the speaker as translating from another language.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers prefer allow with to-infinitive (allow us to stay) and permit in formal contexts (permit them to enter); prevent from is so fixed that prevent to is never heard from a native speaker.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The to of the infinitive is weak /tə/ unless stressed for contrast, and from + -ing runs together: /prɪˈventɪd ðəm frəm ˈspredɪŋ/.

Additional example bank
  • Encourage: The chair encouraged the team to publish early.
  • Discourage: The cost discouraged firms from bidding.
  • Perception: We watched the auditors check every page.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They prevented the error to spread (infinitive after prevent)

✅ Right: They prevented the error from spreading

❌ Wrong: She advised us waiting (gerund after advise with an object)

✅ Right: She advised us to wait
