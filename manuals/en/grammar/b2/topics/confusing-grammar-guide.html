Level B2 Grammar

Confusing Grammar: Pairs that Look Alike

Separate the structures learners mix up most at B2.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can tell apart the pairs that produce the most persistent B2 errors.
Examples in context
  • used to work / am used to working Past habit versus present familiarity. — The -ing marks familiarity.
  • It's time we left / it's time to leave Same meaning, different subject frame. — Past form after we.
  • stop to check / stop checking Pause in order to check versus no longer check. — The complement decides.

👀 What do you observe?

The pairs that trouble B2 learners share a shape and differ in one point. Used to plus the base verb is a past habit; be used to plus the -ing form is present familiarity. It's time plus the past form looks at a shared deadline; it's time plus the infinitive states the action. Stop, remember and try change meaning with the complement. So do look forward to (verb, not preposition) and be used to.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
used to / be used to past habit versus familiarity used to work / am used to working
it's time we + past / it's time to + base deadline framing it's time we left / it's time to leave
stop to / stop -ing pause versus cease stop to check / stop checking
remember to / remember -ing duty versus memory remember to lock / remember locking
look forward to + -ing to is a preposition here look forward to hearing from you
would rather / had better preference versus advice I'd rather wait / you'd better wait

Formula / rule: For each pair, learn the single point that separates them, then test the sentence against that point: is the meaning past or present, duty or memory, preference or warning? The form follows the meaning, and the pairs only stay confusing while they are learned as shapes rather than as meanings.

★ Why it matters

These pairs are the difference between a B2 script that reads as controlled and one that reads as translated. Examiners recognise them immediately because they are the errors that persist after everything else has been fixed, and each one has a clean explanation.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers rarely confuse the pairs, but they do overuse used to in nostalgic talk and had better in warnings; the choice between would rather and prefer is a register choice as much as a meaning one.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The pairs are distinguished by weak forms in speech: used to /ˈjuːstə/ and be used to /biː ˈjuːst tə/ are distinct, and the /tə/ of the infinitive is weaker than the /tə/ of a preposition followed by -ing.

Additional example bank
  • Duty versus memory: I remembered to lock the door, and I remember locking it.
  • Preference versus advice: I'd rather wait; you'd better tell them.
  • Preposition plus gerund: We look forward to seeing the draft.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I am used to work nights (base verb after be used to)

✅ Right: I am used to working nights

❌ Wrong: I look forward to hear from you (infinitive after a preposition)

✅ Right: I look forward to hearing from you
