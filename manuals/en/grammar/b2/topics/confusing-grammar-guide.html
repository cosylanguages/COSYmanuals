B2 Upper-Intermediate Confusing Grammar Master Guide

A vs B structural contrasts, emphasis mechanisms, and stylistic precision for upper-intermediate learners

🎯 Master Goal: Control formal vs informal register, inversion, cleft structures, complex verb complements, and subtle modal distinctions.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these upper-intermediate structures:

  • "I used to live in Berlin." (Past state → 'Used to' works for states AND habits)
  • "Every summer, we would visit our grandparents." (Past repeated action → 'Would' works ONLY for repeated actions, NOT past states!)

💡 Check your understanding (CCQs)

Score: 0 / 2
1. Can you say 'I would live in London' to mean 'I lived in London in the past'?
Correct · 'Would' expresses past repeated actions/habits only, while 'used to' covers both past habits and past states.
2. What happens to word order after negative adverbials like 'Rarely' or 'Seldom' at the beginning of a sentence?
Correct · Negative adverbials at the front trigger subject-auxiliary inversion (e.g. Rarely have I seen...).

🧠 Logic Map: Core B2 Upper-Intermediate Contrasts

Structure AStructure BCore Logic DifferenceKey Examples
Used to Would Past habits AND past states vs. Repeated past activities ONLY (no states) I used to be shy. (State) vs. Every Sunday, we would walk in the park. (Action)
Standard Emphasis Cleft Sentence (What... / It is...) Neutral statement vs. Heavy focus on a specific element I need a holiday. vs. What I really need is a holiday.
Standard Conditional Conditional Inversion Standard 'If' clause vs. Formal literary drop of 'If' with inversion If you should need help... vs. Should you need help, please call.
Gradable Adjectives Extreme / Absolute Adjectives Can take 'very' (very cold, very big) vs. Requires extreme modifiers (absolutely freezing, huge) very cold (NOT ❌ very freezing) | absolutely freezing
Active Report Distancing / Passive Reporting Direct subject statement vs. Formal impersonal distance (It is said that...) People say he is rich. vs. He is said to be rich. / It is reported that...
