Past Tenses Review

Pick the past tense by what the sentence needs to show.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can choose between past simple, past continuous, past perfect and past perfect continuous.
Examples in context
  • They signed in June. Single completed event. — Past simple.
  • They were signing when the lawyers arrived. Event in progress. — Past continuous.
  • They had signed before the audit began. Earlier event. — Past perfect.

👀 What do you observe?

Four past tenses, four jobs. Past simple for completed events in sequence. Past continuous for an action in progress at a past moment, or for the background of a story. Past perfect for an event earlier than another past event. Past perfect continuous for a state or activity that had been going on before a past moment. The time expression usually decides: in 2019, when, before, for months.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
past simple completed events in sequence they signed in June
past continuous action in progress, background they were signing when we arrived
past perfect earlier event they had signed before the audit
past perfect continuous activity continuing before a past point they had been negotiating for weeks
used to / would repeated past behaviour they used to meet on Fridays
past simple after when interruption of a continuous action when the call came, we were meeting

Formula / rule: Build the timeline first: which event is the reference point, which one came before it, which one was in progress. Then choose: reference event in the past simple, earlier event in the past perfect, background in the past continuous, prolonged earlier activity in the past perfect continuous.

★ Why it matters

Past tense control is what makes a report readable: an audit that says the file had been deleted before the review began tells you the sequence without a second sentence. Mixing the four tenses carelessly produces a timeline the reader has to reconstruct, which is where comprehension fails.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the past perfect sparingly in speech and lean on before, after and then; in writing it does the work those words would do. The past perfect continuous is frequent in reports where a state had persisted.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The auxiliary is contracted in speech (they'd signed), so the stress falls on the participle when the order matters: they had SIGNED before the audit.

Additional example bank
  • Sequence: She opened the file, read the first page and closed it again.
  • Earlier: By the time the auditors arrived, the records had gone.
  • Prolonged: The team had been pressing for a decision since March.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: When I arrived, she already left (past simple for an earlier event)

✅ Right: When I arrived, she had already left

❌ Wrong: They were signing the contract before the audit began (continuous for a completed earlier event)

✅ Right: They had signed the contract before the audit began
