Formal and Informal Register

Choose the wording the situation expects.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can move a message between conversational and formal English without losing it.
Examples in context
  • Informal: We'll sort it out next week. — Contracted, phrasal, short.
  • Formal: We will resolve the matter next week. — No contraction, Latinate verb.
  • Informal: Can you check this? — Direct question.
  • Formal: I would be grateful if you could check this. — Indirect, softened.

👀 What do you observe?

Register shifts on four axes: vocabulary (get / obtain, help / assist), grammar (contractions, phrasal verbs, question forms), distance (I want / I would like; direct order / indirect request) and length (one clause or a nominalised phrase). Formal English avoids contractions, prefers single Latinate verbs to phrasal verbs, and puts requests in the conditional.

Formula / rule: Decide the audience, then adjust all four axes at once. Formalising only the vocabulary and leaving contractions produces a letter that reads as uncertain, and formalising only the grammar leaves phrasal verbs that sound out of place.

★ Why it matters

Register is not politeness, it is the signal that a writer knows which genre they are in. A complaint written in casual register is read as unserious; an internal note written in legal register is read as evasive. B2 is where learners are expected to move along the scale rather than fix at one point.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers rarely formalise a whole message; they formalise the opening and the request, and keep the middle plain. Very formal English in the wrong place (an email to a close colleague) reads as coldness or sarcasm.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Contractions reduce the vowels (we'll, can't), and formal speech restores them, which is one reason a formal statement sounds slower and more measured than the same content in conversation.

Additional example bank
  • Formal request: I would be grateful if you could confirm receipt by Friday.
  • Formal refusal: Unfortunately, we are unable to attend on that date.
  • Informal equivalent: Sorry, we can't make it on the 12th.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I want that you send the file (formal vocabulary, ungrammatical pattern)

✅ Right: I would like you to send the file

❌ Wrong: We regret to inform you that we can't come (mixed register within one sentence)

✅ Right: We regret to inform you that we are unable to attend
