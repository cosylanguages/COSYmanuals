Level B2 Grammar

Dependent Prepositions

Keep the preposition the noun or adjective requires.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the preposition that belongs to a noun, an adjective or a verb.
Examples in context
  • an increase in demand noun + in — an increase in demand, a rise in costs
  • responsible for adjective + for — responsible for the budget
  • depend on verb + on — we depend on one supplier

👀 What do you observe?

Prepositions are attached to words, not chosen by logic: an increase in, a decrease in, a demand for, a solution to, evidence for, responsibility for, dependent on, similar to, interested in. The same preposition can be right with one word and wrong with its near-synonym, so the word and the preposition have to be learned as a unit.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
noun + in increase, decrease, rise, fall an increase in demand
noun + for demand, evidence, responsibility, reason the reason for the delay
noun + to solution, answer, access, approach a solution to the problem
adjective + of aware, capable, afraid aware of the risk
adjective + to similar, due, committed, opposed similar to last year
verb + on / for depend on, account for we depend on one supplier

Formula / rule: Learn the pair, then check the pattern: a noun takes in, for, to or of by convention; an adjective takes of, to, for or with; a verb takes its own preposition. Where the preposition carries meaning (responsible for a task, responsible to a manager), the meaning decides, and both are correct.

★ Why it matters

Dependent prepositions are the clearest sign of a text written by someone translating: the meaning survives and the preposition does not. Because the pairs are arbitrary, they have to be learned with the word, and because they are frequent, a wrong one appears in almost every paragraph.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers sometimes differ: different from is standard British, different to is common in British speech, different than is American. In an exam, follow the variety you have been taught and stay with it.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The preposition is unstressed and joins the word before it: reSPONsible for, dePENdent on, an INcrease in.

Additional example bank
  • Noun: There is no evidence for the claim.
  • Adjective: The result is similar to last year's.
  • Verb: The delay accounts for most of the loss.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: an increase of demand (wrong preposition for an uncountable rise)

✅ Right: an increase in demand

❌ Wrong: dependent of one supplier (preposition taken from another adjective)

✅ Right: dependent on one supplier
