Level B2 Grammar

Used to, Would and Be Used To

Separate past habits, past states and present familiarity.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use used to, would and be used to without confusing the three.
Examples in context
  • We used to meet on Fridays. Past habit, now stopped. — Used to + base verb.
  • The office used to be smaller. Past state, now changed. — States take used to, not would.
  • I am used to working nights. Present familiarity. — Be used to + -ing.

👀 What do you observe?

Used to plus the base verb describes a past habit or state that is no longer true: we used to meet, the office used to be smaller. Would describes repeated past actions in narrative but never past states: she would sit by the window, but not she would be smaller. Be used to plus -ing describes present familiarity, and get used to plus -ing describes becoming familiar.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
used to + base past habit or state, now ended they used to meet on Fridays
would + base repeated past action in narrative she would sit by the window
would, not for states states need used to the office used to be smaller
be used to + -ing present familiarity I am used to working nights
get used to + -ing becoming familiar they got used to the new system
be used to + noun familiarity with a thing he is used to pressure

Formula / rule: Ask three questions: is the situation past or present, is it a habit or a state, and does it mean familiarity? Past habit or state: used to. Repeated past action in a story: would is possible, states excepted. Present familiarity: be or get used to plus -ing or a noun.

★ Why it matters

The three forms look alike and mean different things, which is why the error is so common and so visible: I am used to work says the speaker has not noticed that previous familiarity, present state and past habit are three different ideas. Getting them apart is a B2 marker.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use used to freely and avoid would for states without thinking, and they often pronounce used to as /juːstə/, which hides the -ed and makes the written form look arbitrary.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Used to is /ˈjuːstə/ in fast speech; be used to keeps the /d/ before a vowel (used to it), which is the one place the -ed is audible.

Additional example bank
  • Past habit: We used to hold the review on Mondays.
  • Past state: The team used to be much smaller.
  • Familiarity: She is used to presenting to the board.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The office would be smaller in the nineties (would for a past state)

✅ Right: The office used to be smaller in the nineties

❌ Wrong: I am used to work late (infinitive after be used to)

✅ Right: I am used to working late
