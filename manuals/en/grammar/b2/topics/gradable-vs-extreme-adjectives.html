Gradable and Extreme Adjectives

Choose the intensifier the adjective allows.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can grade gradable adjectives and intensify extreme ones without breaking the collocation.
Examples in context
  • very good gradable: more or less good — The proposal is very good.
  • absolutely excellent extreme: no degrees — The proposal is absolutely excellent.
  • slightly late gradable, small degree — The train was slightly late.

👀 What do you observe?

Gradable adjectives admit degrees, so they take very, fairly, quite, slightly and rather, and they have comparatives: long, longer. Extreme adjectives already mean the top of the scale, so they take absolututes like absolutely, utterly, completely and totally, and normally have no comparative: disastrous, not more disastrous. Some adjectives sit in both groups depending on sense.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
gradable + very mid-scale adjectives very long, very useful
gradable + slightly / fairly small or moderate degree slightly late, fairly warm
extreme + absolutely / utterly top-of-scale adjectives absolutely disastrous, utterly worthless
extreme + completely / totally full extent completely exhausted, totally amazed
extreme, no comparative already at the limit disastrous, not more disastrous
both senses sense decides the group a very cold room, absolutely freezing

Formula / rule: Ask whether the adjective can be measured: if it can, grade it with very or slightly; if it already means the limit, intensify it with absolutely. Then check the collocation, because the intensifier and the adjective are learned together: absolutely delighted, utterly ridiculous, highly unlikely.

★ Why it matters

Wrong intensifiers are the most audible collocation error at B2, because native speakers hear very disastrous as a mistake rather than a style choice. The pairs are also a vocabulary test: knowing that exhausted is extreme tells you which intensifier belongs and which comparative does not.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use absolutely with extreme adjectives far more than very, and they often drop the intensifier altogether in speech: it was disastrous. Pretty is the spoken intensifier for gradable adjectives (pretty good), and it is informal.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Absolutely carries the main stress when it intensifies: it was ABSolutely disastrous; the adjective keeps a secondary stress.

Additional example bank
  • Extreme: The delay was absolutely catastrophic for the schedule.
  • Gradable: The reply was rather late.
  • Collocation: The figures are highly unlikely to change.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The failure was very disastrous (very with an extreme adjective)

✅ Right: The failure was absolutely disastrous

❌ Wrong: The evidence is absolutely weak (absolute intensifier with a gradable adjective)

✅ Right: The evidence is very weak
