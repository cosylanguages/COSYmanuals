Rhetorical Questions & Parallelism

Ask a question you will answer, and line up structures that match.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can build a persuasive paragraph with questions that lead and structures that balance.
Examples in context
  • What does the board actually want? It wants certainty, and certainty is expensive. — Question, then answer.
  • Not only did costs rise, but quality fell. Balanced correlatives. — Parallel halves joined by but.
  • We came to listen, to argue, and to decide. Three matching infinitives. — Same form three times.

👀 What do you observe?

A rhetorical question names the question the audience already holds, and the paragraph answers it in its second sentence. Parallelism repeats a grammatical shape - infinitive, infinitive, infinitive or not only X but Y - so that the reader hears the structure and expects the last item. Break the parallel and the sentence loses its grip.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
rhetorical question names the audience's question why does the scheme keep failing?
answer sentence pays the question off because nobody owns it
not only X but also Y balanced correlatives not only did costs rise but quality fell
three-part list same form repeated to listen, to argue, and to decide
antithesis parallel with contrast we came to listen, not to lecture
anaphora repeated opening we need clarity, we need ownership, we need time

Formula / rule: Ask one question and answer it, then build your list or contrast out of identical forms. For a three-part list, keep the same word class and the same length: to listen, to argue, to decide, not to listen, arguing, and a decision. Keep the strongest item last.

★ Why it matters

Persuasion in English leans on rhythm: the audience hears the shape before it judges the content. A balanced structure signals that the speaker has finished, which is why parallels close paragraphs and rhetorical questions open them. Rhetorical questions also carry risk, because an audience that already knows the answer may resent being asked.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, the question ends on a fall-rise and the answer starts on a step up in pitch; in writing, the question mark does the work. Parallel items usually shorten as the list goes on, and the shortest item carries the most weight.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The rhetorical question is marked by a fall-rise on the last stressed word, while the answer falls: Why does the scheme keep failing? Because nobody OWNS it.

Additional example bank
  • Question: What would a serious programme look like? It would name an owner.
  • Parallel: We need clarity, we need ownership, and we need time.
  • Antithesis: The choice is not between speed and care, but between ownership and drift.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: We came to listen, arguing, and to decide (the three items do not match)

✅ Right: We came to listen, to argue, and to decide

❌ Wrong: Not only costs rose, but quality fell (correlative half missing the auxiliary)

✅ Right: Not only did costs rise, but quality fell
