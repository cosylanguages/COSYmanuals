Suffixes & Word Formation

Build the word class you need from the word you have.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can turn a verb or adjective into the noun, adjective or adverb a sentence needs.
Examples in context
  • decide → decision The decision was taken in June. — Verb to noun with -sion.
  • rely → reliable The source is reliable. — Verb to adjective with -able.
  • care → careless The reply was careless. — Noun to adjective with -less.

👀 What do you observe?

A suffix fixes the word class: -tion and -ment make nouns, -able and -ive make adjectives, -ly makes adverbs, -ise makes verbs. The same root takes different suffixes with different meanings, and some combinations are lexical, not logical.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
-tion / -sion verb to noun implement → implementation
-ment verb to noun agree → agreement
-able / -ible verb to adjective rely → reliable
-ive verb to adjective persuade → persuasive
-ly adjective to adverb remarkable → remarkably
-ise / -ify noun or adjective to verb modern → modernise, pure → purify

Formula / rule: Choose the suffix by the slot, not by the root: after a determiner you need a noun (-tion, -ment, -ness), before a noun you need an adjective (-able, -ive, -ous), and a verb needs -ise, -ify or -en. Then check the spelling shift: decide → decision, maintain → maintenance.

★ Why it matters

Word formation is where vocabulary and grammar meet: a sentence can be grammatically perfect and still wrong because the slot holds an adjective where a noun belongs. Suffixes are the cheapest way to expand what you can say at B2 without learning new roots.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers do not build words from rules in real time; they recognise whole words. Use the suffix rules to check a word you are unsure of, not to invent new ones, and note that -ly adverbs of manner are common but some are lexical (hardly means barely, not in a hard way).

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Suffix -tion is /ʃən/ (implementation), -sion after a vowel is /ʒən/ (decision), -ous is weak /əs/ (nervous), and -ly adds a syllable only when the adjective ends in -le or -ble.

Additional example bank
  • Noun: The maintenance of the fleet costs more than its purchase.
  • Adjective: The argument is persuasive but thin.
  • Adverb: The figures were remarkably consistent.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The decision was implement quickly (noun slot filled by a verb)

✅ Right: The decision was implemented quickly

❌ Wrong: She spoke with great confident (adjective where a noun is needed)

✅ Right: She spoke with great confidence
