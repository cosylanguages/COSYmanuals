Get: Meanings and Patterns

Use get as a change, an obtainment, a passive and a causative.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can read and use the four main meanings of get without confusing them.
Examples in context
  • It got colder after lunch. Change of state. — Get + adjective.
  • We got the file at noon. Obtainment. — Get + object.
  • He got promoted. Passive with get. — Get + past participle.

👀 What do you observe?

Get is the workhorse verb of spoken English and carries four distinct patterns. Change: get plus adjective or comparative (get worse, get colder). Obtainment: get plus object (get the file, get approval). Passive: get plus past participle, usually for something unwanted or sudden (get caught, get fired). Causative: get plus object plus past participle, meaning to arrange for something to be done (get the report rewritten).

Element / structure Role in English Application example
get + adjective change of state it got colder
get + object obtainment we got the approval
get + past participle informal passive, often unwanted he got caught
get + object + past participle causative she got the report rewritten
get + object + to-infinitive persuasion I got them to agree
get + -ing start or succeed we got moving at nine

Formula / rule: Identify the pattern, then read the meaning: adjective means change, noun object means obtainment, participle alone means passive, object plus participle means causative, object plus to-infinitive means persuasion. In formal writing, replace get with obtain, become, arrange or be plus participle.

★ Why it matters

Get is the verb learners avoid and native speakers cannot do without, which makes it both a comprehension gap and a register marker. Understanding the four patterns is what lets a learner follow ordinary conversation, and avoiding them in a report is what makes the report formal.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In British English, have got means possession (I've got the file) and have to means obligation; get is also the standard verb for arriving (what time do you get in?). Americans use gotten as the participle (he's gotten worse).

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Get is stressed in all four patterns, and the contracted have got reduces to /aɪv ɡɒt/ in British speech.

Additional example bank
  • Change: The market got quieter after the announcement.
  • Obtainment: We got three bids by Friday.
  • Causative: They got the contract translated before signing.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: She got the report rewrite (base verb where a participle is needed)

✅ Right: She got the report rewritten

❌ Wrong: It was got worse after lunch (passive with a change-of-state adjective)

✅ Right: It got worse after lunch
