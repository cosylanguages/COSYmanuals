Relative Clauses: Review and Deepening

Attach a clause to a noun without breaking the sentence.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can define, add and shorten relative clauses at B2 level.
Examples in context
  • The analyst who wrote the note has left the team. — Defining: identifies which analyst.
  • The note, which was leaked, caused an outcry. — Non-defining: adds information.
  • The cost of which we spoke has doubled. — Preposition plus which in formal register.

👀 What do you observe?

A defining clause tells you which noun is meant and takes no commas: the report that we commissioned. A non-defining clause adds information and takes commas: the report, which we commissioned, has arrived. That never follows a comma; which can. Whose replaces a possessive, where replaces a place adverbial, and preposition + which replaces a prepositional phrase at the end of a formal clause.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
who / that person, subject the analyst who wrote the note
which / that thing, subject or object the report that we commissioned
whose possessive the consultant whose figures we used
where / when place or time adverbial the office where the audit took place
preposition + which formal, replaces a stranded preposition the cost of which we spoke
reduced clause participle replaces the pronoun and verb the report commissioned last year

Formula / rule: Decide first whether the clause identifies (no commas, that allowed) or adds (commas, that forbidden). Then choose the pronoun by its role inside the clause, not in the main sentence. Finally, cut: if the pronoun is the object, delete it; if the clause is passive or progressive, reduce it to a participle.

★ Why it matters

Relative clauses are where B2 writing gains density: one sentence can carry a definition, a comment and a source. They are also where the most visible errors live, because the pronoun's role inside its own clause decides the choice, and the comma changes the meaning rather than the tone.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers drop the object pronoun without noticing (the report we commissioned) and use where for any place at all, including metaphorical ones (the point where we disagree). In careful writing, where should replace an adverbial of place, not of time or reason.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The relative pronoun is unstressed unless it is the subject, and the clause after a comma drops in pitch, which is how a listener hears that the information is an aside rather than a definition.

Additional example bank
  • Reduced: The figures collected in March are now suspect.
  • Non-defining with whose: The chair, whose term ends in May, will not stand again.
  • Prepositional: The meeting at which the decision was taken was not minuted.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The report, that we commissioned, has arrived (that after a comma)

✅ Right: The report, which we commissioned, has arrived

❌ Wrong: The analyst who he wrote the note has left (pronoun and subject both kept)

✅ Right: The analyst who wrote the note has left
