Participle Clauses

Compress a second clause into a participle.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can replace a full clause with an -ing or -ed clause when the subject is shared.
Examples in context
  • Feeling unwell, he left early. He felt unwell and he left early. — Same subject, active.
  • Published in May, the report caused a stir. The report was published and caused a stir. — Passive relation.
  • Having signed the deal, they celebrated. They had signed, then they celebrated. — Earlier action.

👀 What do you observe?

A participle clause replaces a clause when both clauses share the same subject. The -ing participle is active and often simultaneous: walking to the office, she called. The -ed participle is passive: written in 1904, the manual is still used. Having + participle marks an action completed before the main one. A wrong subject is a dangling participle, the error this structure punishes hardest.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
-ing (active) same subject, simultaneous walking to the office, she called the client
-ed (passive) the subject receives the action written in 1904, the manual is still used
having + participle action completed first having finished the audit, the team filed
having been + participle passive action completed first having been warned, they checked again
reduced relative replaces who/which + verb the figures collected in March
with + noun + participle different subject allowed with the deadline moved, the plan changed

Formula / rule: Check that both clauses share a subject, then choose the participle by voice: active -ing, passive -ed, completed then having + participle. If the subjects differ, keep two clauses or use with + noun + participle: with the deadline moved, we replanned.

★ Why it matters

Participle clauses are how academic and journalistic English compresses: one sentence carries a cause, a condition or a sequence without a connector. They are also unforgiving, because a mismatched subject produces a sentence that says something absurd, and readers notice.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, participle clauses are rare outside fixed openers (speaking of which, given the time). In writing, they cluster at the start of sentences, where the shared subject is checked by the reader on the first pass.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The participle is unstressed in the middle of a clause and stressed when it opens one: HAVING finished the audit, the team filed. The comma marks the boundary and the fall in pitch.

Additional example bank
  • Cause: Having missed the deadline, the team lost the contract.
  • Passive: Submitted late, the application was rejected.
  • Different subject: With the vote taken, the meeting closed.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Walking to the office, the phone rang (dangling participle: the phone was not walking)

✅ Right: Walking to the office, she took the call, or: as she walked to the office, the phone rang

❌ Wrong: Having finished the audit, the report was filed (subject of the participle is not the report's)

✅ Right: Having finished the audit, the team filed the report
