Passive Verbs with Two Objects

Decide which object becomes the subject.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can turn give, offer, send and tell into the passive with either object in front.
Examples in context
  • They offered the board a timetable. The board was offered a timetable. — Person becomes the subject.
  • They offered a timetable to the board. A timetable was offered to the board. — Thing becomes the subject, with to.
  • They told us nothing. We were told nothing. — Person first is the usual passive.

👀 What do you observe?

Verbs with two objects (give, offer, send, show, tell, pay, teach, promise, hand, owe) can form two passives. When the person becomes the subject, the thing stays as it is: the board was offered a timetable. When the thing becomes the subject, the person moves into a to- or for-phrase: a timetable was offered to the board. The person-first passive is far more frequent, because people are usually the topic.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
person-first usual passive with two objects the board was offered a timetable
thing-first with to the thing is the topic a timetable was offered to the board
thing-first with for buy, get, book, make a room was booked for the delegation
verbs that allow both give, offer, send, show, tell, pay we were told nothing / nothing was told to us
verbs that allow one explain, suggest, describe, announce the plan was explained to us
reporting with two objects tell, inform, notify we were informed of the delay

Formula / rule: Choose the subject by the topic: if the reader is following a person, put the person first; if the reader is following a document or a sum, put the thing first and use to. With explain, suggest and describe, only the thing-first passive is available, and the person needs to.

★ Why it matters

This structure is the grammar of institutional speech: we were told nothing is what a witness says, while nothing was told to us is what a lawyer writes when the absence is the point. The choice of subject decides who the sentence is about, which is rarely neutral.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech the person-first passive is overwhelming: I was given, we were told, she was sent. The thing-first passive with to appears mostly in writing, where a document or a sum is the topic.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The passive auxiliary carries the stress when the omission of the agent is the point: we WERE told nothing, though everyone knew.

Additional example bank
  • Person-first: The delegation was shown the draft before publication.
  • Thing-first: The draft was shown to the delegation before publication.
  • Single-option verb: The process was explained to us twice.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The plan was explained us (missing to with a single-option verb)

✅ Right: The plan was explained to us

❌ Wrong: A timetable was offered the board (person left without a preposition in a thing-first passive)

✅ Right: A timetable was offered to the board
