Compound Nouns

Join two words into the noun the language has settled on.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can build and stress compound nouns, and know when to hyphenate.
Examples in context
  • a board meeting board modifies meeting — The head noun comes last.
  • a risk assessment risk modifies assessment — Two nouns, no hyphen.
  • a two-page brief number + noun, hyphenated — Hyphen when the compound precedes the noun.

👀 What do you observe?

A compound noun puts the modifier first and the head last, and the head decides number and meaning: a board meeting is a meeting, a meeting board is a board. Most compounds are written as two words (risk assessment), some close up (notebook, database), and compounds with a number or a participle are hyphenated when they come before the noun (a two-page brief, a well-known author). Stress falls on the first element, which is how listeners tell a compound from a phrase.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
noun + noun modifier before head risk assessment, board meeting
head decides number plural on the final noun board meetings, risk assessments
closed form written as one word notebook, database, website
hyphen before the noun compound used as a modifier a two-page brief, a well-known author
no hyphen after the noun predicate position the brief was two pages long
stress on the first element distinguishes compound from phrase a GREENhouse and a green HOUSE

Formula / rule: Decide the head noun first, then the modifier, then the spelling: two words by default, one word if the language has closed it, hyphens only when the compound modifies another noun and would otherwise be misread. Keep the plural on the head.

★ Why it matters

Compounds are where vocabulary and grammar meet, and where a wrong head produces a different object: a training manager manages training, a manager training trains managers. In technical and business English, compounds carry most of the terminology, so the head noun is the word the reader is actually looking for.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers stress the first element so reliably that the stress pattern is the real marker: a blackBIRD is a species, a black BIRD is a bird that is black. Learners who stress the last element sound as if they are describing rather than naming.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The first element is stressed and slightly longer, and the second is weaker: BOARD meeting, RISK assessment. In hyphenated modifiers each element keeps a light stress: a TWO-page BRIEF.

Additional example bank
  • Business: The quarterly risk review is on Friday.
  • Technical: The check-in system failed twice.
  • Predicate: The brief is two pages long.

❌ Wrong: a two-pages brief (plural inside a hyphenated modifier)

✅ Right: a two-page brief
