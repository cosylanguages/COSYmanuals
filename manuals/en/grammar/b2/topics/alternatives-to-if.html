Alternatives to If

Make a condition sound formal, tentative or hypothetical.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can build conditionals with unless, provided that, in case, otherwise and inversion.
Examples in context
  • Unless the audit clears, the launch slips. If the audit does not clear. — Unless = if not.
  • Provided that the terms hold, we sign. Only on that condition. — A stated condition.
  • In case the line fails, we keep a backup. Precaution, not condition. — Different meaning from if.

👀 What do you observe?

Alternatives to if each carry their own logic. Unless means if not, and it cannot take a second negative. Provided that, as long as and on condition that state an explicit requirement. In case means as a precaution, so it describes an action taken before a possible event, not a condition on a result. Otherwise states the consequence of not meeting the condition. Inversion (Had we known, Should the offer fail, Were the data complete) raises the register and removes if altogether.

Formula / rule: Choose by logic, not by style: unless for a negative condition, provided that for a requirement, in case for a precaution, otherwise for the alternative consequence. Then, if the register needs lifting, invert instead of using if: Had we known, Were the data complete, Should the offer fail.

★ Why it matters

These linkers are where legal and technical English lives, because they narrow a condition exactly: provided that the fee is paid is stricter than if the fee is paid. In case is the one learners misuse most, since it looks like if and means the opposite kind of thing.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use unless freely in speech and invert only in writing or prepared remarks. In case is common in speech as a precaution marker, and its negative form (in case it doesn't rain) is rarer than it looks.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Inversion carries a stress on the auxiliary: HAD we known; SHOULD the offer fail. Unstressed, the inverted form can be misheard as a statement.

Additional example bank
  • Condition: As long as the sample holds, the result stands.
  • Precaution: Keep the receipt in case the refund is refused.
  • Consequence: Submit the form by Friday; otherwise the application lapses.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Unless you don't submit by Friday, the offer lapses (double negative)

✅ Right: Unless you submit by Friday, the offer lapses

❌ Wrong: We keep a backup if the line fails (precaution written as a condition)

✅ Right: We keep a backup in case the line fails
