Permission, Obligation and Prohibition

Say what is allowed, required and forbidden, and who decided it.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use modals, semi-modals and formal structures for rules and duties.
Examples in context
  • Permission: You may use the archive on request. — Formal permission.
  • Obligation: You have to register first. — External rule.
  • Prohibition: You must not remove the files. — Absolute ban.
  • No obligation: You don't need to print it. — Optional.

👀 What do you observe?

The same obligation changes strength with its source: must imposes, have to reports, must not forbids, needn't releases. Read each example for who decided.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
must obligation from the speaker you must submit the form today
have to / has to obligation from outside staff have to wear badges
must not / be prohibited from prohibition visitors must not photograph the exhibits
needn't / don't have to no obligation you needn't book in advance
may / be allowed to permission you may use the archive on request
shall / is to be formal requirement in documents the contractor shall complete the work by June

Formula / rule: Obligation comes in two kinds: must and have to for what is required, with must usually expressing the speaker's authority and have to an external rule. Prohibition is must not or is not allowed to; don't have to and needn't mean no obligation at all, which is the opposite. Read the rule from two directions: is the action required or optional, and who decided it. In contracts, shall carries the obligation and may carries the permission, and neither is decorative.

★ Why it matters

A rulebook is read by people looking for what they may and must do, so the modal is the operative word: a prohibition written as you shouldn't is not a prohibition. In contracts the difference between shall and may decides who carries the risk.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers keep must for written rules and self-imposed duties, and use have to for everything institutional in speech: I have to file the report. Mustn't in speech is a warning from the speaker, which is why it sounds stronger than you're not allowed to.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Must is unstressed when it is a plain obligation (I must go) and stressed when it corrects (you MUST read it). Have to reduces to /hæftə/, and mustn't keeps a full /ʌ/ in careful speech.

Additional example bank
  • Notice: Bags must not be left unattended.
  • Contract: The lessee shall maintain the premises.
  • Colleague: You don't need to copy me in.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: You mustn't pay; entry is free (prohibition where no obligation is meant)

✅ Right: You don't need to pay; entry is free

❌ Wrong: You must to submit the form (modal followed by to)

✅ Right: You must submit the form
