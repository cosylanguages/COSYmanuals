Verb Patterns & Adjective Confusions

Mastering infinitives, gerunds, verb + noun structures, and -ed vs -ing adjective feelings

🎯 Communicative Goal: Express desires, needs, hobbies, and emotional states using correct verb complement structures and adjective endings.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these structures:

  • "I want coffee." (Want + Noun) vs. "I want to drink coffee." (Want + TO + Verb)
  • "The movie is boring." (The movie causes boredom) vs. "I am bored." (I feel bored)
  • "She is good at maths." (Ability) vs. "Exercise is good for you." (Health benefit)

🧠 Concept Check Questions (CCQs)

Score: 0 / 3
1. Does -ed describe a person's feeling or the cause of the feeling?
Correct · People feel -ed (bored, interested); things/situations are -ing (boring, interesting).
2. What follows 'want' when you state an action?
Correct · Want requires 'to + infinitive' for actions: 'I want to go'.
3. What preposition is used for skills and abilities with 'good'?
Correct · Abilities take 'good at'; health/benefits take 'good for'.

1. Like, Love, Hate & Preferences (Points 54, 55, 112)

PatternMeaning / FocusExamples
LIKE + Noun Preference for things / objects I like coffee. She likes books.
LIKE + Verb-ing Enjoyment of the activity itself I like swimming. They love cooking.
LIKE + TO + Verb Habit, choice, or routine choice I like to check my emails in the morning.

2. Want & Need: Noun vs TO + Verb (Points 113 & 114)

  • WANT + Noun: Desiring an object. I want a coffee. He wants a new car.
  • WANT TO + Verb: Desiring an action. I want to drink coffee. He wants to buy a car.
  • NEED + Noun: Requiring an object. I need money. We need help.
  • NEED TO + Verb: Requiring an action. I need to work. We need to leave.

3. Adjectives + TO Verb & Prepositions (Points 119, 120)

  • Adjective + TO + Verb: Expressing feelings or difficulty about an action. I am happy to help. She is ready to leave. It is difficult to understand.
  • GOOD AT vs GOOD FOR:
    • Good AT + noun/ing: Skill or ability. She is good at maths. He is good at playing guitar.
    • Good FOR + noun: Benefit or health. Vegetables are good for you. Running is good for health.

4. Feelings vs Characteristics: -ED vs -ING Adjectives (Points 121, 122)

Adjective EndingFocusExamples
-ED (Feeling) How a person feels inside I am bored. She is interested in history. We are tired.
-ING (Cause) The person, thing, or situation causing the feeling The lesson is boring. History is interesting. Long trips are tiring.
