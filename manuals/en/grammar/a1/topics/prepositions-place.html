Prepositions of Place & Movement

Where things are and where they are going

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can accurately describe physical positions (in, on, at, under, between, among) and directions (to, from, into) in everyday English.

👀 What do you notice?

Observe the prepositions used in different situational contexts:

  • The cat is in the box, but the keys are on the table.
  • I am going to Paris (destination), but she comes from Spain (origin).
  • We are at Mark's house / at the doctor's.
  • The shop is between two banks (2 items), but he was standing among the crowd (group).

Common Spatial & Movement Comparisons

PairMeaning & RuleExamples
IN vs ONIn = enclosed 3D space | On = surface contactin the car, in Paris | on the table, on the wall
TO vs FROMTo = movement towards | From = origin/sourcegoing to work | coming from Rome
AT [person's]At + Possessive = visiting someone's home or businessat John's house, at the doctor's, at Google
BETWEEN vs AMONGBetween = 2 distinct objects | Among = 3+ in a groupbetween A and B | among friends

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing Prepositions of Place, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.

More examples
  • The cat is in the basket.
  • The cat is on the table.
  • I am staying at my friend's house.
  • She is traveling from London to Tokyo.
  • The bank is between the bakery and the school.

💡 Think like English

English uses TO for motion towards a goal, FROM for origin, and AT for specific locations or social destinations (at the station, at John's).
