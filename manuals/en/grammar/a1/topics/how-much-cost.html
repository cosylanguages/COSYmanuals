How Much Is It? Prices and Cost

Asking for and giving prices

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can ask the price of something and understand the answer in a shop or a café.
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.

👀 What do you notice?

Look at the questions and answers. Which verb goes with one thing? Which verb goes with more than one thing?

Examples
  • How much is this coffee? It’s two euros.
  • How much are these apples? They’re three euros.
  • How much does the ticket cost? It costs ten euros.
  • How much do the shoes cost? They cost fifty euros.

Rule: Ask with How much + is/are + thing or How much + does/do + thing + cost. Use is / does for one thing and are / do for more than one. After does or do, the verb is cost (no -s). The number is plural: one euro, two euros.

💭 Think like a native speaker

English has two natural ways to ask the same thing. In shops How much is it? is the most common. How much does it cost? sounds a little more careful. Both are polite.

💡 Memory trick

One thing → is / does + it. Many things → are / do + they. Say the price with a number and the money word: two euros.
