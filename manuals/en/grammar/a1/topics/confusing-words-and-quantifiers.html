Confusing Words & Quantifiers Master Guide

Complete contrast guide for frequency, quantifiers, adjectives vs adverbs, and similar words

🎯 Communicative Goal: Distinguish similar words and quantifiers accurately, describe degree and frequency, and position adjectives and adverbs correctly.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these subtle pairs:

  • "I study English every day." (Frequency adverb) vs. "This is my everyday routine." (Adjective)
  • "He is a good driver." (Adjective describing 'driver') vs. "He drives well." (Adverb describing 'drives')
  • "This film is very long." (Acceptable) vs. "This film is too long." (Problem / excessive)

🧠 Concept Check Questions (CCQs)

Score: 0 / 3
1. What is the difference between 'very' and 'too'?
Correct · 'Very' strengthens an adjective (very good); 'too' indicates a negative excess or problem (too expensive).
2. Which word is used for vertical height of people and buildings?
Correct · 'Tall' is used for people and narrow vertical structures; 'high' is used for mountains, walls, and heights above ground.
3. Is 'every day' written as one word or two words when it means 'each day'?
Correct · 'Every day' (two words) = each day; 'everyday' (one word) = common/normal adjective.

1. Quantifiers & Frequency Contrasts (Points 58, 59, 60, 61, 62)

PairRules & DistinctionExamples
EVERY vs ALL Every + singular noun (individual members)
All + plural/uncountable noun (the whole group)		 Every student has a book.
All students passed the exam.
EACH vs EVERY Each = Focus on individuals one by one
Every = Focus on the whole group as individuals		 Each student received a certificate.
Every student must register.
EVERY DAY vs EVERYDAY Every day (two words) = Frequency adverb ("each day")
Everyday (one word) = Adjective ("normal / routine")		 I study English every day.
These are my everyday clothes.
ONCE vs FIRST TIME Once = Frequency ("one time")
First time = Initial experience		 I visit my doctor once a year.
This is my first time in London.
AGO vs BEFORE Ago = Finished past relative to now (with time duration)
Before = Earlier than another moment in past		 I met her two years ago.
I have seen this movie before.

2. Adjective & Adverb Confusions (Points 74, 81, 82, 83, 84, 95)

  • MAYBE vs MAY BE: Maybe = adverb (Maybe he is busy). May be = modal verb phrase (He may be busy).
  • GOOD vs WELL: Good = adjective describing nouns (a good teacher). Well = adverb describing actions (she teaches well).
  • FAST vs QUICKLY: Fast = speed of movement (he runs fast). Quickly = little time required (he finished quickly).
  • HIGH vs TALL: Tall = people and vertical narrow structures (a tall man, a tall building). High = mountains, walls, height above ground (a high mountain, a high wall).
  • BIG vs LARGE vs GREAT: Big = common size word (a big house). Large = formal size / measurement (a large amount). Great = outstanding / important (a great idea).
  • VERY vs TOO: Very = high degree (very good). Too = negative excess / problem (too expensive = cannot buy).

3. Adjective Order & Word Order Rules (Points 123 & 130)

Standard Adjective Order: Opinion ➔ Size ➔ Age ➔ Shape ➔ Colour ➔ Origin ➔ Material ➔ Noun

Example: ✅ a beautiful small old red Italian leather bag (NOT ❌ a leather red small bag).

Adjective & Adverb Position:

  • Adjectives go BEFORE nouns: a big house (NOT ❌ a house big).
  • Adverbs of frequency go BEFORE main verbs, but AFTER the verb to be: I usually drink tea. / I am always tired.
