The Verb 'To Be'

Am, is, are · affirmative, negative & question

1. In English, do we use 'BE' or 'HAVE' to state someone's age? (e.g. He is 20)
Correct · English uses 'To Be' for age: "I am 25 years old", NOT "I have 25 years".
2. How do we describe weather conditions in English?
Correct · English uses "It is + adjective" (It is hot/cold) or "It is + -ing" (It is raining).
3. Sentence check: Can you say "Julia are a teacher"?
No · Singular subject 'Julia' (she) requires 'is', not 'are'.

Key Usage Patterns with 'To Be'

CategoryCorrect English FormulaExample
AgeSubject + am / is / are + [number] (years old)I am 25 years old. (NOT I have 25)
WeatherIt is + Adjective / Weather conditionIt is hot / cold / sunny today.
Jobs & NationalitiesHe/She is a/an + Job | He/She is + NationalityHe is a doctor. She is Italian.
IdentificationIt is / This is + Noun (Identification)It is my book. This is Paul.

★ Why this matters

To be is the most common verb in English by far · and it's the building block for structures you'll meet soon, like the continuous tenses (is/are + -ing).

💡 Think like English

In English, your age is a state of being (you ARE 20 years old), not a physical object you possess (you don't HAVE years). Weather is also a state of the environment (It IS cold, not It makes cold).

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Notice how 'am', 'is', 'are' contract in speech: I'm /aɪm/, he's /hiːz/, they're /ðeə/. Unstressed auxiliary forms reduce naturally in spoken phrases.

More examples
