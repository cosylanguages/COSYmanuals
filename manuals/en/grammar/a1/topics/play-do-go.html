Play vs Do vs Go

Sports, activities, and musical instruments

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can correctly describe your sports, hobbies, physical activities, and musical skills without confusing play, do, and go.

👀 What do you notice?

Look at the verbs used with different sports and activities:

  • I play football on Saturdays. She plays the piano.
  • He does yoga every morning. We do karate.
  • They go swimming in summer. I go running at 7 AM.

1. Which verb do we use with team sports played with a ball (like football or basketball)?
Correct · 'Play' is used for team sports, ball games, and competitive games.
2. Which verb is used with activities that end in '-ing' (like swimming, running, skiing)?
Correct · 'Go' is used with activities ending in -ing that involve moving from place to place.
Verb Usage Rule Examples
PLAY Team sports, ball games, board games, competitive sports, & musical instruments (with the). play football, play tennis, play chess, play the piano
DO Individual non-team sports, martial arts, exercise routines, and non-ball physical activities. do yoga, do karate, do gymnastics, do exercise
GO Activities ending in -ing that involve movement or traveling outside. go swimming, go running, go skiing, go shopping

Golden Rule: Use Play for ball games and musical instruments (play football, play the guitar).
Use Do for self-contained exercises and martial arts (do yoga, do karate).
Use Go for outdoor/movement activities ending in -ing (go swimming, go running).

🧠 Think like English

English separates hobbies by how you perform them: competitive ball games involve 'playing' against rules or opponents, martial arts and exercises involve 'doing' repetitions, and travelling activities involve 'going' to a venue or moving through space.

💡 Memory Trick

P-D-G Formula:
🏀 Play = Points & Pianos (basketball, chess, guitar)
🧘 Do = Discipline & Dojo (yoga, karate)
🏊 Go = Going out with -ING (swimming, running)
