Too vs Enough

Excess (Too) vs Sufficiency (Enough)

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can explain when something is excessive (too hot, too expensive) or sufficient (warm enough, enough money) and place "enough" correctly before nouns and after adjectives.
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare the position and meaning of too and enough:

  • This coffee is too hot to drink. (Excessive / Problem)
  • This tea is warm enough. (Sufficient / Just right)
  • I have enough money for the bus. (Sufficient quantity)

💡 Check your understanding

Score: 0 / 2
1. Does "too hot" mean it is very hot in a good way, or more than acceptable/desired?
Correct · 'Too' indicates excess that often creates a problem or limitation.
2. Where does 'enough' go when describing an adjective (e.g., tall)?
Correct · Adjectives come BEFORE 'enough' (tall enough, old enough).
Word Position Rule Example
TOO TOO + Adjective / Adverb The room is too cold. He drives too fast.
ENOUGH (Adjective) Adjective + ENOUGH She is old enough to drive. (Not: enough old)
ENOUGH (Noun) ENOUGH + Noun We have enough time. Do you have enough water?

Formulas:
• Too + Adjective = excess (too expensive)
• Adjective + Enough = sufficient degree (big enough)
• Enough + Noun = sufficient quantity (enough chairs)

🧠 Think like English

Don't confuse very with too. Very increases intensity ("This cake is very good!"), while too signals an unwelcome excess or obstacle ("This cake is too sweet for me").

💡 Memory Trick

The Sandwich Rule:
• Adjectives come FIRST: Old + Enough
• Nouns come SECOND: Enough + Money
Think: Adjective-Enough-Noun (Enough sits in the middle!)
