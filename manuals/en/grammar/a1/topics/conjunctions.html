Conjunctions

And, but, or, because, so, when, if, although

Situational Context Examples
  • Alex likes coffee and tea.
  • Sarah wanted to go to the park, but it rained.
  • Do you want water or juice?
  • Alex stayed home because he was tired.
  • It was cold, so Sarah wore a coat.
  • Alex listens to music when he walks to work.
  • If it rains tomorrow, we will stay inside.
  • Although it was cold, Alex went for a walk. (receptive recognition)

👀 What do you notice?

Notice how conjunctions connect two words or full ideas (clauses). When if, when, or although starts a sentence, notice the comma , separating the two parts!

🧠 Concept check

  • Does because introduce the cause or reason (Why?) for something? (Yes)
  • Does so introduce the outcome or result (What happened next?)? (Yes)
  • In "If it rains, we stay inside", do we stay inside every day? (No, only on the condition that it rains)
  • Is although used to connect ideas that naturally agree or ideas with a surprising contrast? (Surprising contrast)

🪜 Sentence-Building Ladder

Watch how a short 3-word sentence grows step-by-step into richer communication:

1. Base sentence: I go for a walk.
2. Add info (+ AND): I go for a walk and listen to music.
3. Add contrast (+ BUT): I go for a walk, but it is cold outside.
4. Add reason (+ BECAUSE): I go for a walk because I love fresh air.
5. Add result (+ SO): It is sunny, so I go for a walk.
6. Add condition (+ IF): If the weather is fine, I go for a walk.
7. Add time (+ WHEN): I wear a scarf when I go for a walk.
8. Add surprising contrast (+ ALTHOUGH): Although it is cold, I go for a walk.

🌳 Quick Logic Decision Guide: Which word do I need?

  • ➕ Adding information? ➔ Use AND
  • 🔀 Choosing between options? ➔ Use OR
  • ⚡ Direct contrast? ➔ Use BUT
  • 😲 Surprising contrast? ➔ Recognize ALTHOUGH
  • ❓ Explaining WHY (the cause)? ➔ Use BECAUSE
  • ➡️ Explaining WHAT HAPPENS NEXT (the result)? ➔ Use SO
  • 🔑 Setting a condition (only if)? ➔ Use IF
  • ⏰ Pointing to a time or moment? ➔ Use WHEN

🧠 Think like English: Sentence Connectors

English conjunctions act as bridges between ideas. Word order inside each connected clause stays completely normal: [Subject + Verb] + Conjunction + [Subject + Verb].

When sentence-linking words like If, When, or Although come at the beginning of the sentence, place a comma , after the first clause before continuing to the main thought!

⚠ Common mistakes
❌It was cold, because I put on my jacket.
tap to see the correct form
✅It was cold, so I put on my jacket. (Putting on a jacket is the result, not the cause of cold weather!)
❌Although it was raining, but we went out.
tap to see the correct form
✅Although it was raining, we went out. (Do not double-link with 'although' and 'but' together in one sentence!)
❌I'm tired because I not sleep well.
tap to see the correct form
✅I'm tired because I didn't sleep well.
❌I call you if I will arrive.
tap to see the correct form
✅I will call you when I arrive. / I will call you if I arrive late.

💡 Memory Trick: The Conjunction Compass

BECAUSE = Back to Cause (Look backwards: Why?)
SO = So What Happens Next? (Look forward: Result!)
IF = Key to the Door (Unlocks the result only when condition happens)
WHEN = Clock on the Wall (Marks the exact time of action)

More examples
  • We like pizza and pasta.
  • He is tired, but he is still working.
  • I stayed home because I was ill.
  • She studied hard, so she passed the exam.
  • Call me when you arrive at the station.
  • If you need help, ask the teacher.
  • Although she was late, she didn't miss the bus.
