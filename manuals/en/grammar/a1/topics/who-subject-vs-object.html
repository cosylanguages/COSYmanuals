Who: Subject vs Object Questions

Who called you? vs Who did you call?

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can ask "Who...?" correctly whether you are asking about the person performing the action (subject) or receiving the action (object).

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these two questions with Who:

  • Who called you? (Someone called you. You are asking for the Subject ➔ No auxiliary 'do/did'!)
  • Who did you call? (You called someone. You are asking for the Object ➔ Auxiliary 'did' required!)

Score: 0 / 2
1. In "Who broke the window?", is 'Who' the person who performed the breaking (Subject)?
Correct · When Who replaces the Subject, the verb follows directly without do/does/did.
2. In "Who did Sarah meet yesterday?", who performed the meeting?
Correct · Sarah is the subject performing the action, so 'Who' asks for the Object of her meeting.
Question Type Target Structure Formula Example
Subject Question Who performed the action? Who + Main Verb + Object?
(No Do/Does/Did!)		 Who lives in this house?
Who wants ice cream?
Object Question Who received the action? Who + Auxiliary (do/does/did) + Subject + Verb? Who do you live with?
Who did you see at school?

Rule Summary:
• When Who IS the subject ➔ Who + verb directly (Who bought this car?)
• When Who IS NOT the subject ➔ Who + auxiliary + subject + verb (Who did you buy this car from?)

🧠 Think like English

In English, auxiliary verbs (do / does / did) are only needed to flip the subject and verb when the subject is already present in the sentence. If Who itself is the subject, there is nothing to flip!

⚠️ Common mistakes

💡 Memory Trick

The Test Answer Shortcut:
• If the answer is "HE / SHE" (Subject) ➔ Question uses No Auxiliary (Who called? ➔ He called!)
• If the answer is "HIM / HER" (Object) ➔ Question uses DO / DID (Who did you call? ➔ I called HIM!)
