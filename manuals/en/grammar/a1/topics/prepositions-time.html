Prepositions & Expressions of Time

In, on, at, ago, during, for & time markers

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can express exact times, days, dates, past moments (ago), future plans (in 2 days), durations (during/for), and routine time expressions.

👀 What do you notice?

Observe the prepositions and time markers in context:

  • I arrived two hours ago. (Past moment: number + ago)
  • We leave in two days. (Future moment: in + number + time)
  • I slept for 8 hours during the night. (Duration: for + quantity, during + event/noun)
  • I drink coffee in the morning, but I drank tea this morning.

Time Markers & Duration Expressions

MarkerMeaning & FormulaExample
AGOPast moment measured from present ([Time] + ago)I arrived three days ago.
IN (Future)Future moment from present (in + [Time])She leaves in ten minutes.
FOR vs DURINGFor + numbers/duration | During + noun eventfor 2 hours | during the concert
EVERY vs THISEvery = routine | This = today's periodin the morning / every morning vs this morning
THIS vs NEXTCurrent year/week vs Following year/weekthis year (2026) vs next year (2027)

Zoom in like a camera: wide shot (in) → medium shot (on) → close-up (at). Bigger period, bigger word first.

💡 Exceptions to remember

at night (not in the night) · the one time expression that breaks the 'in = part of day' rule.
at Christmas / Easter / the weekend · holiday periods use at, not in.
on time = punctual, exactly when planned. in time = before it's too late.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing Prepositions of Time, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.
