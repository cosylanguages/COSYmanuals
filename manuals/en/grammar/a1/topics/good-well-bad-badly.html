Good vs Well & Bad vs Badly

Adjectives vs Adverbs and Irregular Forms

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can distinguish between describing things/people (Good, Bad) and describing actions/performance (Well, Badly, Fast, Hard) without common beginner mix-ups.
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.
🎯 Practice this →

👀 What do you notice?

Notice how words describe either nouns (things/people) or verbs (actions):

  • She is a good singer. ➔ She sings well.
  • It was a bad game. ➔ They played badly.
  • He is a fast runner. ➔ He runs fast (not fastly!).

💡 Check your understanding

Score: 0 / 2
1. In the sentence "He speaks English well", what word does "well" modify?
Correct · 'Well' is an adverb that describes the manner of the action 'speaks'.
2. Can you say "He drives fastly"?
Correct · 'Fast', 'hard', and 'late' do not take -ly when functioning as adverbs.
Type Adjective (describes Noun) Adverb (describes Verb) Example Pair
Good / Well Good Well (Irregular!) Good cook ➔ Cooks well
Bad / Badly Bad Badly (-ly regular) Bad driver ➔ Drives badly
Fast / Fast Fast Fast (Irregular, no -ly) Fast car ➔ Drives fast
Hard / Hard Hard Hard (Irregular, no -ly) Hard job ➔ Works hard

Core Rule:
• Use Good / Bad with linking verbs or nouns: She is good. The movie was bad.
• Use Well / Badly with action verbs: She cooks well. He sings badly.
• Note on Health: "I feel well" refers to physical health; "I feel good" refers to emotional mood/state.

🧠 Think like English

Ask yourself: "Am I answering HOW someone does something, or WHAT kind of person/thing they are?"
If HOW ➔ use an adverb (well, badly, quickly, fast).
If WHAT KIND ➔ use an adjective (good, bad, quick, fast).
