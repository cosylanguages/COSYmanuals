Confusing Verbs, Questions & Negatives

Complete contrast guide for present forms, question auxiliaries, negatives, and verb types

🎯 Communicative Goal: Form accurate present tense sentences, ask natural questions, and express negative preferences without confusing auxiliary verbs.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these pairs:

  • "Is he a doctor?" (asking about a noun) vs. "Does he work here?" (asking about an action verb)
  • "Are you tired?" (adjective) vs. "Do you like pizza?" (preference verb)
  • "I don't know." (verb negative) vs. "I am not tired." (adjective negative)

1. Which verb carries the grammar in 'Does she work here?'
Correct · DO/DOES carries the third-person grammar marker, so the main verb remains base form!
2. Can stative verbs like 'know', 'love', and 'like' usually be used in -ing continuous forms?
Correct · State verbs (know, like, want, need, believe) describe permanent states, not ongoing activities.
3. What is the difference between 'I don't like fish' and 'I hate fish'?
Correct · 'Don't like' is a mild negative preference; 'hate' expresses strong dislike.

1. Present Simple vs Present Continuous (Points 45 & 46)

FeaturePresent SimplePresent Continuous
Core IdeaFacts, routines, habits, repeated actionsHappening around now, temporary situations
StructureSubject + Verb (s/es for he/she/it)am / is / are + Verb-ing
ExamplesI drink coffee every morning. She works at a bank.I am drinking coffee now. She is studying this month.
With ALWAYSNormal habit: He always arrives early.Annoyance or complaint: You are always losing your keys!

2. State Verbs vs Action Verbs (Point 47)

State Verbs: Describe thoughts, feelings, possession, and senses (e.g. know, like, love, want, need, believe, understand, remember, have [possession]). They do NOT normally take continuous -ing forms.

  • ✅ I know the answer. (NOT ❌ I am knowing the answer.)
  • ✅ I have a car. (Possession = state) vs. ✅ I am having lunch. (Action/Activity = continuous)
  • ✅ I think it is good. (Opinion = state) vs. ✅ I am thinking about my future. (Mental activity = continuous)

3. Questions: Do/Does vs Is/Are (Points 48, 49 & 50)

Question FormUsed WithExamples
DO / DOES + subject + verb Action verbs, routines, habits Do you play tennis? Does she live in Paris?
IS / ARE + subject + noun/adj/place/-ing Adjectives, nouns, places, continuous now Are you tired? Is he a doctor? Are you working now?
💡 Logic Trick: If the sentence has an action verb (work, play, live, like), use DO / DOES! If it has an adjective/noun/location, use AM / IS / ARE!

4. Negatives: Don't/Doesn't vs Not vs No (Points 51 & 52)

  • Don't / Doesn't + base verb: Used to negate action verbs. I don't know. She doesn't like coffee.
  • Not (am not / isn't / aren't): Used with adjectives, nouns, and auxiliaries. I am not tired. He is not a doctor.
  • No + Noun: Zero quantity of something. There is no water. I have no money.

Compare: There is no food. (= zero food) vs. The food is not good. (= food exists, but quality is bad).
