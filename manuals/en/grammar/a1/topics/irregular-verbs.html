💡 Why Grouping Verbs by Pattern Beats Alphabetical Memory

Organizing irregular verbs into pattern families (such as A-A-A invariant forms, A-B-B past ending shifts, A-B-C vowel series, and phonological rhyme families like sing/sang/sung) activates cognitive schema recognition. Rather than forcing your brain to store hundreds of isolated, arbitrary dictionary entries, pattern grouping taps into natural auditory rhyming and structural analogies—allowing you to recall conjugated forms instantly and generalize sound changes to new vocabulary.
