Possessive Adjectives: My, Your, His, Her…

Saying who something belongs to

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can say who something belongs to: my sister, her bag, their house.

👀 What do you notice?

Read the examples. What word comes before the noun every time? What changes when the owner changes from Tom to Anna?

Examples
  • I have a sister. My sister is a nurse.
  • You have a dog. Your dog is big.
  • Tom has a car. His car is red.
  • Anna has a bag. Her bag is blue.
  • We have a house. Our house is old.
  • They have a cat. Their cat is black.
  • The dog has a bone. Its bone is big.

Rule: Put my, your, his, her, its, our or their before a noun. Choose the word that matches the owner, not the thing owned. Do not add the: my car, not the my car.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In many languages the possessive changes with the noun it describes (Spanish mi / mis, French mon / ma / mes, Russian мой / моя / моё). English only looks at the owner: his brother, his sister, his parents all use the same his.

💡 Memory trick

its never has an apostrophe, just like his and her. It’s always means it is.
