Verb Patterns: Infinitive vs -ing

Connecting two verbs: Verb + to + Infinitive vs Verb + -ing

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can connect two verbs correctly in English (e.g. want to go, enjoy reading, decide to leave, stop smoking) without guessing prepositions.

👀 What do you notice?

Look at how the second verb changes depending on the first verb:

  • I want to study English. / She decided to buy a car.
  • We enjoy listening to music. / He stopped smoking.
  • They can swim. / You must wait. (Bare infinitive with modals!)

Pattern Group Trigger Verbs Formula & Example
Verb + TO + Infinitive want, need, decide, hope, plan, learn, agree, promise, try Verb + TO + Base Verb
I want to learn. He decided to leave.
Verb + -ING enjoy, finish, stop, avoid, keep (continue), mind, suggest Verb + Gerund (-ING)
She enjoys reading. They finished eating.
Modal + Bare Infinitive can, could, must, should, may, might Modal + Base Verb (No 'to')
I can drive. You must study.

Master Summary:
• Future / Decisions / Goals: want to, need to, hope to, plan to, decide to.
• Activities / Experience / Habits: enjoy -ing, finish -ing, stop -ing, keep -ing.
• Modals: can, must, should + base verb (NO 'to'!).

🧠 Think like English

In English, 'to' points forward like an arrow toward a goal or decision (decide ➔ to act). On the other hand, -ing looks at the experience of an action as it happens or ends (enjoy ➔ reading).

💡 Memory Trick

The 3-Group Rule:
🎯 Target / Goal ➔ TO + Verb (want to, need to)
🎉 Fun / Experience ➔ -ING (enjoy -ing, finish -ing)
⚡ Modal Superpower ➔ BARE VERB (can run, must go)
