Can / Can't

Ability, permission, possibility & modal contrasts

Observe how can differs from know how to, want, and must:

Contrasting Can with Other Verb Patterns

Verb PatternMeaningExample
Can + Base VerbGeneral ability, current possibility, or permissionI can play chess.
Know how to + VerbLearned skill / knowledge of a procedureI know how to fix bikes.
Want to + VerbDesire or intention (NOT ability)I want to learn French.
Must / Have to + VerbObligation or necessity (NOT choice)You must wear a seatbelt.

💡 Think like English

Modal verbs like can never change form (no cans, no canning) and never take 'to'. They express physical ability or permission directly before any verb.

⚠️ Common mistakes
❌She can to swim.

✅She can swim. (Never use 'to' after can!)
❌He cans swim.
tap to see the correct form
✅He can swim. (No -s for third person with modal verbs!)
❌I can not swim good.

✅I can't swim well.

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

In affirmative sentences, 'can' is usually unstressed /kən/ (I can SWIM). In negative sentences, 'can't' is stressed with a long vowel /kɑːnt/ or /kænt/.

More examples
  • She can speak three languages.
  • Can you help me, please?
  • We can't come to the party tonight.
  • I know how to ride a bicycle.
