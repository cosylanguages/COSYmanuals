Basic Word Order

Subject + Verb + Object

👁️ What do you notice?

Look at these two sentences: "She reads books" vs. "Books reads she". Why does only the first one make sense in English? Notice how word position fixes the meaning.

★ Why this matters

Word order is the single most important rule in English. Get this right, and a sentence with mistakes elsewhere is usually still understood; get it wrong, and native speakers may not follow you at all.

Rule: a basic English sentence follows the order Subject → Verb → Object. The subject does the action; the verb is the action; the object receives the action. This order does not usually change.

1. SUBJECT (Who performs the action?) She / I / They

🗣️ Pronunciation Note

Focus on natural sentence rhythm: emphasize the main content words when expressing Basic Word Order, while keeping grammatical function words unstressed.

More examples
  • I like pizza.
  • They play football.
  • My brother drives a car.
  • The children eat breakfast.

💭 Think like a native speaker

English lost most of its word endings centuries ago, so word order does the job those endings used to do. 'Dog bites man' and 'Man bites dog' use identical words but mean opposite things.
