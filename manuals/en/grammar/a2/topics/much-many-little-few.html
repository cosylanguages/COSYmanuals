Level A2 Grammar

Much, Many, Little, Few

Talk about quantity with uncountable and countable nouns.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use much and little with uncountable nouns, many and few with countable ones.
Examples in context
  • How much time do we have? Uncountable noun. — Much with time.
  • How many tickets did you buy? Countable noun. — Many with tickets.
  • There is little water left. Almost none, uncountable. — Little is negative in tone.
  • Few people came to the meeting. Almost none, countable. — Few is negative in tone.

👀 What do you observe?

Much and little go with uncountable nouns such as time, money and water; many and few go with countable plurals such as tickets and people. Little and few without a mean not enough; a little and a few mean some. Much and many are common in questions and negatives, and sound formal in positive sentences, where a lot of is usual.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
much + uncountable questions and negatives how much time
many + countable plural questions and negatives how many tickets
little + uncountable not enough little water
a little + uncountable some a little water
few + countable not enough few people
a few + countable some a few people

Formula / rule: Check whether the noun can be counted, then choose much or many. Add a when you mean some rather than not enough. In positive sentences prefer a lot of or plenty of with both types.

★ Why it matters

The countable and uncountable split runs through English quantity words, and using much with a plural or many with an uncountable noun is the error listeners notice first. Little and few also change the meaning of the whole sentence with one letter.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use a lot of in almost every positive sentence and keep much and many for questions and negatives. Quite a few means a surprising number, not a small one.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Much and many are stressed in questions: how MUCH TIME. Little and few are stressed when the shortage is the point.

Additional example bank
  • Countable: We have a few questions about the plan.
  • Uncountable: There is a little milk in the fridge.
  • Formal positive: Much of the report is still missing.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: How much people came (much with a countable noun)

✅ Right: How many people came

❌ Wrong: There are few water left (few with an uncountable noun)

✅ Right: There is little water left
