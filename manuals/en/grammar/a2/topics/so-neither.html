So and Neither

Agree with someone in one short sentence.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can agree with a positive statement using so and a negative one using neither.
Examples in context
  • I like coffee, and so does she. Positive, so plus the auxiliary. — So does she = she likes it too.
  • I don't like tea, and neither do I. Negative, neither plus the auxiliary. — Neither do I = I don't either.
  • She can't come, and neither can he. Modal negative. — Neither can he.

👀 What do you observe?

So and neither agree with the previous speaker by repeating the auxiliary and inverting the subject. A positive statement takes so: so do I, so has she, so can we. A negative statement takes neither (or nor in formal writing): neither do I, neither has she. The auxiliary must match the original verb, and the tense stays the same.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
so + auxiliary + subject positive agreement so does she
neither + auxiliary + subject negative agreement neither do I
auxiliary repeats the original be, have, do or a modal so have we, neither can he
tense stays the same no change of tense she has signed — so have we
nor formal variant of neither nor has she
me too / me neither informal equivalents me too, me neither

Formula / rule: Look at the statement you are agreeing with. Positive: so plus the same auxiliary plus the subject. Negative: neither plus the same auxiliary plus the subject. Keep the auxiliary and the tense, and invert the subject.

★ Why it matters

The pair is the shortest way to agree in English, and mixing them produces the opposite meaning: neither do I after a positive statement contradicts the speaker instead of agreeing. That is why the choice of so or neither is the whole point of the structure.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers replace the whole thing with me too and me neither in informal speech, and use so do I mainly in writing or when the auxiliary is contrastive: so DOES she.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The auxiliary is stressed when it corrects an assumption: so DOES she. Otherwise so and neither are unstressed and the subject carries the stress.

Additional example bank
  • Positive: We have finished, and so have they.
  • Negative: He won't sign, and neither will the chair.
  • Formal: The panel has not reported, nor has the board.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I like coffee, and so do I (so with the wrong subject for agreement)

✅ Right: I like coffee, and so does she

❌ Wrong: I don't like tea, and so don't I (so used with a negative statement)

✅ Right: I don't like tea, and neither do I
