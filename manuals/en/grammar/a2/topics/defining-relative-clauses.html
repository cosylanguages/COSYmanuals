Defining Relative Clauses

Identify which person or thing you mean.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can identify a noun with who, which, that and where, without commas.
Examples in context
  • The man who called you is my uncle. Who for people. — The clause identifies the man.
  • This is the book that changed my mind. That for things, informal. — Which also works here.
  • The town where I grew up has changed. Where for places. — Where replaces in which.

👀 What do you observe?

A defining relative clause tells the listener which person or thing you mean, so it has no commas and the sentence does not work without it. Use who for people, which for things, that for either in informal English, where for places and whose for possession. The relative pronoun can be left out when it is the object: the book I bought.

Formula / rule: Use a defining clause to identify. No commas, who for people, which for things, where for places, and drop the pronoun when it is the object of the clause.

★ Why it matters

Without the clause the sentence points at nothing in particular, and with commas it becomes extra information about something already known. That difference decides whether your listener knows which man you mean.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers drop the pronoun whenever they can: the book I bought is far more common in speech than the book that I bought, and whom is rare outside formal writing.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The clause runs on without a pause: the MAN who CALLED you is my UNcle. A pause signals a non-defining clause, which is a different meaning.

Additional example bank
  • People: The woman who runs the café knows everyone.
  • Things: I found the key that fits this door.
  • Places: That is the street where we met.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The man which called you is my uncle (which for a person)

✅ Right: The man who called you is my uncle

❌ Wrong: The book, that changed my mind, was a gift (commas in a defining clause)

✅ Right: The book that changed my mind was a gift
