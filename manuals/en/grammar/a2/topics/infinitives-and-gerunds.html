Level A2 Grammar

Infinitives and Gerunds

Choose between to + verb and the -ing form.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the infinitive after verbs of intention and the gerund after verbs of liking, avoiding and finishing.
Examples in context
  • I want to leave early. Intention, plan or aim. — Want takes the infinitive.
  • I enjoy working here. Enjoyment of an activity. — Enjoy takes the gerund.
  • They finished writing the report. Completion of an activity. — Finish takes the gerund.

👀 What do you observe?

Some verbs take the infinitive, some the gerund, and the pattern belongs to the verb. Want, need, decide, hope, plan, promise, refuse and agree take to plus the verb. Enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep and practise take the -ing form. A few take both with a change of meaning, and after a preposition the -ing form always follows.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
verb + to + base want, decide, hope, plan, promise I want to leave early
verb + -ing enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest I enjoy working here
verb + object + to + base ask, tell, want, expect they asked us to wait
after a preposition always the -ing form good at writing
both, same meaning begin, start, continue it started to rain / raining
both, different meaning stop, remember, try stop smoking / stop to smoke

Formula / rule: Learn the pattern with the verb, the way you learn the preposition with an adjective. If a preposition comes before the verb, use the -ing form. If the verb expresses intention or a decision, use to plus the verb.

★ Why it matters

The pattern decides who does the action and when. Remember to lock the door and remember locking the door describe different memories, and stop to rest and stop resting describe different events, which is why the choice matters more than a style preference.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the gerund after prepositions without thinking and switch to the infinitive with want and need. Where both are possible, the -ing form often sounds more habitual and the infinitive more specific.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The infinitive is unstressed (to /tə/) and the gerund carries the stress on its stem: I enjoy WORKing here.

Additional example bank
  • Intention: We plan to publish in June.
  • Activity: She avoids answering direct questions.
  • After a preposition: He is good at negotiating.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I enjoy to work here (infinitive after enjoy)

✅ Right: I enjoy working here

❌ Wrong: She decided applying (gerund after decide)

✅ Right: She decided to apply
