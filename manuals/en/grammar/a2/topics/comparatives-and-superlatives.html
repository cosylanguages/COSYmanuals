Comparatives and Superlatives

Compare two things, or one against all.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can compare two things with -er or more and rank one against the group with -est or the most.
Examples in context
  • This room is bigger than mine. Two things compared. — Than follows the comparative.
  • She is the most patient person I know. One against the group. — The most with a long adjective.
  • It was the best film of the year. An irregular superlative. — Good becomes best.

👀 What do you observe?

Short adjectives take -er and -est, longer ones take more and the most. The comparative takes than, the superlative takes the and usually of or in for the group. One-syllable adjectives ending in a consonant double it: big, bigger. Adjectives ending in y change to i: happy, happier. Good, bad and far are irregular: better, best, worse, worst, further, furthest.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
-er + than short adjectives, two things bigger than mine
more + adjective + than long adjectives, two things more expensive than mine
the -est short adjectives, the group the biggest room in the flat
the most + adjective long adjectives, the group the most patient person
irregular good, bad, far better, worse, further
double the consonant big, hot, thin bigger, hotter, thinner

Formula / rule: Choose the comparative for two things and the superlative for one thing against the group. Short adjectives take -er and -est, long ones more and the most, and the irregulars are learnt as pairs.

★ Why it matters

It is the everyday tool of opinion and choice, from which flat to take to which candidate to hire. The mistake that damages meaning most is a double comparative, more bigger, because it marks the speaker as unsure of the pattern.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers often use the superlative where grammar books expect the comparative: of the two, the cheaper one. Both are heard; the superlative is the safer choice in writing.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The comparative ending takes the stress: BIGger, and than is weak. In the superlative the stress stays on the adjective: the MOST PAtient.

Additional example bank
  • Two things: This option is cheaper than that one.
  • The group: It is the cheapest option available.
  • Irregular: The weather is worse than yesterday.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: This room is more bigger than mine (double comparative)

✅ Right: This room is bigger than mine

❌ Wrong: She is the most tallest in the class (double superlative)

✅ Right: She is the tallest in the class
