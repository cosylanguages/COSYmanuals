Too and Enough

Say something is more than you want, or as much as you need.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use too plus adjective and adjective plus enough, with to plus verb.
Examples in context
  • This coffee is too hot to drink. More than is wanted. — Too before the adjective.
  • The flat isn't big enough for four people. Less than is needed. — Enough after the adjective.
  • He is old enough to drive. The required degree. — Enough after the adjective, then to plus verb.

👀 What do you observe?

Too means more than is wanted or possible: too hot, too late, too expensive. Enough means as much as is needed, and it comes after an adjective or adverb but before a noun: big enough, quickly enough, enough money. Both are usually followed by to plus the base verb. With nouns use too much for uncountable and too many for countable.

too much + uncountable excess too much sugar

too ... to / enough to followed by the base verb too late to call, early enough to help

Formula / rule: Use too for excess, before the adjective, and enough for sufficiency, after the adjective but before a noun. Finish both with to plus the base verb when you name the action.

★ Why it matters

The pair carries judgement about quantity and suitability, so the position of enough, before or after the word, decides whether the sentence is about having what you need or lacking it.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers negative enough for the opposite of too: not warm enough for too cold. The combination too ... to is fixed, and too much, too many are used as pronouns too: that's too much.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Too and enough carry the stress when they are the point: it's TOO hot, it's not big eNOUGH.

Additional example bank
  • Excess: The case was too heavy to lift.
  • Sufficiency: The room was warm enough to work in.
  • Quantity: We have enough chairs for everyone.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The flat isn't enough big for four people (enough before the adjective)

✅ Right: The flat isn't big enough for four people

❌ Wrong: There were too much people (much with a countable noun)

✅ Right: There were too many people
