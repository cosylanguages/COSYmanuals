Level A2 Grammar

As ... as

Compare two things that are equal.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can say that two things are equal with as plus adjective plus as.
Examples in context
  • She is as tall as her brother. Equality between two people. — As closes the comparison.
  • This flat isn't as big as the old one. Inequality, the negative form. — Not as big as = smaller.
  • The film was twice as long as we expected. A multiplier before the pattern. — Twice as long as.

👀 What do you observe?

The pattern as plus adjective plus as states that two things are equal on that quality. The negative not as ... as means less, and it is the polite way to compare without using a comparative. A multiplier or fraction goes before the first as: twice as long as, half as expensive as. Between the two as phrases you put the adjective, never the adverb of degree very.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
as + adjective + as equality as tall as her brother
not as + adjective + as less than not as big as the old one
twice / half / three times multiplier before the first as twice as long as
as much as / as many as quantity as many as ten people
as ... as possible a maximum come as soon as possible
the same as identity, no adjective the same colour as mine

Formula / rule: Use as plus adjective plus as for equality and not as plus adjective plus as for less. Put multipliers and fractions before the first as, and use as much or as many when you count instead of describing.

★ Why it matters

It gives you a comparison without picking a winner, which is why it carries most of the neutral and polite comparisons: this flat isn't as big as the old one says the flat is smaller without calling it small.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use not as ... as far more than less ... than in speech, because it is softer. The pattern also survives in fixed phrases: as soon as possible, as far as I know.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Both as words are unstressed and the adjective carries the beat: she's as TALL as her BROther.

Additional example bank
  • Equal: My phone is as fast as yours.
  • Not equal: The journey wasn't as easy as it looked.
  • Quantity: We have as many as forty applicants.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: She is as tall than her brother (than after as)

✅ Right: She is as tall as her brother

❌ Wrong: It is not as bigger as the old one (comparative inside the pattern)

✅ Right: It is not as big as the old one
