Level A2 Grammar

Prepositions of Movement

Say where somebody moves to.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can describe movement with to, into, onto, through, across, along, over and out of.
Examples in context
  • She walked into the room. Movement to the inside. — In becomes into when it moves.
  • We drove through the tunnel. Movement from one end to the other. — Through, not across.
  • He ran across the road. From one side to the other. — Across for surfaces crossed.
  • They climbed over the wall. Movement above an obstacle. — Over for something in the way.

👀 What do you observe?

Movement takes its own prepositions. To for a destination, into for entering a space, onto for getting on a surface, out of for leaving, through for a tunnel or an opening, across for going from one side to the other, along for following a line, over for passing above and past for going by. In, on and at describe position, not movement.

to destination we drove to the airport
into entering a space she walked into the room
onto getting on a surface he stepped onto the platform
out of leaving a space they came out of the building
through from end to end we drove through the tunnel
across / along / over crossing, following, passing above across the road, along the street, over the wall

Formula / rule: Ask what the movement does to the space: enters it (into), leaves it (out of), crosses it (through, across), follows it (along) or passes above it (over). Position prepositions in, on and at do not move.

★ Why it matters

These prepositions carry the geometry of the sentence, and choosing the wrong one changes the picture: across the road is a crossing, along the road is following it.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use get in and get out of for cars and get on and get off for trains, buses and planes, which is a fixed usage rather than a rule about movement.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The preposition is unstressed and the place carries the stress: she walked into the ROOM.

Additional example bank
  • Entering: He came into the office at nine.
  • Crossing: Swim across the lake, not along it.
  • Leaving: She ran out of the classroom.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: She walked in the room (position instead of movement)

✅ Right: She walked into the room

❌ Wrong: We drove across the tunnel (crossing instead of through)

✅ Right: We drove through the tunnel
