Level A2 Grammar

Indefinite Pronouns

Talk about people and things without naming them.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use some, any, no and every with -one, -body, -thing and -where.
Examples in context
  • Someone left a bag on the table. An unnamed person, positive sentence. — One person, unknown.
  • I didn't see anybody at the door. Nobody, negative sentence. — Any- after a negative.
  • Nothing was open that early. No thing at all. — No- makes the verb positive.

👀 What do you observe?

Some- is used in positive sentences and in offers and requests, any- in negatives and questions, no- for a negative meaning with a positive verb, and every- for all of a group. All of them are singular: everybody is here, nothing was open. Adjectives go after them: something strange, nowhere quiet.

Formula / rule: Decide the sentence type first, then the ending: -one and -body for people, -thing for things, -where for places. Keep the verb singular, and put the adjective after the pronoun.

★ Why it matters

They let you talk about people and things you cannot or do not want to name, which is most of everyday reference. The rule that decides between some- and any- is the sentence type, not politeness.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use somebody and someone interchangeably, with -one slightly more formal. Nobody and nothing with a positive verb is the normal negative; not nobody is a double negative and non-standard.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The pronoun is stressed and the ending carries a secondary beat: SOMEthing STRANGE. In fast speech -thing becomes /θɪŋ/ with no vowel.

Additional example bank
  • Positive: Somebody called while you were out.
  • Negative: I didn't find anything useful.
  • Question: Is there anywhere to eat nearby?
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I didn't see nobody (double negative)

✅ Right: I didn't see anybody

❌ Wrong: Everybody are ready (plural verb)

✅ Right: Everybody is ready
