Past Participles 1

Build the third form of common verbs.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can recognise and use the past participle of regular verbs and the first group of irregulars.
Examples in context
  • I have finished the report. Regular participle, same as the past simple. — Finish, finished, finished.
  • She has worked here for years. Regular participle after has. — Work, worked, worked.
  • They have asked for more time. Regular participle in the perfect. — Ask, asked, asked.

👀 What do you observe?

The past participle is the third form of the verb. Regular verbs add -ed, so the past simple and the participle are the same: worked, finished, opened. Irregular verbs have their own third form, which you learn with the other two: be, was, been; go, went, gone; see, saw, seen; take, took, taken; give, gave, given; write, wrote, written. The participle follows have to make the perfect tenses and be to make the passive.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
-ed regular participle worked, finished, opened
same as past simple regular verbs only asked, asked
be, was, been irregular I have been here
go, went, gone irregular she has gone
see, saw, seen irregular we have seen it
write, wrote, written irregular the letter was written

Formula / rule: Learn each verb as three forms, not two. Regular verbs take -ed for both the past simple and the participle; irregular verbs need the third form for have and for be plus the participle.

★ Why it matters

Everything in the perfect tenses and the passive depends on this form, so a missing third form blocks half the grammar of the level. Regular verbs are safe, irregulars need the list.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers simplify some irregulars in casual speech, but in the perfect tenses they keep the standard form: I have gone, not I have went.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The participle is stressed in the perfect: I have FINished. With have contracted, I've FINished, the participle carries the whole beat.

Additional example bank
  • Perfect: We have seen that film twice.
  • Passive: The letter was written in English.
  • Adjective use: a broken window, a finished report
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I have went there (past simple instead of participle)

✅ Right: I have gone there

❌ Wrong: The letter was write yesterday (base form instead of participle)

✅ Right: The letter was written yesterday
