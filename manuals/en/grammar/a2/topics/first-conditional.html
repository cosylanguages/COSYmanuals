Level A2 Grammar

First Conditional

Talk about a real future possibility.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can predict a real future result with if plus the present simple and will in the other half.
Examples in context
  • If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home. A real possibility. — Will carries the result.
  • If you finish early, you can leave. Permission in the result. — A modal can replace will.
  • If they don't hurry, they will miss the train. A negative condition. — Present simple after if.

👀 What do you observe?

The first conditional presents a real or likely future situation and its result. The if half takes the present simple, never will, and the result half takes will plus the base verb or another modal such as can, may or should. The order of the halves is free, and the comma goes only with the if half first.

Formula / rule: Use the first conditional for a future situation that is real or likely. Present simple after if, will or a modal in the result, and never will in the if half.

★ Why it matters

It is the workhorse of plans, warnings and promises, so it carries most of the practical future talk in English. Will in the if half is the mistake that marks a learner fastest, because it turns the condition into a prediction.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers soften the result with might or could when the outcome is uncertain, and use going to when the result is already decided: if she agrees, we are going to sign.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The if half is one unit and falls fast: if it RAINS toMORrow, we'll STAY at HOME. Contractions are normal in speech, we'll and they'll.

Additional example bank
  • Warning: If you press that, the alarm will sound.
  • Promise: If you help me now, I will help you later.
  • Plan: We will leave at six if the traffic is clear.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: If it will rain, we will stay at home (will in the if half)

✅ Right: If it rains, we will stay at home

❌ Wrong: If you will finish early, you can leave

✅ Right: If you finish early, you can leave
