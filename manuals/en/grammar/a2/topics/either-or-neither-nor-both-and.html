Level A2 Grammar

Either, Neither, Both and And

Choose between two, or take all two.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can offer a choice with either ... or, exclude two with neither ... nor and join two with both ... and.
Examples in context
  • You can have either tea or coffee. A choice between two. — One of the two.
  • Neither the food nor the service was good. Both excluded. — Nor after neither.
  • Both my sisters and my brother live abroad. Both included. — And after both.

👀 What do you observe?

Either pairs with or to offer a choice of two, neither with nor to exclude both, and both with and to include both. Either and neither are singular in careful writing, so neither of them is here and neither answer is right. Not either works as a less formal either in negatives.

either of + plural one of two, singular verb either of the answers is fine
neither of + plural none of two, singular verb neither of them is here
not ... either informal negative I don't like tea or coffee either

Formula / rule: Decide first how many you mean: one of two takes either ... or, none of two takes neither ... nor, both of two takes both ... and. Keep the second word of each pair matched to the first.

★ Why it matters

The pairs are the shortest way to be precise about two things, and mixing them, neither ... or, is heard often enough to be worth avoiding in writing because it blurs whether you exclude one or both.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers say me neither and me either, and use neither do I for agreement. In fast speech either often becomes /ˈaɪðə/ in British English and /ˈiːðər/ in American.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Either and neither carry the stress when they are the point: EIther of them. In the pairs, the nouns after or, nor and and carry it.

Additional example bank
  • Choice: We can meet either Monday or Tuesday.
  • Exclusion: Neither of the options is cheap.
  • Inclusion: Both the manager and the staff agreed.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Neither the food or the service was good (or after neither)

✅ Right: Neither the food nor the service was good

❌ Wrong: Both my sisters or my brother live abroad (or after both)

✅ Right: Both my sisters and my brother live abroad
