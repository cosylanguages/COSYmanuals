Past Participles 2

Handle the irregular forms that catch learners out.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the third form of the irregular verbs that differ most from the past simple.
Examples in context
  • I have brought the papers. Bring, brought, brought. — The same for past and participle.
  • She has bought a new car. Buy, bought, bought. — Same pattern as bring.
  • They have chosen the date. Choose, chose, chosen. — Long vowel in the participle.
  • We have drunk all the coffee. Drink, drank, drunk. — Three different vowels.

👀 What do you observe?

Irregular participles fall into families, and learning them in families is faster than one by one. Bring, bought and buy, bought keep the same form for past and participle. Choose, chose, chosen and speak, spoke, spoken change the vowel. Drink, drank, drunk and begin, began, begun use three vowels. Verbs such as put, cut and hit do not change at all.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
bring, brought, brought same past and participle I have brought the papers
buy, bought, bought same pattern as bring she has bought a car
choose, chose, chosen vowel change they have chosen the date
speak, spoke, spoken vowel change he has spoken to her
drink, drank, drunk three forms we have drunk the coffee
put, cut, hit no change I have put it there

Formula / rule: Group the irregulars by shape: same past and participle, one vowel change, three vowels, and no change at all. Then use the third form after have and be.

★ Why it matters

The irregular participles are where the perfect tenses break down in writing, and the commonest forms are also the ones used constantly, so the list pays back immediately.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers regularise rare verbs in speech, but the frequent ones stay standard. Learners should learn drunk, not dranked, and chosen, not choosed.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The participle takes the stress in the perfect: she has BOUGHT a car. With have contracted, the participle is the only stressed word.

Additional example bank
  • Same form: He has brought his own lunch.
  • Vowel change: They have spoken to the manager.
  • No change: She has put the file on the desk.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They have choosed the date (regularised irregular)

✅ Right: They have chosen the date

❌ Wrong: We have drank all the coffee (past simple instead of participle)

✅ Right: We have drunk all the coffee
