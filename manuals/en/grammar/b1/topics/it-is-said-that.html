It Is Said That

Report general opinion without naming the source.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use it is said that and the passive reporting patterns.
Examples in context
  • It is said that the merger will close. Opinion without a source. — It + passive + that.
  • The company is said to be restructuring. Subject + passive + to-infinitive. — Same meaning, tighter.
  • He is believed to have left. Earlier action with a perfect infinitive. — To have + participle.

👀 What do you observe?

These patterns report what people generally say without naming them. Two forms are equivalent: it is said that the merger will close, and the merger is said to be closing. Verbs used this way include say, think, believe, report, consider, expect and understand. When the reported action is earlier than the reporting, the infinitive is perfect: he is believed to have left.

it is said that + clause impersonal framing it is said that the merger will close
subject + is said to + base same meaning, subject first the company is said to be restructuring
to have + participle earlier action he is believed to have left
it is thought / believed / reported other reporting verbs it is thought that the delay was avoidable
it is understood that formal, in minutes it is understood that the terms still hold
there is said to be existence framing there is said to be a second file

Formula / rule: Choose the framing: impersonal (it is said that) when the source is irrelevant or unknown, or subject-first (the subject is said to) when the topic matters. Then set the infinitive: to do for simultaneous, to have done for earlier.

★ Why it matters

This is the register of journalism and of official prose that must not attribute: it is believed that reports a consensus without a witness. It also protects the writer, which is why it appears in reports where a claim cannot be sourced.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use these patterns chiefly in news and formal writing, and they say people say or apparently in speech. Is said to is also used to distance a rumour politely.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The reporting verb is unstressed and the infinitive carries the stress: the company is said to be reSTRUCturing.

Additional example bank
  • News: The firm is reported to be seeking a buyer.
  • Rumour: He is understood to have resigned.
  • Existence: There is said to be a second version.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The company is said restructuring (missing to be)

✅ Right: The company is said to be restructuring

❌ Wrong: He is believed to left the country (base verb where a perfect infinitive is needed)

✅ Right: He is believed to have left the country
