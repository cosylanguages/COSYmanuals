Present Perfect Simple vs Continuous

Choose between the result and the activity.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can tell a finished result from an activity that has been going on.
Examples in context
  • I have sent the file. Completed action, result now. — Simple: the job is done.
  • I have been sending reminders all week. Repeated activity up to now. — Continuous: the activity continues.
  • She has written three reports. Completed, counted. — Simple with a quantity.

👀 What do you observe?

The present perfect simple reports a completed action with a present result, and it is the form used with quantities: I have sent it, she has written three reports. The present perfect continuous reports an activity that has been going on up to now, often without a result: I have been sending reminders, she has been writing since eight. With for and since, both are possible, and the continuous emphasises duration.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
have + participle completed action, result I have sent the file
have been + -ing activity continuing to now I have been sending reminders
simple + quantity counted results she has written three reports
continuous + duration how long the activity lasted she has been writing since eight
simple with stative verbs no continuous form I have known her for years
both with for / since simple for result, continuous for duration I have worked here since 2019 / I have been working here since 2019

Formula / rule: Ask whether the sentence reports a result or an activity. A result, especially with a number, takes the simple form. An activity that is still going, especially with a duration, takes the continuous. Stative verbs stay in the simple form.

★ Why it matters

The pair carries a claim about completion: I have written the report tells the reader it exists, I have been writing it tells them it is not finished. In a work context that difference is the point of the sentence, which is why the choice is worth getting right.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the continuous for complaints and explanations (I have been waiting for an hour) and the simple for announcements (I have finished). In American English the simple is used where British English would use the continuous with just and already.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The auxiliary contracts (I've sent, I've been sending) and the stress falls on the participle or on the duration phrase: I've been WAITing for an HOUR.

Additional example bank
  • Result: We have published the figures.
  • Activity: We have been reviewing the figures since Monday.
  • Quantity: They have opened four new offices this year.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I have been sending the file yesterday (continuous for a completed action with a past time)

✅ Right: I have sent the file

❌ Wrong: She has been writing three reports (continuous with a quantity)

✅ Right: She has written three reports
