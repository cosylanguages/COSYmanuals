Embedded and Indirect Questions

Put a question inside a sentence without inverting it.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can embed a question inside another clause with correct word order.
Examples in context
  • Where is the office? → Could you tell me where the office is? Inversion disappears. — Subject before verb.
  • Did the file arrive? → I wonder if the file arrived. Yes/no takes if. — No auxiliary in the clause.
  • When does the panel meet? → Do you know when the panel meets? Do disappears. — The verb takes its normal ending.

👀 What do you observe?

When a question is embedded in another clause, the word order becomes statement order: subject before verb, and no do or did. The frame can be a polite opener (could you tell me, do you know, I wonder), a reporting verb (she asked, he wanted to know), or a statement (the question is). Yes/no questions take if or whether, and the auxiliary moves into the tense rather than vanishing.

tense shift in reporting backshift after a past verb she asked what the deadline was

Formula / rule: Build the question first, then strip the inversion: move the subject before the verb, remove do or did and put the tense on the main verb, and add if for a yes/no question. Keep the question mark only when the frame itself is a question.

★ Why it matters

Indirect questions are the grammar of politeness and of reported enquiry. Where is the office? is a demand for information; could you tell me where the office is? is a request, and the difference matters in every email a learner writes. The same structure is what makes minutes report questions without quoting them.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use indirect questions constantly as politeness, and they often keep the inversion in very informal speech (could you tell me where's the office?), which is common but not standard written English.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The frame carries a rising tone and the embedded clause falls: could you TELL me / where the OFFice is.

Additional example bank
  • Polite: Do you know whether the deadline has moved?
  • Reported: He asked when the audit would close.
  • Statement: The question is whether the file was ever sent.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Could you tell me where is the office (inversion kept in an embedded question)

✅ Right: Could you tell me where the office is

❌ Wrong: I wonder if did the file arrive (do kept inside the clause)

✅ Right: I wonder if the file arrived
