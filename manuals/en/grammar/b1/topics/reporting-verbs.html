Reporting Verbs

Report what was said with a verb that carries the attitude.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use reporting verbs with their own patterns, from admit and deny to warn and insist.
Examples in context
  • She admitted taking the file. Verb + -ing. — Admit, deny, suggest, recommend.
  • He warned us not to sign. Verb + object + to-infinitive. — Warn, persuade, remind, urge.
  • The chair insisted on a written reply. Verb + preposition + noun or -ing. — Insist on, apologise for, object to.

👀 What do you observe?

Reporting verbs carry the speaker's attitude and each has its own pattern. Admit, deny, suggest, recommend and regret take the -ing form. Agree, refuse, offer, promise and threaten take the to-infinitive. Warn, persuade, remind and urge take an object plus the to-infinitive. Insist on, object to, apologise for and accuse of take a preposition. Many also accept a that-clause, which is the safest option when the pattern is uncertain.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
verb + -ing admit, deny, suggest, recommend she admitted taking the file
verb + to-infinitive agree, refuse, offer, promise they refused to sign
verb + object + to-infinitive warn, persuade, remind, urge he warned us not to sign
verb + preposition + -ing insist on, object to, apologise for insisted on a written reply
verb + that-clause safe option for most verbs she admitted that she had taken the file
verb + object + of accuse, suspect they accused him of leaking the file

Formula / rule: Choose the verb for the attitude first, then follow its pattern. If you are unsure of the pattern, use the that-clause, which nearly all reporting verbs accept. Keep the negative before the infinitive: warned us not to sign.

★ Why it matters

Reporting verbs are where a summary becomes an interpretation. Said is neutral; admitted, claimed, conceded and insisted each carry a judgement about the speaker's credibility, which is why they are the vocabulary of journalism and minutes.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use said and told for most reporting and reach for the attitude verbs in writing, where the choice of verb is doing the analysis. Claim in particular implies doubt, which is why lawyers avoid it for their own clients.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The reporting verb is usually unstressed before its clause, and the attitude verbs carry stress when the judgement is the point: she ADmitted it.

Additional example bank
  • Admission: The supplier admitted shipping the wrong batch.
  • Denial: The board denied authorising the payment.
  • Warning: The auditors warned the team against early sign-off.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They denied to have seen it (infinitive after deny)

✅ Right: They denied having seen it

❌ Wrong: He warned us to not sign (split negative in the wrong place)

✅ Right: He warned us not to sign
