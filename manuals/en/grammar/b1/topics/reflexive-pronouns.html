Level B1 Grammar

Reflexive Pronouns

Use myself, yourself and themselves where they belong.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use reflexive pronouns for a shared subject and for emphasis.
Examples in context
  • She taught herself to negotiate. Same person acts and receives. — Reflexive object.
  • The board itself approved the change. Emphasis on the subject. — Emphatic reflexive.
  • The system restarted by itself. Fixed phrase: alone, unaided. — By + reflexive.

👀 What do you observe?

A reflexive pronoun is used when the subject and the object are the same: she taught herself, they blamed themselves. It also marks emphasis, placed after the noun or at the end of the clause: the board itself approved it, the board approved it itself. Fixed phrases include by myself (alone), in itself (considered alone) and help yourself (serve yourself). It is not used after verbs that take no object.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
reflexive object subject and object are the same she taught herself to negotiate
emphatic reflexive emphasis, not a new object the board itself approved the change
by + reflexive alone, unaided the system restarted by itself
in + reflexive considered alone the delay is not a problem in itself
help / behave yourself fixed imperative phrases help yourself to the file
not after intransitive verbs no object to reflect he sat down, not he sat himself down

Formula / rule: Ask whether the object is the same person or thing as the subject. If yes, use a reflexive; if the sentence only needs emphasis, place the reflexive after the noun. Then check the verb: verbs with no object take no reflexive.

★ Why it matters

Reflexives carry responsibility. The departments blamed themselves and the departments blamed the consultants are different findings in a review, and the reflexive is what makes the difference visible in one word.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use reflexives less than learners expect, especially after verbs of movement and rest: sit down, wake up, feel better. Overusing them is one of the clearest signs of translated English.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The -self part is stressed when the emphasis matters: the board itSELF approved it.

Additional example bank
  • Shared subject: The team organised itself into three groups.
  • Emphasis: The chair herself signed the minutes.
  • Fixed phrase: The decision is defensible in itself.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: He sat himself down at the table (reflexive with an intransitive verb)

✅ Right: He sat down at the table

❌ Wrong: The board approved itself the change (reflexive where emphasis is meant)

✅ Right: The board itself approved the change
