Adjective + Preposition

Keep the preposition the adjective requires.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the fixed preposition after common adjectives.
Examples in context
  • interested in about a subject or offer — we are interested in the offer
  • satisfied with about a result — they were satisfied with the result
  • responsible for about a duty — she is responsible for the file

👀 What do you observe?

Adjectives take a preposition by convention, and the pair must be learned together: interested in, good at, afraid of, responsible for, satisfied with, similar to, capable of, aware of, dependent on, married to. The same preposition can be right with one adjective and wrong with its near neighbour, so the pair is the unit.

Formula / rule: Learn the adjective with its preposition, then check the pattern in a sentence. Where the pair can vary (responsible for a task, responsible to a manager), the meaning decides and both are correct.

★ Why it matters

Adjective-preposition pairs are the clearest sign of a text written by translation: the meaning survives, the preposition does not. Since the pairs are arbitrary and frequent, a wrong one appears in almost every paragraph a learner writes.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers vary in a few pairs (different from, different to, different than), but the rest are fixed. In exams, follow the variety you were taught and stay with it.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The preposition is unstressed and attaches to the adjective: INterested in, saTISfied with, reSPONsible for.

Additional example bank
  • Ability: She is capable of running the audit alone.
  • Awareness: The team was aware of the risk.
  • Similarity: The terms are similar to last year's.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: interested on the offer (preposition from another adjective)

✅ Right: interested in the offer

❌ Wrong: responsible of the file (wrong preposition)

✅ Right: responsible for the file
