Future Continuous

Say what will be happening at a future moment.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use will be + -ing for an action in progress at a future time.
Examples in context
  • At ten, we will be presenting. In progress at ten. — Will be + -ing.
  • This time tomorrow, I will be travelling. In progress at that moment. — Time phrase fixes the point.
  • Will you be using the room at three? Polite enquiry about a plan. — Question form.

👀 What do you observe?

The future continuous describes an action that will be in progress at a future moment. It is built with will be plus the -ing form, and it usually comes with a time expression (at ten, this time tomorrow, at three). It also softens an enquiry about someone's plans, and it presents a future event as a matter of course rather than as a decision.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
will be + -ing in progress at a future point at ten, we will be presenting
time phrase fixes the point this time tomorrow
negative won't be + -ing they won't be working on Friday
question will + subject + be + -ing will you be using the room?
polite enquiry softens the question will you be joining us?
contrast with future perfect in progress versus completed will be finishing / will have finished

Formula / rule: Fix the future moment first, then decide whether the action will be in progress or completed. In progress: will be plus -ing. Completed: will have plus the participle. Use the continuous for polite enquiries about plans, which asks what someone will be doing rather than what they have decided.

★ Why it matters

The future continuous is how English talks about a moment rather than an event, which is what most schedules need: at ten we will be presenting says where to find us, while we will present at ten commits the team to starting then. It is also the politest way to ask about someone's availability.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use it for assumptions about the present too (they'll be arriving about now), and for polite requests (will you be needing the car?), which is a marker of British politeness.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The -ing form carries the stress: at ten, we will be preSENTing; the auxiliaries run together as /wɪlbi/.

Additional example bank
  • Moment: At noon, the auditors will be checking the accounts.
  • Polite: Will you be attending the briefing?
  • Negative: We won't be accepting new bids until March.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: At ten, we will presenting (missing be after will)

✅ Right: At ten, we will be presenting

❌ Wrong: This time tomorrow I will be travel (base verb instead of -ing)

✅ Right: This time tomorrow I will be travelling
