Defining and Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Decide whether the clause identifies or merely adds.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use defining and non-defining relative clauses with the right punctuation and pronouns.
Examples in context
  • The report that we sent is late. Defining: tells you which report. — No commas, that is allowed.
  • The report, which we sent in June, is late. Non-defining: extra information. — Commas, never that.
  • The team that won the bid has disbanded. Defining: identifies the team. — Removing it changes the meaning.

👀 What do you observe?

A defining relative clause identifies which person or thing is meant, so it carries no commas and that is allowed. A non-defining clause adds information about something already identified, so it sits between commas, that is not allowed, and who or which is required. In a defining clause the pronoun can be left out when it is the object: the report we sent. It cannot be left out in a non-defining clause.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
defining, no commas identifies the noun the report that we sent
non-defining, commas adds information the report, which we sent in June,
that defining only the team that won the bid
who / which both types, people and things my manager, who joined in May,
pronoun omitted defining, object the report we sent
whose possession, both types the analyst whose model failed

Formula / rule: Ask whether the reader already knows which thing you mean. If not, the clause identifies it: no commas, that is fine, and the pronoun can vanish when it is the object. If yes, the clause only adds: commas, no that, and the pronoun must stay.

★ Why it matters

The comma is not decoration; it decides what the sentence asserts. The staff who passed the audit were promoted says only some passed. The staff, who passed the audit, were promoted says all of them did, and the audit is a side note. In a report that distinction is the finding.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers drop the object pronoun in defining clauses without thinking (the file we sent) and almost never make the reverse error, because the comma is audible as a pause and a pitch change when the clause is non-defining.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

A non-defining clause is spoken as a separate tone unit: the report, / which we sent in June, / is late. The defining clause runs as one unit with no pause.

Additional example bank
  • Defining, object omitted: The figures we published in March were revised.
  • Non-defining, added fact: The figures, which we published in March, were revised.
  • Whose: The client whose contract lapsed has reappeared.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: My manager, that joined in May, approved it (that in a non-defining clause)

✅ Right: My manager, who joined in May, approved it

❌ Wrong: The report which we sent in June is late and out of date (missing commas around a non-defining clause)

✅ Right: The report, which we sent in June, is late and out of date
