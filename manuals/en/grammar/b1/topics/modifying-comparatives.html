Modifying Comparatives

Say how much bigger, how much better, how much less.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can modify a comparative with much, far, slightly, a bit and no.
Examples in context
  • much faster a large difference — The new system is much faster.
  • slightly clearer a small difference — This draft is slightly clearer.
  • no better no difference at all — The second offer is no better.

👀 What do you observe?

A comparative can be graded by the size of the gap. Large: much, far, considerably, significantly, a great deal. Small: slightly, a little, a bit, marginally. None: no, not any. The modifier goes before the comparative adjective or adverb, and much and far work with both short and long forms.

2. Which pair marks a large difference?
Much and far mark a large gap.
Element / structure Role in English Application example
much / far large difference much faster, far more expensive
considerably / significantly large, formal considerably higher
slightly / marginally small difference slightly clearer
a bit / a little small, informal a bit cheaper
no / not any no difference no better, not any worse
even surprising extra degree even faster than expected

Formula / rule: Judge the gap, then pick the modifier: much or far for a large one, slightly or marginally for a small one, no for none. Keep the comparative form intact after the modifier: much more expensive, not much expensive.

★ Why it matters

These modifiers are the vocabulary of comparison in reports, where the size of a difference is the finding: marginally higher and far higher describe two different worlds. Learners often overstate with very, which cannot modify a comparative at all.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use way in speech for a large difference (way faster) and avoid it in writing. A bit and a little soften criticism, which makes them frequent in feedback.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The modifier is stressed when the size is the point: FAR faster, SLIGHTly clearer, NO better.

Additional example bank
  • Large: The second version is significantly cheaper to run.
  • Small: Response times are marginally slower.
  • None: The revised offer is no more attractive than the first.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The new system is very faster (very with a comparative)

✅ Right: The new system is much faster

❌ Wrong: This draft is slightly more clear (double comparative form)

✅ Right: This draft is slightly clearer
