Causative: Have and Get Something Done

Say that someone else did the work for you.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use have and get with an object and a past participle.
Examples in context
  • We had the report translated. Someone else translated it. — Have + object + participle.
  • She got the contract checked. Same meaning, more informal. — Get + object + participle.
  • I had my laptop stolen. Something happened to me, not arranged. — Have + object + participle, bad event.

👀 What do you observe?

The causative have something done means that someone else performs the action: we had the report translated, I need to have my laptop repaired. Get does the same job in a more conversational register. The same pattern with an unwanted event means that something happened to the subject: he had his laptop stolen. The object is the thing acted on, and the participle carries the action.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
have + object + participle arranged service we had the report translated
get + object + participle same, more informal she got the contract checked
have + object + participle, bad event something happened to the subject he had his wallet stolen
have + object + base verb cause someone to act they had the courier wait
get + object + to-infinitive persuade someone I got the supplier to reissue the invoice
need / want + have + participle future arrangement I need to have the car serviced

Formula / rule: Ask who did the work. If the subject arranged it, use have or get plus object plus past participle. If the subject did it, use the active voice. If the subject caused another person to act, use have plus object plus base verb, or get plus object plus to-infinitive.

★ Why it matters

This is the structure of service and delegation, and English uses it constantly: I had the figures checked, we got the room cleaned. Without it, learners describe arrangements by naming the agent, which sounds unnecessarily specific, or by using the passive, which loses the fact that the subject arranged it.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers prefer have in writing and get in speech, and they use the unpleasant-event pattern without any sense of arrangement: she had her bike stolen. Get is avoided in formal reports for this reason: it can suggest the subject was careless.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Have is unstressed in the causative (we had the report transLAted), and the participle carries the main stress.

Additional example bank
  • Service: We had the audit reviewed by an external firm.
  • Arrangement: She is getting the signage replaced.
  • Bad event: They had their offices flooded in June.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: We had translated the report by someone (active voice where the causative is meant)

✅ Right: We had the report translated

❌ Wrong: She got the contract check (base verb where a participle is needed)

✅ Right: She got the contract checked
