Level B1 Grammar

Future Perfect

Say what will be finished by a future point.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use will have + past participle with by and by the time.
Examples in context
  • By June, we will have completed the audit. Finished before June. — Will have + participle.
  • She will have left by the time you arrive. Earlier than your arrival. — By the time marks the point.
  • Will you have signed it by then? Question form. — Will + subject + have + participle.

👀 What do you observe?

The future perfect describes an action that will be complete before a specified future time. It is built with will have plus the past participle, and it almost always appears with by, by the time, before or a date. It is the future counterpart of the past perfect, and it is common in project planning and in promises with a deadline.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
will have + participle completed before a future point by June, we will have completed the audit
by / by the time the future reference point by the time you arrive
negative won't have + participle they won't have finished by Friday
question will + subject + have + participle will you have signed it by then?
with before the point may be an event we will have left before the panel sits
contrast with future continuous finished versus in progress will have finished / will be finishing

Formula / rule: Set the future deadline first, then say what will be finished by then: will have plus the participle. If the action will be in progress rather than complete, use the future continuous; if it is simply later, use will.

★ Why it matters

The future perfect is the tense of commitments: we will have delivered by June is a promise with a date attached, and it is checkable in a way that we will deliver is not. That is why it appears in contracts, project plans and performance reviews.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use it less than the past perfect, and often replace it in speech with will be done by or should have finished by. In writing it signals a firm commitment.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The stress falls on the participle: by June, we will have comPLETED the audit; the auxiliary strings are weak.

Additional example bank
  • Deadline: By the end of the quarter, the migration will have finished.
  • Earlier than an event: The panel will have reported before the board meets.
  • Question: Will the auditors have signed off by then?
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: By June, we will complete the audit (simple future where completion by a point is meant)

✅ Right: By June, we will have completed the audit

❌ Wrong: She will has left by the time you arrive (have after will)

✅ Right: She will have left by the time you arrive
