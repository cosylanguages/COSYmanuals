-ed and -ing Adjectives

Match the adjective to who feels and who causes.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use -ed for the person who feels and -ing for the thing that causes the feeling.
Examples in context
  • I was bored. The subject feels the feeling. — -ed for people.
  • The talk was boring. The subject causes it. — -ing for things.
  • We were confused by the form. Cause: the form. — Confusing form, confused people.

👀 What do you observe?

Adjectives ending in -ed describe how a person or animal feels: bored, confused, exhausted, interested. Adjectives ending in -ing describe what produces that feeling: a boring talk, a confusing form, an exhausting audit. The -ed form is not a passive; it is a state. A thing can be -ed only if something acted on it: a closed door, a bored student.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
-ed the feeler I was bored
-ing the cause the talk was boring
pairs feel versus cause confused / confusing, interested / interesting
state, not passive -ed is an adjective a bored student
things with -ed only if something acted on them a closed door, a broken window
intensifiers very, really, absolutely absolutely exhausted, really boring

Formula / rule: Ask who holds the feeling. If it is the subject, use -ed. If the subject causes the feeling in someone else, use -ing. Then check the intensifier, because exhausted and fascinating behave like extreme adjectives and take absolutely rather than very.

★ Why it matters

The pair is a small window onto English's habit of putting the experiencer in the subject slot: the news is depressing, the staff are depressed. Confusing them reverses who is affected, which in a report about morale is not a small error.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the -ing form for things and situations without thinking, and they extend -ing to people when describing their effect: he is exhausting to work with. That extension is normal and idiomatic.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

-ed is weak and quick (/bɔːd/), while -ing keeps a full ending (/ˈbɔːrɪŋ/), and the -ing form is longer by a syllable, which matches its 'more' meaning.

Additional example bank

  • Pair: a tiring week, a tired team
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I was boring by the presentation (the feeler takes -ed)

✅ Right: I was bored by the presentation

❌ Wrong: The presentation was bored (the cause takes -ing)

✅ Right: The presentation was boring
