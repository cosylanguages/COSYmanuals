Level B2 Grammar

Broader Intensifiers

Grade adjectives without repeating very.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use a range of intensifiers that carry register and collocation.
Examples in context
  • extremely encouraging very strong, neutral — Extremely works with most adjectives.
  • highly unusual strong, formal collocation — Highly suits unlikely, unusual, effective.
  • rather warm moderate, slightly negative — Rather implies more than expected.

👀 What do you observe?

Intensifiers form a scale: extremely and incredibly at the top, very and really in the middle, fairly and quite lower, slightly at the bottom. Some are restricted by collocation, since highly goes with unlikely, unusual and effective but not with tired, and utterly goes with negative extremes such as ridiculous or useless. Rather and fairly often carry an implied criticism.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
extremely / incredibly top of the scale extremely encouraging
very / really middle, neutral really useful
fairly / quite moderate, weaker than expected fairly thin evidence
rather moderate with implied criticism rather warm
highly restricted collocations highly unlikely, highly effective
utterly / completely negative extremes utterly useless

Formula / rule: Pick the strength first, then check the collocation. If the adjective is an extreme (exhausted, ridiculous), the intensifier is absolutely or utterly; if it is ordinary, very or extremely; if the speaker wants to imply a problem, rather or fairly.

★ Why it matters

The scale is what lets a report calibrate: fairly thin evidence and extremely thin evidence describe the same finding with different confidence. Reaching for very every time flattens that calibration, which is why examiners notice a narrow intensifier range at B1.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use pretty in speech for a moderate degree (pretty good) and avoid it in writing. Quite in British English usually means fairly, while in American English it often means very, which produces real misunderstanding.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The intensifier carries the main stress: exTREMEly encouraging; RATHER warm, with a falling tone that signals the criticism.

Additional example bank
  • Strong: The response was highly encouraging.
  • Moderate: The case is fairly strong.
  • Collocation: It is highly unlikely that the date will hold.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: highly tired (collocation: highly does not take tired)

✅ Right: extremely tired

❌ Wrong: very exhausted (very with an extreme adjective)

✅ Right: absolutely exhausted
