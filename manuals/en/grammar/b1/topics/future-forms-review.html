Future Forms Review

Choose will, going to, the present continuous or the present simple.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can pick the future form that matches the plan, the prediction or the timetable.
Examples in context
  • I'll call you tonight. Decision made now. — Will for spontaneous offers.
  • We're going to move the office. Plan decided earlier. — Going to for intentions.
  • I'm meeting the auditor at ten. Fixed arrangement. — Present continuous for appointments.

👀 What do you observe?

Four forms divide the future work. Will for decisions made at the moment of speaking, promises and predictions based on opinion. Going to for intentions decided before and for predictions based on visible evidence. Present continuous for fixed arrangements with a time. Present simple for timetables and schedules. Shall appears in offers and suggestions in British English.

will spontaneous decision, promise, opinion I'll call you tonight
going to prior intention, evidence-based prediction we're going to move the office
present continuous fixed arrangement I'm meeting the auditor at ten
present simple timetable, schedule the train leaves at 07:40
shall offers and suggestions, British shall I open the window?
future continuous / perfect in progress or completed by a future point I'll be working / I'll have finished

Formula / rule: Ask what kind of future it is. A decision made now takes will. An intention made earlier takes going to. An arrangement with a time and a person takes the present continuous. A published timetable takes the present simple. Then refine with the continuous or perfect future if the action will be in progress or finished by a future point.

★ Why it matters

The choice signals commitment. I'm seeing the client on Tuesday tells a colleague the appointment exists, while I'll see the client suggests it is still a matter of intention. In scheduling and project work the difference decides whether a date is real.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use will and going to almost interchangeably in speech when the distinction is not at stake, and they use the present continuous constantly for arrangements. Shall survives mainly in questions and in the north of England.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Contractions carry the future meaning: I'll /aɪl/, we're going to /ˈɡənə/ in fast speech, and the arrangement form is stressed on the verb.

Additional example bank
  • Decision: I'll send it now.
  • Intention: We're going to redesign the form.
  • Timetable: The panel sits on the first Thursday.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I am knowing the answer tomorrow (stative verb in the continuous)

✅ Right: I'll know the answer tomorrow

❌ Wrong: We will moving the office in June (missing be)

✅ Right: We are going to move the office in June
