Level B1 Grammar

Second Conditional

Talk about an unreal present or an unlikely future.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use if + past simple with would to describe hypotheses.
Examples in context
  • If I had more time, I would take the course. Unreal present. — Past form, present meaning.
  • If the office were closer, we would walk. Were for an unreal state. — Were for all persons in formal use.
  • What would you do if you lost the contract? Unlikely future. — Hypothetical question.

👀 What do you observe?

The second conditional describes something untrue now or unlikely in the future: if plus the past simple, then would plus the base verb. The past form does not refer to past time; it marks distance from reality. With be, were is used for all persons in careful English, and the if-clause can come second: I would take the course if I had more time.

if + past simple unreal present if I had more time
would + base the unreal result I would take the course
were for all persons formal unreal be if she were here
if-clause second result first I would tell you if I knew
unlikely future hypothetical event if you lost the contract
would in the result only never in the if-clause if I knew, I would say

Formula / rule: Decide whether the situation is real or unreal. Real present or future takes the first conditional (if + present, will). Unreal present or unlikely future takes the second (if + past, would). Put the past form in the if-clause and would in the result, never the other way round.

★ Why it matters

The second conditional is the grammar of advice, negotiation and imagination: it lets a speaker propose something without committing to it. That is why it appears in every polite suggestion (would you consider...), and why learners who avoid it sound blunt.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use were in careful speech and was in casual speech without noticing, and they often reduce would to /d/ (I'd take it). The past form in the if-clause is never stressed unless the contrast is the point.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The stress falls on the hypothetical element: if I HAD more time, I would TAKE the course; the modal is weak.

Additional example bank
  • Unreal present: If the team were larger, we could take both clients.
  • Unlikely future: If the deadline moved, would you still bid?
  • Advice: If I were you, I would ask for the minutes.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: If I would have more time, I would take the course (would in the if-clause)

✅ Right: If I had more time, I would take the course

❌ Wrong: If she was here, she would know (indicative for an unreal present in careful writing)

✅ Right: If she were here, she would know
