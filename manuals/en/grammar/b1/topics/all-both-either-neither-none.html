Level B2 Grammar

All, Both, Either, Neither, None

Talk about two things, several things, or none of them.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use all, both, either, neither and none with the right number and verb.
Examples in context
  • Both reports were late. Two, affirmative, plural verb. — Both + plural.
  • Neither report was complete. Two, negative, singular verb. — Neither + singular in formal use.
  • None of the reports was complete. More than two, negative. — None of + plural noun.

👀 What do you observe?

Both, either and neither work with two things. Both is affirmative and takes a plural verb. Either means one or the other, and takes a singular verb. Neither means not one and not the other, and is singular in careful use. All and none work with three or more: all takes a plural verb when it means every one, none can take either, with the singular preferred in formal writing.

both + plural verb two, affirmative both proposals were rejected
either + singular verb one of two, any either option works
neither + singular verb two, negative neither proposal was acceptable
all + plural verb three or more, affirmative all the bids were opened
none of + plural noun three or more, negative none of the bids was opened
not either / neither ... nor negative of two neither the chair nor the members replied

Formula / rule: Count first: two things take both, either or neither; three or more take all or none. Then match the verb: both and all plural, either and neither singular, none singular in formal writing and plural in speech. Never write both of them was, and never use neither with three things.

★ Why it matters

These quantifiers carry the difference between a choice and an exclusion, which is the substance of most decisions: either option works leaves the choice open, neither option works closes it. Getting the number right also keeps the verb agreement right, which is where the error becomes visible.

💭 Think like a native speaker

In speech, none takes a plural verb freely (none of them were ready) and nobody objects; in writing, the singular is safer. Neither ... nor with two subjects takes the verb of the nearer subject.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Both is stressed when the contrast is the point (BOTH proposals), and neither takes a stress on the first syllable /ˈnaɪ.ðə/ in British English.

Additional example bank
  • Two, negative: Neither of the two suppliers met the deadline.
  • Three or more: None of the three suppliers met the deadline.
  • Open choice: Either candidate would suit the role.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: Both of the proposals was rejected (singular verb after both)

✅ Right: Both of the proposals were rejected

❌ Wrong: None of the three report was complete (singular noun after none of)

✅ Right: None of the three reports was complete
