Phrasal Verbs: Grammar Patterns

Know whether the object goes before or after the particle.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can separate transitive phrasal verbs correctly and keep inseparable ones together.
Examples in context
  • They turned down the offer. Separable: the object can follow. — Turn the offer down.
  • She looks after the archive. Inseparable: the particle stays. — Never look the archive after.
  • We ran into the client. Inseparable, and idiomatic. — Ran into = met by chance.

👀 What do you observe?

Separable phrasal verbs allow the object between the verb and the particle, or after both: turn down the offer, turn the offer down. A pronoun must go between: turn it down. Inseparable verbs keep the particle next to the verb: look after the archive, run into a client. Three-part verbs are always inseparable: put up with the delay.

Formula / rule: Check the pattern in a dictionary before you split. If the verb is separable, put a pronoun inside and a noun either side. If it is inseparable, keep the particle attached. In formal writing, replace the whole thing with a single verb.

★ Why it matters

The pattern decides where the object goes, and the error is audible: turn off it is a learner error, while turn it off is automatic for a native. In writing, the phrasal verb is also a register marker, which is why reports say postpone rather than put off.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers split separable verbs freely in speech (turn the light off) and often keep the object first in writing (turn off the light). Inseparable verbs are learned as single words.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The particle is stressed when it carries the meaning: turn it DOWN; the plan fell THROUGH.

Additional example bank
  • Separable: They called the meeting off at short notice.
  • Inseparable: The lawyer looked into the complaint.
  • Three-part: Nobody will put up with that behaviour.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They turned down it (pronoun after the particle)

✅ Right: They turned it down

❌ Wrong: She looks the archive after (inseparable verb split)

✅ Right: She looks after the archive
