Level B2 Grammar

Have To, Must, Should, Had Better

Grade the pressure from a rule to a piece of advice.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can choose the modal that matches how strong the obligation is and who imposed it.
Examples in context
  • You must submit it today. Strong, speaker's authority. — Must.
  • We have to file by 31 March. External rule. — Have to.
  • You should check the figures. Advice. — Should.

👀 What do you observe?

Must and have to both express obligation, but must usually carries the speaker's authority while have to reports a rule imposed from outside. Should gives advice with no compulsion. Had better gives advice with a warning of consequences and takes the base verb. Ought to sits with should but is slightly more formal and less common in speech.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
must strong obligation, speaker's authority you must submit the form today
have to external rule or necessity we have to file by 31 March
should / ought to advice, expectation you should check the figures
had better + base advice with a warning you'd better tell them
don't have to no obligation you don't have to attend
mustn't prohibition you mustn't share the file

Formula / rule: Decide the strength first: obligation, advice or warning. Then decide the source: the speaker (must), an institution (have to), or nobody in particular (should). Keep mustn't apart from don't have to, since one forbids and the other releases.

★ Why it matters

These four carry the difference between a rule and a suggestion, which is the difference between a compliance failure and a missed improvement. In a workplace, a task described with should is optional in practice, and a task described with must is auditable, so the choice has consequences.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers keep must for written rules and self-imposed duties, and use have to for everything institutional in speech. Had better is usually contracted and can sound like a threat when the stress falls on better.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Have to reduces to /hæftə/ and had better to /ˈbetə/, with the auxiliary almost lost; should is unstressed unless it corrects.

Additional example bank
  • Rule: Visitors must sign in at reception.
  • Necessity: We have to resubmit the whole application.
  • Advice: You should ask for the minutes in writing.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: You don't have to share the file; it is confidential (no obligation where a prohibition is meant)

✅ Right: You mustn't share the file; it is confidential

❌ Wrong: You had better to check the figures (to after had better)

✅ Right: You had better check the figures
