Level B1 Grammar

Past Perfect Continuous

Say how long something had been going on before a past event.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use had been + -ing for an activity in progress before a past point.
Examples in context
  • They had been negotiating for weeks. Duration before a past event. — Had been + -ing.
  • The line had been running since dawn. Started earlier, still true at the point. — Since marks the start.
  • She had been working there for a year. Duration, with a visible result. — For marks the length.

👀 What do you observe?

The past perfect continuous describes an activity that had been going on up to a past moment, often explaining a visible result: the ground was wet because it had been raining. It takes for with a length of time and since with a starting point, and it is the past counterpart of the present perfect continuous.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
had been + -ing activity up to a past point they had been negotiating for weeks
for + duration length of time for a year
since + starting point when it began since dawn
visible result explains the state at the moment the ground was wet because it had been raining
negative hadn't been + -ing the system hadn't been working for days
contrast with past perfect activity versus completed action had been writing / had written

Formula / rule: Fix the past moment, then ask whether the activity was completed by it or still running into it. Completed: past perfect. Still running, with duration: past perfect continuous. Add for or since to make the duration explicit.

★ Why it matters

This tense is the grammar of explanation in reports: the figures were unreliable because the system had been running on a patched version for months. It carries the cause and the duration in one clause, which is why incident reports lean on it.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use it freely in speech when explaining a state (I'd been waiting for an hour), and often reduce had been to /ɪdbɪn/ or /bɪn/.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Been is weak and the -ing form carries the stress: they had been neGOtiating for WEEKS.

Additional example bank
  • Duration: The team had been pressing for a decision since March.
  • Explanation: The file was incomplete because it had been edited offline.
  • Negative: The alarm hadn't been working for days.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: They had been negotiate for weeks (base verb instead of -ing)

✅ Right: They had been negotiating for weeks

❌ Wrong: She had been working there since a year (since with a duration)

✅ Right: She had been working there for a year
