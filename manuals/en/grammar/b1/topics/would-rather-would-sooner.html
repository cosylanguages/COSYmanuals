Would Rather and Would Sooner

Express a preference, with one subject or two.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use would rather and would sooner with the base verb and with a past form for another subject.
Examples in context
  • I would rather wait. Same subject, base verb. — I'd rather wait.
  • I'd rather you waited. Different subject, past form. — Unreal past for politeness.
  • She would sooner resign than move. Comparison with than. — Rather ... than.

👀 What do you observe?

Would rather and would sooner express preference. With the same subject, the base verb follows: I would rather wait. With a different subject, the past form follows: I would rather you waited, which is not about the past but about distance and politeness. Comparison takes than plus the base verb: rather resign than move. The negative puts not first: I'd rather not go.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
would rather + base same subject I would rather wait
would rather + subject + past different subject I'd rather you waited
would rather ... than comparison, base verbs rather resign than move
negative not before the base verb I'd rather not discuss it
would sooner same patterns, slightly more formal she would sooner resign
would prefer same meaning, to-infinitive I would prefer to wait

Formula / rule: Decide who is choosing. Same subject: base verb after would rather. Different subject: past form. Comparison: than plus the base verb. Negative: not plus the base verb.

★ Why it matters

Would rather is how English states a preference without demanding it, and the past form with another subject is the politest way to say what you want someone else to do. That is why it fills negotiation and correspondence.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers contract it constantly (I'd rather) and use it more than would prefer in speech. The past form after another subject is not a past meaning and natives never read it as one.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Rather carries the stress: I'd RATHER wait; the contraction I'd is barely audible.

Additional example bank
  • Preference: I'd rather keep the current supplier.
  • Other subject: We'd rather the panel decided first.
  • Comparison: They would sooner delay than sign a weak contract.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I'd rather you wait outside with a different subject (base verb where the past form is expected)

✅ Right: I'd rather you waited outside

❌ Wrong: I'd rather to wait (infinitive after would rather)

✅ Right: I'd rather wait
