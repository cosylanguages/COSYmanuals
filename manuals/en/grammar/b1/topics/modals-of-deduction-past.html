Modals of Deduction: Past

Say how certain a past event is.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use must have, might have and can't have to judge past events.
Examples in context
  • The file must have been deleted. Almost certain. — Must have + participle.
  • She might have missed the email. Possible. — Might have + participle.
  • They can't have known. Almost impossible. — Can't have + participle.

👀 What do you observe?

Past deduction uses a modal plus have plus the past participle. Must have marks near certainty that something happened. Might have, may have and could have mark possibility. Can't have and couldn't have mark near certainty that it did not happen. Should have and ought to have add expectation or regret, and needn't have marks something done that was unnecessary.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
must have near certainty, affirmative the file must have been deleted
might / may / could have possibility she might have missed the email
can't / couldn't have near certainty, negative they can't have known
should have expectation, often criticism you should have told me
needn't have unnecessary but done you needn't have waited
modal + have + participle never a past form after the modal must have been, not must was

Formula / rule: Judge the evidence first, then pick the modal: strong positive evidence takes must have, possible evidence takes might have, strong contrary evidence takes can't have. The verb after the modal is always have plus the participle, whatever the tense of the story.

★ Why it matters

Past deduction is how English reasons aloud about what it cannot see: the file must have been deleted says the evidence is overwhelming without saying who did it. That is a useful move in an audit and a dangerous one in a disciplinary hearing, which is why the modal choice is scrutinised.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers contract the pattern heavily (must've, might've, can't've) and the contracted forms sound identical to must of, might of, which is a common spelling error even among natives.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

Have is weak /əv/ in all four, and the modal carries the stress: it MUST have been deleted; they CAN'T have known.

Additional example bank
  • Certain: The panel must have received the report late.
  • Possible: The supplier may have misread the specification.
  • Impossible: They couldn't have finished in one day.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: The file must was deleted (past form after a modal)

✅ Right: The file must have been deleted

❌ Wrong: They mustn't have known (mustn't for a negative deduction)

✅ Right: They can't have known
