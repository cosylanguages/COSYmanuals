Level B2 Grammar

Verb + Preposition

Keep the preposition that belongs to the verb.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use the fixed preposition after common verbs and the gerund that follows it.
Examples in context
  • We depend on the supplier. Verb + on + noun. — Depend on, rely on, insist on.
  • She apologised for the delay. Verb + for + noun. — Apologise for, thank for, blame for.
  • He insisted on a written reply. Verb + on + noun or -ing. — Insisted on replying.

👀 What do you observe?

Verbs take prepositions by convention and the pair is the unit: depend on, rely on, insist on, apologise for, agree to (a proposal) or agree with (a person), apply for (a job) or apply to (an institution), look after, refer to, deal with. When a verb or adjective plus preposition is followed by an action, the gerund follows: insisted on replying, apologised for being late.

on depend, rely, insist, concentrate we depend on the supplier
for apologise, thank, blame, apply she apologised for the delay
to agree, refer, object, listen they agreed to the terms
with agree with a person, deal with a problem we dealt with the complaint
of accuse of, approve of, think of they accused him of leaking
preposition + -ing the gerund after the preposition insisted on replying

Formula / rule: Learn the verb with its preposition and with the gerund that follows. Where a verb takes different prepositions, the meaning decides: agree to a proposal, agree with a person, agree on a price.

★ Why it matters

These pairs decide meaning in exactly the cases that matter: apply for a job and apply to a company are both correct and not interchangeable, and accuse of is not the same as blame for. A wrong preposition turns a precise claim into a vague one.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers vary in a few pairs (different from, different to) and are fixed in the rest. The gerund after a preposition is automatic for a native and a common gap for a learner.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The preposition is unstressed and leans on the verb: dePEND on, aPOlogise for, inSIST on.

Additional example bank
  • Depend: The timetable depends on the audit.
  • Apply: She applied for the post and applied to the board.
  • Preposition plus gerund: They insisted on seeing the file.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: We depend of the supplier (wrong preposition)

✅ Right: We depend on the supplier

❌ Wrong: He insisted to see the file (infinitive after a preposition)

✅ Right: He insisted on seeing the file
