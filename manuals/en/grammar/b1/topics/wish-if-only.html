Wish and If Only

Express regret about the present, the past and other people.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use wish and if only for three different times.
Examples in context
  • I wish I had more time. Regret about the present. — Past simple after wish.
  • I wish I had taken the job. Regret about the past. — Past perfect after wish.
  • I wish they would answer. Wish for a change in others. — Would after wish.

👀 What do you observe?

Wish and if only take the same tense pattern, and if only is the stronger form. Regret about the present takes the past simple: I wish I had more time. Regret about the past takes the past perfect: I wish I had taken the job. A wish for someone else to change takes would: I wish they would answer. Wish plus the infinitive (I wish to complain) is a formal request, not a regret.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
wish + past simple regret about the present I wish I had more time
wish + past perfect regret about the past I wish I had taken the job
wish + would wish for change in others I wish they would answer
if only + past perfect stronger regret about the past if only we had known
wish + to-infinitive formal request I wish to complain
wish + could regret about ability I wish I could help

Formula / rule: Decide the time of the regret, then move one step back: present regret takes the past simple, past regret takes the past perfect, and a wish about someone else's behaviour takes would. If only does the same work with more force and can stand alone as an exclamation.

★ Why it matters

Wish is how English expresses regret without naming a mistake, which makes it the polite form of criticism: I wish the file had been checked says the same as the file was not checked, without an accusation. If only adds the sense that it is now too late.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use I wish I had in speech constantly, often reduced to I wish I'd, and they use if only mostly in writing or in exclamations. Wish plus would can sound irritated, which is why it is avoided in formal correspondence.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The contracted had is weak (I wish I'd known), and the stress falls on the regret: I wish I HAD more time; if ONly we had known.

Additional example bank
  • Present: I wish the office were closer to the station.
  • Past: If only we had kept the original file.
  • Other people: I wish the panel would publish its reasons.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I wish I would have taken the job (would in a past regret)

✅ Right: I wish I had taken the job

❌ Wrong: I wish I have more time (present tense after wish)

✅ Right: I wish I had more time
