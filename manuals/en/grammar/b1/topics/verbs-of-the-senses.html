Level B2 Grammar

Verbs of the Senses

Describe what you see, hear and feel precisely.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can use sense verbs with a bare infinitive, an -ing form or an adjective.
Examples in context
  • I saw him leave. The whole action, start to finish. — Bare infinitive.
  • I saw him leaving. An action already under way. — The -ing form.
  • The room felt cold. Sense verb plus adjective. — Feel + adjective describes.

👀 What do you observe?

Verbs of the senses take an object plus either a bare infinitive or an -ing form. The bare infinitive reports a complete action: I saw him leave. The -ing form reports an action in progress: I saw him leaving. Feel, look, sound, smell and taste also take adjectives to describe the impression: the room felt cold, the plan sounds risky.

Element / structure Role in English Application example
see / hear + object + bare infinitive complete action I saw him leave
see / hear + object + -ing action in progress I saw him leaving
feel / look / sound / smell / taste + adjective impression the plan sounds risky
feel like + -ing inclination I feel like leaving early
passive be seen to + infinitive he was seen to leave
watch / notice / observe same pattern as see we watched them work

Formula / rule: Decide whether the action was complete or in progress, then choose the bare infinitive or the -ing form. For impressions, use an adjective after the sense verb, not an adverb.

★ Why it matters

The difference between saw him leave and saw him leaving is evidential: the first reports the whole event, the second catches it mid-way. In a witness statement or an incident report, that distinction is the finding.

💭 Think like a native speaker

Native speakers use the -ing form far more in speech, because most things we notice are already in progress when we notice them. The bare infinitive appears mainly after see and hear with a completed action.

🗣️ Pronunciation note

The -ing form carries the stress when the progress is the point: I saw him LEAVing.

Additional example bank
  • Complete: We saw the panel sign the minutes.
  • In progress: We saw the panel arguing in the corridor.
  • Impression: The proposal sounds expensive.
⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid

❌ Wrong: I saw him to leave (to-infinitive after see)

✅ Right: I saw him leave

❌ Wrong: The room felt coldly (adverb where an adjective is needed)

✅ Right: The room felt cold
