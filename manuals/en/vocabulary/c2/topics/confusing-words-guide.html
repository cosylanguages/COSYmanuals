C2 Confusing Words & Near-Synonyms Master Guide

Dissecting minute semantic boundaries, false friends, academic verb pairs, and near-synonym discrimination

🎯 Master Goal: Wield native-like precision across subtle C2 word pairs without confusing imply/infer, historic/historical, assure/ensure/insure, or rob/steal.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these two sentences: "The author implied that the report was flawed." (Suggests without stating directly) vs. "The reader inferred that the report was flawed." (Deduces/concludes from evidence). One word belongs to the speaker/writer, the other to the listener/reader!

1. Precision check: "I assure you that the data is correct." What is the target of 'assure'?
Correct · 'Assure' takes a personal object (assure someone), whereas 'ensure' takes a clause (ensure that X happens).
2. Theft check: "The thieves robbed the bank." Did they rob the person/institution or steal the cash object?
Correct · You 'rob' a person or place (rob a bank/victim), but 'steal' a specific object/money (steal cash).
