B2 Confusing Words Master Guide

Academic cause/effect, corporate business terminology, research vocabulary, and social distinction pairs

🎯 Master Goal: Deploy precise business, academic, and social vocabulary without confusing affect/effect, evidence/proof, customer/consumer, or economic/economical.

👀 What do you notice?

Compare these two sentences: "The marketing campaign affected our sales." (Verb: influenced) vs. "The marketing campaign had a positive effect on sales." (Noun: result/outcome). One letter distinguishes the verb from the noun!

💡 Check your understanding (CCQs)

Score: 0 / 2
1. Business check: If a company pays someone a fixed monthly or annual figure regardless of hours worked, is that a 'salary' or a 'wage'?
Correct · 'Salary' is fixed professional annual/monthly pay, while 'wage' is calculated by hours worked.
2. Research check: Is 'evidence' countable or uncountable in academic English?
Correct · 'Evidence' is uncountable in standard English (say 'a piece of evidence').

🧠 Logic Map: Core B2 Upper-Intermediate Semantic Confusion Groups

Word AWord BCore Semantic DifferenceKey Examples
Affect (v) Effect (n) Verb: to influence or produce a change vs Noun: the result or consequence of a change This decision will affect everyone. vs It had a huge effect.
Cause Reason The origin/factor that produces an event vs The explanation/justification given by a person the cause of the fire vs the reason for his absence
Evidence Proof Uncountable facts/data suggesting a conclusion vs Conclusive facts establishing absolute certainty scientific evidence vs irrefutable mathematical proof
Customer Consumer Person who buys the product from a store vs Person who ultimately uses/consumes the product store customer vs end consumer in the market
Salary Wage Fixed professional monthly/annual pay vs Hourly, daily, or weekly payment for labor an executive salary vs minimum hourly wage
Economic Economical Relating to the economy or commerce vs Thrifty / money-saving / avoiding waste economic growth vs an economical small car
Habit Routine Personal automatic repeated behavior vs Sequence of actions followed regularly as a plan biting nails is a bad habit vs a morning workout routine
