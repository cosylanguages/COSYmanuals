Unit 1: First Contact & Social Greetings

Lesson 1: Greetings & Introductions

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Verb 'to be' (present simple)
  • Possessive adjectives (my, your)
🎯 I can: Can greet people, introduce oneself, and ask basic questions in spoken English.
Task
Speed-meeting roleplay: exchange names and greetings with 3 partners in 3 minutes.
Speaking focus
Natural greeting intonation and clear vowel pronunciation in names.

Lesson 2: Asking Simple Questions & Clarification

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Question words (What, Where, How)
  • Can for requests
🎯 I can: Can ask simple questions and request clarification when speaking.
Task
Clarification repair exercise: listen to mumbled phrases and request repetition or clarification.
Speaking focus
Rising intonation on polite clarification requests.

Lesson 3: Personal Details: Origin & Numbers

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Where + to be (Where are you from?)
  • Numbers 1-30 for age and phone numbers
🎯 I can: Can exchange personal details including origin, age, and phone numbers in conversation.
Task
Event registration roleplay: state full name, country of origin, age, and phone number to a partner.
Speaking focus
Rhythmic digit grouping when dictating phone numbers aloud.

Lesson 4: Everyday Courtesy & Social Polish

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Imperatives for social formulas
  • Polite modal triggers (Please, Thank you, Excuse me)
🎯 I can: Can use essential courtesy phrases naturally in everyday social encounters.
Task
Doorway and hallway micro-simulations: practice passing, bumping, and thanking in realistic pairs.
Speaking focus
Melodic rise-fall on 'You're welcome' and polite 'Excuse me'.

Lesson 5: First Contact Fluency Roleplay & Unit Review

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Synthesis of to be, question words, numbers, and politeness formulas
🎯 I can: Can carry out a complete 3-minute first-contact introduction dialogue fluently.
Task
Networking cocktail party simulation: circulate and complete 3 full introductory conversations.
Speaking focus
Sustaining speech flow and handling unexpected questions without L1 pause.

Unit 2: Everyday Situational Conversations

Lesson 1: Basic Shopping & Ordering Phrases

Grammar Focus Points:
  • I would like...
  • How much is...?
🎯 I can: Can order food, drinks, and buy basic goods in shop transactions.
Task
Market stall interaction: order items from a price list and ask for the total bill.
Speaking focus
Polite sentence stress on 'I would LIKE...' rather than blunt demands.

Lesson 2: Asking for & Giving Directions

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Prepositions of place
  • Imperatives (Turn left, Go straight)
🎯 I can: Can ask for and give basic street directions.
Task
Map navigation dialogue: guide a partner through a city grid from station to museum.
Speaking focus
Clear stress on directional keywords (LEFT, RIGHT, STRAIGHT).

Lesson 3: Cafés, Snacks & Preferences

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Like / don't like + noun
  • Can I have...?
🎯 I can: Can order drinks and snacks in a café and state simple taste preferences.
Task
Café waiter and customer simulation: order a hot drink and snack, specifying milk or sugar.
Speaking focus
Natural contracted forms in 'Can I have...' (/kənaɪ hæv/).

Lesson 4: Public Transport & Travel Tickets

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Which ticket / Which train...?
  • Time prepositions (at 9 o'clock, in 10 minutes)
🎯 I can: Can buy public transit tickets and ask about bus or train departure times.
Task
Ticket office roleplay: inquire about departure time, ticket price, and platform number.
Speaking focus
Articulating times and numbers clearly in noisy public transit contexts.

Lesson 5: Everyday Survival Roleplay & Unit Review

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Synthesis of transactional request structures and direction imperatives
🎯 I can: Can navigate a multi-step city transaction (buying a ticket, asking directions, ordering a coffee).
Task
3-station city survival circuit: complete tasks at transit kiosk, coffee shop, and street corner.

Smooth transition between transaction types and sustained spoken fluency.

Unit 3: Personal Life & Daily Routines

Lesson 1: Talking About Family & Friends

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Possessive 's (John's sister)
  • Have / Has got for family relationships
🎯 I can: Can introduce family members and describe basic family relationships in conversation.
Task
Photo album talk: show 3 photos (or draw family members) and describe relationships to a partner.
Speaking focus
Pronouncing possessive 's clearly (/z/ vs /s/).

Lesson 2: Describing Your Home & Space

Grammar Focus Points:
  • There is / There are
  • Basic room prepositions (in the kitchen, on the table)
🎯 I can: Can describe home layout and furniture items in spoken English.
Task
Home tour roleplay: guide a visitor through your apartment describing rooms and key items.
Speaking focus
Distinguishing 'There is' (singular) and 'There are' (plural) in connected speech.

Lesson 3: Daily Routine & Daily Habits

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Present Simple (I / You / We / They)
  • Frequency adverbs (always, usually, sometimes, never)
🎯 I can: Can describe daily routines and habit frequency in conversation.
Task
Day-in-the-life exchange: interview partner about their typical morning and evening routine.
Speaking focus
Correct placement of frequency adverbs BEFORE main verbs in speech.

Lesson 4: Telling Time & Schedules

Grammar Focus Points:
  • What time is it? / At + exact time
  • Present simple question forms (What time do you...?)
🎯 I can: Can ask and state clock times and discuss simple event schedules.
Task
Timetable alignment: compare daily calendars with a classmate to find a free time slot.
Speaking focus
Rhythmic stress on time expressions (half past TEN, at FIVE o'clock).

Lesson 5: Personal Life Storytelling & Unit Review

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Synthesis of Present Simple, habits, time connectors, and possessives
🎯 I can: Can present a 2-minute spoken summary of personal life, family, and weekly routines.
Task
Personal podcast introduction: record a 2-minute monologue about yourself, family, and daily routine.
Speaking focus
Using sequence markers (First, Then, After that) for fluent monologue cohesion.

Unit 4: Work, Study & Free Time

Lesson 1: Professions & Workplace Tasks

Grammar Focus Points:
  • A / An with job titles
  • Present Simple 3rd person singular intro (He/She works as...)
🎯 I can: Can state job titles, work locations, and basic workplace responsibilities.
Task
Networking speed round: state your job (or student status) and workplace to 3 partners.
Speaking focus
Using 'a' vs 'an' smoothly before consonant and vowel job titles.

Lesson 2: Hobbies, Sports & Free Time

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Like / Love / Enjoy + -ing
  • Play + sports / Do + activities
🎯 I can: Can discuss leisure interests, sports, and favorite free-time activities.
Task
Hobby matchmaker: interview classmates about hobbies to find shared interests.
Speaking focus
Soft pronunciation of -ing suffix without harsh /ɡ/ sound.

Lesson 3: Expressing Opinions & Simple Likes/Dislikes

Grammar Focus Points:
  • I think... / In my opinion...
  • Why + Because for simple reasons
🎯 I can: Can express simple opinions about movies, sports, or food with basic reasons.
Task
Opinion line-up: stand on a scale from 'Love it' to 'Hate it' for various activities and justify why.
Speaking focus
Using enthusiastic or contrastive voice tone when giving opinions.

Lesson 4: Weekend Plans & Inviting Friends

Grammar Focus Points:

🎯 I can: Can invite a partner to a weekend activity and accept or decline politely.
Task
Weekend planner roleplay: invite a friend to the park or cinema and agree on time/place.
Speaking focus
Warm, enthusiastic intonation on 'Sounds good!' and polite regret when declining.

Lesson 5: Social Inviting & Workplace Chat Roleplay

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Synthesis of job descriptions, leisure preferences, and invitation structures
🎯 I can: Can engage in workplace small talk about hobbies and make lunch invitations fluently.
Task
Office coffee machine simulation: chat with a colleague about work and invite them to lunch.

Natural conversational pacing during spontaneous workplace small talk.

Task
Grocery market roleplay: buy ingredients for a recipe, asking for prices and weights.

Lesson 2: Clothes, Sizes & Trying Things On

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Demonstratives (this, that, these, those)

Clothing boutique simulation: request a size medium shirt, try it on, and decide whether to buy.

🎯 I can: Can describe simple physical symptoms and ask for basic medication at a pharmacy.
Task
Pharmacist consultation: explain a headache or fever and ask for appropriate medicine.
Speaking focus
Expressing physical discomfort clearly using 'I have a...'.

Unit 6: Past Experiences & Future Dreams

Lesson 1: Talking About Yesterday (Past Simple 'To Be')

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Was / Were (affirmative, negative, questions)
  • Past time markers (yesterday, last night, ago)
🎯 I can: Can talk about where you were yesterday and describe past states or locations.
Task
Yesterday's location check: interview classmate about where they were at various times yesterday.
Speaking focus
Pronouncing 'was' (/wəz/) and 'were' (/wɜː/) accurately without over-stressing.

Lesson 2: Sharing Past Weekend Activities

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Past Simple regular (-ed) & key irregular verbs (went, had, bought, saw)
  • Did you...?
🎯 I can: Can narrate basic past weekend events using common past simple verbs.
Task
Monday morning catch-up: recount 3 things you did last weekend to a group of colleagues.
Speaking focus
Correct past -ed endings (/t/, /d/, /ɪd/) and key irregular past forms.

Lesson 3: Future Plans & Intentions ('Going to')

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Be going to + verb for future plans
  • Future time markers (tomorrow, next week, next month)
🎯 I can: Can state future personal plans, travel intentions, and upcoming events.
Task
Bucket list sharing: talk about 3 things you are going to do next summer or next year.
Speaking focus
Natural reduced pronunciation of 'going to' (/ɡənə/) in casual speech.

Lesson 4: Spontaneous Offers & Simple Predictions

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Will / Won't for spontaneous offers and simple predictions
  • I think it will...
🎯 I can: Can make spontaneous offers of assistance and simple weather predictions in conversation.
Task
Helping hand roleplay: respond to partner's situational problems with spontaneous 'I'll help you' offers.
Speaking focus
Contraction 'I'll' (/aɪl/) in immediate spoken offers.

Lesson 5: A1 Spoken Capstone: Personal Story & Fluency Review

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Synthesis of Present Simple, Past Simple, and Future 'Going to'
🎯 I can: Can present a coherent 3-minute spoken personal narrative combining past, present, and future.
Task
A1 Showcase Monologue: deliver a 3-minute talk covering where you are from, your daily routine, a past memory, and future plans.
Speaking focus
Smooth tense transitions between past memory, present routine, and future aspirations.
