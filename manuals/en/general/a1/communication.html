EN Communication Manual — General (A1)

Interactive dialogues, production tasks, and pedagogical adaptations.

Unit 1: Hello — Greetings, names & alphabet

Lesson 1: Greetings, names & the alphabet

Self-Portrait Task: Hello, good morning! My name is Alex, A-L-E-X.

A: Hello, good morning! B: Hi! Good morning!
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Learn the alphabet song with gestures and TPR body movements for each letter, followed by a fast-paced flashcard greeting game.
  • Teens: Interactive digital spell-off competition using smartphones/tablets and online group polls to race spelling names aloud.
  • Adults: Focus on real-life transactional workplace introductions, business card exchange roleplays, and explicit phonics/spelling rules.
  • Seniors: Practice greetings in familiar concrete social contexts with large-print letter cards, a deliberate pace, and comfortable acoustic repetition.

Lesson 2: To be — I am, you are (positive & negative)

Self-Portrait Task: I am a student. I am not a teacher.

A: Are you a student? B: Yes, I am. You are a friend.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Puppet roleplay and matching game linking pronoun/verb cards ('I am' / 'You are') with physical movements and high-fives.
  • Teens: Competitive pair-matching game on digital whiteboards creating avatar profile cards with positive/negative identity statements.
  • Adults: Explicit grammar rule breakdown of contractions ('I'm' vs 'I am') applied directly to professional self-introductions and identity forms.
  • Seniors: Guided turn-taking dialogue practice in a quiet setting using clear visual contrast charts for affirmative and negative forms.

Lesson 3: Meeting someone for the first time

Self-Portrait Task: Nice to meet you! I'm fine, thanks.

A: What's your name? B: I'm Alex. How are you? A: I'm fine, thanks. And you?
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Circle greeting game passing a ball while singing 'How are you?' and practicing handshakes with funny character voices.
  • Teens: Speed-friending rotation activity and digital messaging roleplay using polite first-meeting phrases and peer feedback.
  • Adults: Formal vs informal context comparison (networking event vs casual meetup) with explicit intonation practice for polite inquiries.
  • Seniors: Pair dialogues focused on daily neighborhood encounters, emphasizing clear pronunciation, steady pacing, and gentle repetition.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: Hello, who are you?

A: Welcome! Excuse me, is this the English class? B: Yes, it is! Take care and see you later.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: TPR action story game responding to spoken imperatives ('listen', 'repeat') with energetic physical gestures and rhythm chants.
  • Teens: Peer video recording challenge creating short greeting clips and voting on the best polite spoken expressions.
  • Adults: Transactional spoken simulations (arriving at a reception desk or customer service counter) focusing on natural polite intonation.
  • Seniors: Structured conversational practice simulating community club greetings with audio-supported repetition and ample thinking time.

Lesson 5: Unit 1 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Interactive review board game with visual station challenges for spelling, greeting puppets, and 'to be' chant rewards.
  • Teens: Gamified digital quiz competition with instant leaderboard feedback covering alphabet, greetings, and 'to be' questions.
  • Adults: Self-assessment check-grid with targeted error-analysis exercise and practical written/spoken grammar review tasks.
  • Seniors: Unhurried paper-based or group review session with clear visual layouts, reviewing core Unit 1 concepts through shared recall.

Unit 2: Where are you from? — Countries & nationalities

Lesson 1: Countries, nationalities & languages

Self-Portrait Task: I am from France. I speak French and English.

A: Where are you from? B: I am from France. I speak French.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: World map flag-matching game with physical country tag racing and chant drills for nationalities.
  • Teens: Digital map quiz racing peers to identify flag icons and spell nationality adjectives online.
  • Adults: Business origin introductions and travel document reading exercises focusing on capital letter capitalization rules.
  • Seniors: Large-print geographical card sorting with steady pronunciation practice for country and language pairs.

Lesson 2: To be — he/she/it is · They are · Questions

Self-Portrait Task: My friend is from Italy. She is Italian.

A: Where is she from? B: She is from Italy. She is Italian.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Famous cartoon character guessing game using 'Where is he/she from?' card prompts and movement responses.
  • Teens: Avatar celebrity origin trivia challenge with digital polls and timed third-person question rounds.
  • Adults: International team profile review practicing third-person singular and plural 'to be' origin statements.
  • Seniors: Paired picture inquiry using clear pronoun visual charts ('he is' / 'they are') in a calm, structured dialogue.

Lesson 3: Talking about where people are from

Self-Portrait Task: I come from France, but I live in London.

A: Where do you live? B: I live in London, but I come from France.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Roleplay passport stamp activity where kids 'fly' between classroom stations and state where they live.
  • Teens: Interactive messaging simulation filling out student profile bios with residence and spoken language details.
  • Adults: Expat relocation profile workshop contrasting 'come from' (origin) and 'live in' (current residence).
  • Seniors: Guided interview exercise sharing life background, native languages, and current city residence with steady repetition.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: Where are you from?

A: Where do you live? B: I live in London. I speak a little English. A: Really? How interesting!
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Exclamatory reaction game practicing 'Really?' and 'How interesting!' with expressive facial gestures.
  • Teens: Pair audio recording challenge demonstrating falling intonation on Wh- questions in speed conversations.
  • Adults: Conversational networking drill practicing warm conversational reactions during origin exchanges.
  • Seniors: Unhurried partner exchanges focusing on polite question intonation and active listening responses.

Lesson 5: Unit 2 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Team-based world map review game identifying countries, capital cities, and flags.
  • Teens: Gamified online review contest covering third-person 'to be' and origin questions.
  • Adults: Structured written assessment and spoken self-check grid on origin and language forms.
  • Seniors: Clear paper review worksheet reviewing country, capital, and nationality concepts together.

Unit 3: My family — People & relationships

Lesson 1: Family members & ages

Self-Portrait Task: My mother is 50 years old. I have one brother and one sister.

A: How old is your mother? B: She is 50 years old.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Drawing a cartoon family tree with puppet roleplays stating ages and family titles.
  • Teens: Digital photo story creation building a family tree slideshow with ages and names.
  • Adults: Practical family background introductions focusing on numbers 1–100 and plural noun forms.
  • Seniors: Sharing family photographs in pairs with comfortable turn-taking and large-print age cards.

Lesson 2: Possessive adjectives — my, your, his, her, our, their

Self-Portrait Task: My brother's name is Mark. His wife is my aunt's daughter.

A: What is your brother's name? B: His name is Mark.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Object-matching game with physical tags identifying 'his car' / 'her hat' / 'their toys'.
  • Teens: Whiteboard team race matching possessive adjectives to celebrity family trees.
  • Adults: Structural possessive breakdown contrasting possessive 's ('John's father') vs verb 'to be' ('John's tall').
  • Seniors: Guided visual chart practice clarifying 'his' vs 'her' through structured picture prompts.

Lesson 3: Talking about your family

Self-Portrait Task: My younger sister is single. We live together in a close family.

A: Is your sister married? B: No, she is single. We live together.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Flashcard comparison game describing older vs younger siblings using funny avatar drawings.
  • Teens: Pair-matching activity creating digital profiles describing marital status and family closeness.
  • Adults: Structured writing task building a 5-sentence family description with 'have/has got'.
  • Seniors: Conversational photo-sharing circle discussing family members and who looks like whom.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: Tell me about your family

A: Tell me about your family. How many brothers do you have? B: I have one brother. He is very nice.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Ball-pass interviewing game asking 'How many brothers do you have?' with energetic verbal responses.
  • Teens: Short audio recording task interviewing a classmate about family members and their jobs.
  • Adults: Pair roleplays practicing open-ended family questions in friendly social conversations.
  • Seniors: Structured partner dialogues asking clear 'How many...?' questions with supportive prompts.

Lesson 5: Unit 3 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Board game review station solving family tree puzzles and possessive challenge cards.
  • Teens: Interactive quiz game reviewing family terms, numbers, and possessive 's.
  • Adults: Self-checking grammar and vocabulary worksheet covering Unit 3 family concepts.
  • Seniors: Group review session discussing generations and family tree diagrams at a relaxed pace.

Unit 4: My home — Rooms, furniture & location

Lesson 1: Rooms, furniture & prepositions of place

Self-Portrait Task: My desk is in front of the window in my bedroom.

A: Where is the table? B: The table is in the kitchen next to the door.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Building a toy house with block furniture and hiding toy animals under/on/next to objects.
  • Teens: Designing a digital floorplan using interactive interior design tools and labeling furniture locations.
  • Adults: Real estate location description drills placing furniture accurately using prepositions of place.
  • Seniors: Describing room arrangements from memory using clear preposition cards in unhurried pair work.

Lesson 2: There is / there are — positive, negative, questions

Self-Portrait Task: There is a sofa and a television in my living room.

A: Is there a balcony in the flat? B: Yes, there is. There are two rooms.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Memory game observing a room illustration for 10 seconds, then stating 'There is a...' / 'There are two...'.
  • Teens: Digital room inspection quiz identifying differences between two photo interiors using 'there is/are'.
  • Adults: Rental apartment verification roleplay listing amenities using 'there is/are' positive and negative forms.
  • Seniors: Guided visual checklists describing living room contents using large-print contrast charts.

Lesson 3: Describing your home

Self-Portrait Task: I live in a small, quiet flat. It is very cozy and clean.

A: Is your house big? B: No, it is small, but it is comfortable and quiet.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Drawing a 'cozy' or 'big' dream house and presenting descriptive adjectives with visual gestures.
  • Teens: Writing a social media post evaluating a living space using opinion and size adjectives.
  • Adults: Writing a descriptive email to a penpal detailing apartment rental features, size, and environment.
  • Seniors: Pair exchanges comparing quiet vs noisy neighborhoods and cozy home features using clear adjective scales.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: Your perfect home

A: What is your dream house like? B: I like a cozy house near the park. It has got a beautiful view.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Fantasy home presentation describing an ideal room with a view of a park or garden.
  • Teens: Recording a 1-minute video blog pitching a dream house design with spatial preferences.
  • Adults: Conversational roleplays discussing housing choices, views, and proximity to amenities.
  • Seniors: Structured conversation sharing ideal living preferences with audio-supported repetition.

Lesson 5: Unit 4 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Interactive board game reviewing home items, prepositions, and 'there is/are'.
  • Teens: Online leaderboard challenge reviewing floorplans, furniture, and location prepositions.
  • Adults: Written self-check and floorplan description worksheet evaluating Unit 4 mastery.
  • Seniors: Group review session identifying home appliances and location terms at a comfortable pace.

Unit 5: My day — Daily routine & time

Lesson 1: Daily routine verbs & time expressions

Self-Portrait Task: I wake up at 7 o'clock in the morning. I go to bed at 11 o'clock.

A: What time do you wake up? B: I wake up at 7 o'clock.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Pantomime action game acting out morning routines ('brush teeth', 'wake up') with clock face practice.
  • Teens: Digital routine timeline creation mapping daily schedules with clock icons.
  • Adults: Work-life balance timetable analysis practicing precise time prepositions ('at 7 o'clock').
  • Seniors: Daily activity recall in pairs using large clock visual aids and clear verb repetition.

Lesson 2: Present simple — I / you / we / they (affirmative, negative, questions)

Self-Portrait Task: I usually drink coffee in the morning. On weekdays, I always start work at 9.

A: Do you always drink coffee in the morning? B: Yes, I always do.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Habit frequency scale activity placing cards ('always', 'never') on a weekly chart with physical actions.
  • Teens: Interactive poll rating daily habits (gaming, studying) with frequency adverbs.
  • Adults: Grammar position drill placing adverbs of frequency correctly before main verbs.
  • Seniors: Pair discussion on weekly routines using clear percentage frequency charts.

Lesson 3: Talking about your daily routine

Self-Portrait Task: First, I have breakfast. Then, I go to work. After that, I go home.

A: What do you do on your day off? B: First I wake up late. Then I meet friends.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Comic strip sequencing task using 'first, then, after that, finally' to arrange a daily story.
  • Teens: Creating a vlog script connecting daily activities chronologically with transition words.
  • Adults: Paragraph construction exercise describing a typical busy workday using sequence markers.
  • Seniors: Structured recall sharing weekend habits chronologically with gentle prompt cards.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: A day in my life

A: What time do you usually wake up? B: I usually wake up at 7. What do you do after that? A: Then I have breakfast.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Interviewer roleplay holding a toy microphone and asking classmates about their schedules.
  • Teens: Pair interview recording activity presenting a summary of a partner's daily routine.
  • Adults: Communicative interview simulation asking precise Present Simple routine questions.
  • Seniors: Pair interview exchanges sharing daily habits at a relaxed pace with structured guides.

Lesson 5: Unit 5 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Timetable board game review matching routines, times, and adverbs.
  • Teens: Digital speed review quiz testing frequency adverbs and Present Simple forms.
  • Adults: Self-assessment checklist and written error-correction exercise on routines.
  • Seniors: Paper-based review exercise summarizing daily schedule concepts calmly.

Unit 6: Food & drink — Eating, shopping & preferences

Lesson 1: Food, drink & quantities

Self-Portrait Task: I eat bread and cheese for breakfast. I drink some tea.

A: Is there any milk in the fridge? B: Yes, there is some milk and two apples.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Play grocery store roleplay sorting toy food items into 'countable' (apples) and 'uncountable' (milk).
  • Teens: Interactive recipe builder choosing ingredients with 'some' and 'any'.
  • Adults: Grocery list preparation and budget practice contrasting 'how much' vs 'how many'.
  • Seniors: Guided sorting exercise classifying food cards into countable and uncountable groups.

Lesson 2: Present simple — he/she/it: the third person -s rule

Self-Portrait Task: My father cooks dinner. He drinks coffee, but he prefers tea.

A: What does he eat for lunch? B: He eats chicken and rice.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Cooking puppet show describing what chef animals eat ('He eats fish', 'She drinks milk').
  • Teens: Digital partner profile task describing a friend's food preferences with 3rd-person -s.
  • Adults: Grammar transformation drill converting 'I eat' to 'She eats' and 'He cooks'.
  • Seniors: Third-person habit description pairing picture cards with clear verb -s endings.

Lesson 3: Talking about food preferences

Self-Portrait Task: I love eating fresh fruit. I don't like spicy food.

A: Do you like fast food? B: No, I prefer healthy food. I love fresh salad.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Taste-test card game rating foods as 'delicious', 'sweet', or 'spicy' with thumbs up/down.
  • Teens: Restaurant review post on a digital board rating menu items with taste adjectives.
  • Adults: Menu evaluation and preference roleplay expressing likes and dislikes with reasons.
  • Seniors: Sharing favorite recipes and dietary preferences in a calm conversational format.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: My favourite meal

A: Can I have a cup of coffee, please? B: Sure! Anything else? A: I would like a chocolate cake. How much is it?
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Play cafe ordering game taking orders with toy menus and 'Can I have...?' prompts.
  • Teens: Audio recording roleplay simulating ordering a meal at a trendy cafe with polite requests.
  • Adults: Restaurant ordering simulation practicing 'I would like' and requesting the bill.
  • Seniors: Pair roleplays ordering drinks and snacks with clear visual menus.

Lesson 5: Unit 6 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Food bingo and restaurant review board game.
  • Teens: Interactive quiz on food categories, 3rd-person verbs, and ordering phrases.
  • Adults: Written review and cafe conversation self-assessment.
  • Seniors: Group review session discussing mealtimes and food preferences.

Unit 7: Work & study — Jobs, places & abilities

Lesson 1: Jobs, workplaces & study

Self-Portrait Task: I work as a programmer in an office.

A: What is your job? B: I am a programmer. I work in an office.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Costume dress-up game matching professions to workplaces ('a doctor in a hospital').

  • Adults: Professional networking drill stating occupations and workplaces accurately.
  • Seniors: Sharing past career roles and workplaces using clear job title flashcards.

Lesson 2: Can / can't — ability and possibility

Self-Portrait Task: I can use a computer and speak English. I can't play the piano.

A: Can you drive a car? B: Yes, I can drive, but I can't fix it.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Talent show pantomime demonstrating physical abilities ('I can swim', 'I can't fly').
  • Teens: Skill matrix survey checking off tech and language abilities with classmates.
  • Adults: Workplace skill inventory listing technical and practical abilities using 'can/can't'.
  • Seniors: Pair exchanges discussing hobbies and practical skills with clear stress patterns.

Lesson 3: Talking about your job and abilities

Self-Portrait Task: I am good at my job. I am responsible for a big project.

A: Are you interested in this project? B: Yes, I am. I am good at computer work.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Team-building puzzle activity learning collocations ('have a meeting', 'take notes').
  • Teens: Digital project pitch stating what tasks students are 'good at' and 'interested in'.
  • Adults: Writing a short professional bio describing responsibilities and skills.
  • Seniors: Guided discussion sharing career experiences and skills in a friendly setting.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: What do you do?

A: What do you do? Where do you work? B: I am a programmer. I work in a company. I am good at my work.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Interview roleplay asking friends 'What is your job?' with fun character cards.
  • Teens: Audio recording task introducing job skills and answering interview questions.
  • Adults: Job interview simulation answering questions about career and capabilities.
  • Seniors: Structured conversation practicing career introduction phrases peacefully.

Lesson 5: Unit 7 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Career path board game reviewing jobs, abilities, and 'can/can't'.
  • Teens: Gamified skills assessment covering modal verbs and work collocations.
  • Adults: Self-assessment checklist evaluating workplace communication skills.
  • Seniors: Unpressured group review summarizing occupations and abilities.

Unit 8: My city — Places, transport & directions

Lesson 1: Places in a city & transport

Self-Portrait Task: I take the bus to the supermarket near the park.

A: How do you go to work? B: I go by train, but I walk to the station.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Neighborhood map activity moving toy cars and buses between the bank, park, and supermarket.
  • Teens: Digital transit route challenge choosing between train, bus, or bicycle to reach city locations.
  • Adults: City navigation drills practicing prepositions of motion ('walk past the museum to the station').
  • Seniors: Large-print city map navigation exercises identifying key services like pharmacy and bank.

Lesson 2: Imperatives & directions + question words review

Self-Portrait Task: To go to my office, turn right and go straight ahead.

A: Excuse me, where is the bank? B: Turn left and go straight ahead. It is opposite the park.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Blindfolded obstacle course game where a partner calls 'Turn left!', 'Stop!', 'Go straight ahead!'.
  • Teens: Digital GPS navigation challenge writing direction steps for a friend.
  • Adults: Street map direction-giving roleplay utilizing imperatives and location terms.
  • Seniors: Clear diagrammatic direction practice giving simple instructions turn-by-turn.

Lesson 3: Getting around the city

Self-Portrait Task: I buy a train ticket at the station. It is not far.

A: How much does a ticket cost? B: It costs ten pounds. The train leaves from platform 2.
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Ticket booth roleplay buying bus passes with toy money and asking 'How much is it?'.
  • Teens: Online transit schedule lookup calculating travel time and ticket costs.
  • Adults: Ticket office simulation asking travel questions ('How far is it?', 'How long does it take?').
  • Seniors: Train station info desk roleplays inquiring about platform schedules and ticket fares.

Lesson 4: Spoken course lesson: Getting around

A: Excuse me, how do I get to the museum? Is it near here? B: Take the first turning on the right. A: Thank you very much!
Age Adaptations:
  • Children: Street maze roleplay asking politely 'Excuse me, how do I get to the zoo?'.
  • Teens: Audio recording task simulating asking a pedestrian for directions in a foreign city.
  • Adults: Polite street direction simulations with natural conversational intonation.
  • Seniors: Pair roleplay practicing direction inquiry phrases with clear audio support.

Lesson 5: Unit 8 exam & review

Age Adaptations:
  • Children: City navigation map game reviewing places, directions, and transport.
  • Teens: Digital city quest quiz testing imperatives, prepositions, and travel expressions.
  • Adults: Self-assessment checklist evaluating city navigation and direction giving.
  • Seniors: Group review session discussing city maps and transit routes at a comfortable pace.
