Unit 1: Unit 1: Personal Profiles, Upbringing & Life History

Lesson 1: Past Simple vs Past Continuous

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Past Simple for completed past actions and main storyline events
  • Past Continuous for background actions in progress (was/were + -ing)
  • Interrupted actions using when and while
🎯 I can: Can narrate a simple past event and describe background actions using when and while
🗣️ Past simple -ed endings
Distinguish between /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/ endings for regular past tense verbs.
started /wɜːkt/ / /pleɪd/ / /ˈstɑː.tɪd/

Lesson 2: Past Habits & Life Experiences (Used to & Present Perfect)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Used to + infinitive for past habits and states no longer true
  • Present Perfect with ever and never for life experiences
  • Contrast between Past Simple (finished time) and Present Perfect (unfinished time)
🎯 I can: Can describe past habits and talk about general life experiences
🗣️ The used to liaison
The final d in used and initial t in to blend into a single /tuː/ sound.
used to /ˈjuːs.tə/

Unit 2: Unit 2: Housing, Renting & Living Spaces

Lesson 1: Obligation & Rules in Housing (Have to / Must / Mustn't)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Have to / don't have to for external obligation and lack of obligation
  • Must for strong personal obligation; Mustn't for prohibition
🎯 I can: Can discuss housing rules, lease conditions, and tenant obligations
🗣️ Silent t in mustn't
Practice saying mustn't cleanly without pronouncing the middle t.
mustn't /ˈmʌs.ənt/

Lesson 2: Describing Homes & Prepositions of Place

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Prepositions of place and location (opposite, next to, between, across from)
  • There is / There are with quantifiers (some, any, several)
🎯 I can: Can describe property layouts, room features, and neighbourhood locations

Unit 3: Unit 3: Work, Professions & Career Development

Lesson 1: Workplace Tasks & Verb Collocations (Do vs Make)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Workplace verb collocations: do business/research vs make a decision/effort
  • Present Simple vs Present Continuous for current projects vs regular job duties
🎯 I can: Can talk about job duties, office projects, and everyday work responsibilities
🗣️ Office vocabulary stress
Primary stress placement in workplace terms (dePARTment, PROductive, EFFICient).
department /dɪˈpɑːt.mənt/

Lesson 2: Present & Past Passive in Workplace Processes

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Present Simple Passive (is/are + past participle) for workflow routines
  • Past Simple Passive (was/were + past participle) for completed reports and tasks
🎯 I can: Can describe office procedures and task status using simple passive forms

Unit 4: Unit 4: Health, Symptoms & Medical Consultations

Lesson 1: Describing Health Symptoms & Giving Advice (Should / Shouldn't)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Should / shouldn't for health recommendations
  • Expressing physical symptoms (have got a headache, feel dizzy, hurt)
🎯 I can: Can explain illness symptoms and give appropriate health advice using should
🗣️ Silent l in should
The letter l in should and shouldn't is silent (/ʃʊd/, /ˈʃʊd.ənt/).
should /ʃʊd/

Lesson 2: Healthy Lifestyle & Physical Wellbeing

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Adverbs of degree and frequency (extremely, fairly, regularly, rarely)
  • Imperatives for fitness and wellbeing instructions
🎯 I can: Can discuss dietary habits, exercise routines, and personal wellbeing goals

Unit 5: Unit 5: Travel, Accommodation & Sightseeing

Lesson 1: Booking Travel & Asking for Information (Indirect Questions)

Grammar Focus Points:

Using gentle rising-falling pitch contours when asking polite service questions.
Could /kʊd juː tel miː/

Lesson 2: Sightseeing & Prepositions of Movement

Unit 7: Unit 7: Digital Communication & Technology

Lesson 1: Future Plans & Intentions (Will vs Be Going To)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Be going to for intentions and planned tech upgrades
  • Will for spontaneous decisions, predictions, and tech promises
🎯 I can: Can discuss technology habits, device preferences, and planned digital updates
🗣️ Weak form of going to
In casual conversation, going to frequently reduces to gonna /ˈɡən.ə/.
gonna /ˈɡən.ə/

Lesson 2: First Conditional in Tech & Daily Life

Grammar Focus Points:
  • First conditional: If + Present Simple, Will + infinitive
  • Time clauses with when and as soon as
🎯 I can: Can explain cause-and-effect conditions and basic cybersecurity rules

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Second conditional intro: If + Past Simple, Would + infinitive for imaginary situations

🎯 I can: Can talk about hypothetical future situations and offer personal advice using second conditional

Unit 9: Unit 9: Entertainment, Arts & Socializing

Lesson 1: Defining Relative Clauses (Who, Which, That, Where)

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Defining relative clauses: who for people, which/that for things, where for places
  • Omitting relative pronouns when they are object of the clause
🎯 I can: Can describe movies, books, venues, and cultural events using relative clauses

Lesson 2: Question Tags & Social Agreement

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Question tags for checking information and seeking agreement (is it?, don't you?)
  • So / Neither for expressing agreement (So do I / Neither am I)
🎯 I can: Can maintain social conversation, express agreement, and use natural question tags
🗣️ Intonation on question tags
Falling intonation expects agreement; rising intonation asks a real question.
wasn't it /wɒz.ənt ɪt/
