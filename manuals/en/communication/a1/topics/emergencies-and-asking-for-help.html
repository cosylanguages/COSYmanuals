CEFR A1 Urgent Communication

Emergencies & Asking for Help

Calling for urgent assistance, offering help to others, and describing immediate problems clearly.

💬 How do I call for help or deal with an urgent emergency?

Being able to call for help, report lost items, or request urgent assistance ensures personal safety when traveling.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can call for help ("Help! Call an ambulance!"), ask for assistance ("Can you help me, please?"), and express urgent problems ("I lost my passport", "I'm lost").

🧩 Modular Building Blocks: Emergency & Help Chips

Help! / Excuse me, can you help me? Call the police! / Call an ambulance! I lost my wallet / passport / phone. I'm lost / I feel sick. Do you need help? / Are you OK? Don't panic! / I'll help you! Yes, please! Thank you! Thank you so much for your help!

🗺️ Emergency Call Flow Map

1
Urgent Call: "Excuse me! Help, please! I'm lost."
2
Offer Assistance: "Are you OK? What's the problem?"
3
State Problem: "I lost my hotel key and phone." — "Don't worry, let's call reception."
4
Relief Close: "Thank you so much! You're very kind."

👀 Notice: Direct & Clear Phrasing

In emergency situations, short, direct sentences ("Help!", "I'm lost", "Call an ambulance!") are much more effective than long, complex explanations.

🧠 Think in Conversation

Bystanders in English-speaking countries respond quickly when you use "Excuse me, can you help me?" or "I need help". Don't hesitate to speak up.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Stiff: "An emergency event has occurred."
✅ Natural: "Help! Can someone call an ambulance, please?"

💡 Pragmatic Memory Trick

Remember the 3 Safety Words: Help ➔ Lost ➔ Call!

💬 Sample Exchange

A: Excuse me! Can you help me, please?
B: Of course! What's wrong?
A: I'm lost. Where is the train station?
B: It's just straight ahead, two blocks down!
