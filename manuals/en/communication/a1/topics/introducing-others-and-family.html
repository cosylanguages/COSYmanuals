Introducing Others & Family

Introducing third parties, sharing age and family details, and connecting people smoothly.

Introducing friends and sharing family information deepens social relationships in everyday speech.

🧩 Modular Building Blocks: Social & Family Chips

This is my friend / colleague [Name]. I'd like you to meet Anna. I'm 25 years old. / He's 30. I'm married / single. I have one brother and two sisters. How old are you? / Do you have children? Nice to meet you, Anna! It was great meeting you both!

🗺️ 3-Way Introduction Flow Map

1
Third-Party Intro: "John, this is my sister Sarah."
2
Greeting & Response: "Hi Sarah! Nice to meet you!" — "Nice to meet you too, John!"
3
Detail Exchange: "Sarah is a doctor in London." — "Oh really? That's awesome!"
4
Group Close: "Great meeting you! Enjoy the party!"

👀 Notice: English Age Rule (BE, not HAVE)

In English, age uses the verb TO BE ("I am 20 years old"), NOT "I have 20 years" as in French, Italian, or Spanish.

🧠 Think in Conversation

Saying "This is [Name]" is the universal, friendly way to introduce someone standing next to you in both informal and business settings.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Stiff: "I have 25 years of age."
✅ Natural: "I'm 25."

💡 Pragmatic Memory Trick

Remember: English IS age (I am 25), not HAVE!

💬 Sample Exchange

A: Tom, this is my friend Maria. She's from Spain.
B: Hi Maria! Nice to meet you!
C: Nice to meet you too, Tom!
B: Are you visiting London for holiday?
C: Yes! I'm here with my family for one week.
