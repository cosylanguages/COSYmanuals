Asking & Giving Permission

Requesting permission politely using 'Can I...?' or 'May I...?', granting it warmly, and setting polite boundaries.

💬 How do I ask for permission and grant or refuse it politely?

Asking permission before using shared spaces or items shows respect and prevents awkward misunderstandings.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can ask permission ("Can I sit here?", "Is it OK if I use this?"), grant permission ("Sure, go ahead!"), and decline gently ("Sorry, someone is sitting here").

🧩 Modular Building Blocks: Permission Chips

Can I sit here, please? Is it OK if I open the window? May I leave early today? Sure! Go right ahead! Yes, of course! No problem. Sorry, this seat is taken. Not right now, sorry. Thanks a lot! / Thank you!

🗺️ Permission Exchange Flow Map

1
Permission Request: "Excuse me, is it OK if I charge my phone here?"
2
Grant or Decline: "Sure, go ahead!" OR "Sorry, this socket isn't working."
3
Action or Alternative: "Great, thank you!" OR "Oh I see, thanks anyway!"
4
Closing Politeness: "You're welcome!" / "No problem!"

👀 Notice: Softening Refusals

When refusing permission, native English speakers use "Sorry" plus a quick reason rather than a simple "No" to remain polite.

🧠 Think in Conversation

"Can I...?" is standard in daily casual life. "May I...?" adds an extra layer of formal politeness, ideal for classrooms or formal offices.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Stiff: "I command to sit on this chair."
✅ Natural: "Is anyone sitting here? Can I sit?"

💡 Pragmatic Memory Trick

Remember the Green Light Phrase: Sure! Go ahead!

💬 Sample Exchange

A: Excuse me, is it OK if I open the window? It's a bit warm in here.
B: Yes, of course! Go right ahead.
A: Thank you so much!
B: No problem at all.
