CEFR A1 Social Interactions

Invitations & Responses

Inviting friends out, accepting enthusiastically, and declining politely without giving offense.

💬 How do I invite someone out and respond politely?

Making plans and reacting to invitations is essential for building real-world friendships and social connections.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can extend simple invitations ("Would you like to...?"), accept warmly ("I'd love to!"), and decline gently ("Sorry, I can't today").

🧩 Modular Building Blocks: Invitation Chips

Would you like to get a coffee? Do you want to come to the park? Let me invite you! / Join us! Yes! I'd love to! Sure, that sounds great! Sorry, I can't today. Maybe next time! See you there! / Catch you later!

🗺️ Invitation Interaction Map

1
Invitation Opener: "Hey Mark! Would you like to have lunch together?"
2
Accept or Decline: "I'd love to!" OR "Sorry, I can't today. I have a meeting."
3
Time & Place / Softener: "Great! How about 1 o'clock?" OR "No problem! Maybe next time."
4
Confirmation Close: "Perfect, see you at 1!" — "See you then!"

👀 Notice: Softening Refusals

Notice how native speakers never say a blunt "No" when declining an invitation. They buffer it with an apology ("Sorry"), a reason ("I have to work"), or a future bridge ("Maybe next time!").

🧠 Think in Conversation

"Would you like to...?" is polite and friendly for any acquaintance, while "Do you want to...?" and "Let me..." are ideal for close friends and colleagues.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Stiff: "No. I refuse your invitation."
✅ Natural: "I'd love to, but I can't today! Maybe next time?"

💡 Pragmatic Memory Trick

Remember the Soft Decline Sandwich: Sorry ➔ Reason ➔ Maybe next time!

💬 Sample Exchange

A: Hey Anna! Would you like to get a coffee this afternoon?
B: Sure, that sounds great! What time?
A: How about 3 o'clock at the central cafe?
B: Perfect! See you there at 3!
