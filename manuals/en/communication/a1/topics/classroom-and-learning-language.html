CEFR A1 Academic & Study Skills

Classroom & Learning Language

Understanding teacher instructions, interacting during lessons, and managing learning tasks.

💬 How do I follow instructions and interact during language lessons?

Understanding classroom imperatives and asking quick study questions helps you learn faster and participate with confidence.

🎯 What you can communicate after this page: You can follow teacher directives ("Open your book", "Listen and repeat"), ask questions ("What page are we on?"), and state learning status ("I'm finished!", "I don't speak French well").
🎯 Ready to test your skills? Reinforce this topic with interactive practice drills.

🧩 Modular Building Blocks: Classroom Chips

Open your book to page 10. Listen and repeat after me. I'm learning English. / I speak a little. What page are we on? Does anyone have a question? I'm finished! / We are ready! Work together with a partner. Great job everyone! See you next lesson!

🗺️ Lesson Interaction Flow Map

1
Teacher Instruction: "Please open your book to page 12 and read exercise 1."
2
Student Clarify: "Sorry, what page did you say?" — "Page 12!"
3
Task Completion: "Work with a partner. I'm finished!"
4
Lesson Close: "Well done! See you tomorrow!"

👀 Notice: Direct Imperatives in Directives

Notice how teachers use direct imperatives (Listen, Repeat, Read, Write) in class. In learning environments, this is encouraging and clear, not rude.

🧠 Think in Conversation

Saying "I'm learning English" or "Could you speak a bit slower?" immediately sets realistic expectations and invites patient, helpful responses.

🗣 Natural Speech Add-on

❌ Stiff: "My learning task has reached completion."
✅ Natural: "I'm finished! What's next?"

💡 Pragmatic Memory Trick

Remember the 4 Learning Action Verbs: Listen ➔ Read ➔ Write ➔ Repeat!

💬 Sample Exchange

Teacher: Please work together with a partner and write three sentences.
Student A: Sorry, what page are we on?
Student B: We are on page 15! Let's write sentence number one.
Student A: Great! I'm ready!
