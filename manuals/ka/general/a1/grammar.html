Unit 0: პირველი ნაბიჯები (A0)

Lesson 1: Hello & Welcome

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Verb "to be" (am/is/are) — positive forms
🎯 I can: Can greet people and say goodbye
🗣️ The English Alphabet
English has 26 letters but many more sounds. Start with the names of the letters.
A /eɪ/ B /biː/ C /siː/ D /diː/ E /iː/ F /ef/ G /dʒiː/ H /eɪtʃ/ I /aɪ/ J /dʒeɪ/ K /keɪ/ L /el/ M /em/ N /en/ O /əʊ/ P /piː/ Q /kjuː/ R /ɑː/ S /es/ T /tiː/ U /juː/ V /viː/ W /ˈdʌb.əl.juː/ X /eks/ Y /waɪ/ Z /zed/
💡 Did you know? The letters A, E, I, O, U are vowels. All other letters are consonants. In English, one letter can often represent multiple different sounds!
Homework
Learn the alphabet song

Lesson 2: What is your name?

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Verb "to be" — questions (What is...?)
🎯 I can: Can introduce themselves
🗣️ Contractions
In spoken English, we often combine words. 'What is' becomes 'What's'.
What's /wɒt ɪz/ I'm /aɪ æm/ My name's /maɪ neɪm ɪz/
💡 Contractions make your English sound more natural and fluent. We use them almost all the time when speaking!

Practice spelling your name

Lesson 4: Minimal Pairs 1: Vowels

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Basic sentence structure
🎯 I can: Can distinguish between short and long vowel sounds
🗣️ Short vs Long Vowels
Short sounds vs long sounds can change the meaning of a word.
ship /ʃɪp/ ↔ sheep /ʃiːp/ bit /bɪt/ ↔ beat /biːt/ fit /fɪt/ ↔ feet /fiːt/
💡 The length of the vowel is very important in English. Notice how your mouth stays in a similar position but the sound lasts longer for 'sheep' and 'beat'.

Unit 1: My Life Today

Lesson 1: A day in my life — routines revisited

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Present simple: all persons + frequency adverbs (mid-position)
🎯 I can: Can describe daily routines using all frequency adverbs
🗣️ Starter Foundation: The Alphabet Sounds
Master the vowel sounds in the alphabet which often cause confusion.
Alphabet /eɪ/ Email /iː/ Identity /aɪ/
💡 English vowels 'A' and 'E' are often swapped by learners from other languages!
🗣️ The Schwa Sound /ə/
The most common sound in English. It's short, weak, and found in unstressed syllables.
about /ə'baʊt/ teacher /'tiːtʃə/ police /'pəʊlɪs/
💡 Relax your mouth completely to make this sound.

Grammar Focus Points:
  • Past simple: regular + irregular review + time expressions (in 2015, after that, then)
🎯 I can: Can narrate a basic career history in past simple
🗣️ Past Simple '-ed' Endings
The suffix '-ed' can be pronounced in three different ways: /t/, /d/, or /ɪd/.
worked /wɜːkt/ played /pleɪd/ started /ˈstɑː.tɪd/
💡 Use /ɪd/ only when the base verb ends in 't' or 'd'. For most others, it's just a quick /t/ or /d/ sound.

🎯 I can: Can use past continuous for an ongoing past action

In sentences, these auxiliary verbs are usually unstressed and sound weak.
I was working /aɪ wəz/ They were talking /ðeɪ wə/
💡 Instead of saying 'WAZ' or 'WERE' strongly, use the schwa sound /ə/ to sound more natural.

💡 'Been' implies a return, while 'Gone' implies the person is still there. Pronunciation is clear: /iː/ vs /ɒ/.
